# Settings
Settings gathers the options of orOS itself in one place: how it looks, notifications, sync, backups, language and search, and the system tools. Each option still works exactly as before; it just lives here now instead of in the menu.

## Getting started
1. Open the menu and choose `Settings`, at the bottom under the apps. You'll also find it in the `System` category.
2. Pick a section on the left: `Appearance`, `Accessibility`, `Menu`, `Notifications`, `Sync`, `Screen lock`, `Backups`, `Language & search`, `Opening files`, `Connected services` or `System`.
3. Change what you like. Every change applies at once; there is no save button.

On a phone, Settings first shows the list of sections. Tap one to open it, and `Back` takes you to the list again.

Next to each card you'll see where it applies: `All devices` means it travels with [sync](help:t/sync), `This device` means it stays on this device only.

To find one option quickly, type in `Search settings` at the top of the list: a word from its name or a related word (dark, password, keyboard, ringtone…) in English or Greek. The list shows the matching sections and options; pick one and Settings opens there, scrolls to the option and lights it up for a moment. If the option needs something switched on first (the Dock, the lock, sync), the switch that turns it on lights up instead. `Escape` or × clears the search.

## The sections
- `Appearance`: `Theme` (`Dark` or `Light`), `System colour`, `Wallpaper`, `Seasonal decorations`, the `Dock` when your orOS has one, and the `Screen pet`. See [Appearance](help:t/appearance).
- `Accessibility`: `Text size` (100% to 150%), `High contrast` (`Automatic`, `On` or `Off`) and `Always show the focus ring`, for orOS and every app, on this device. See [Appearance](help:t/appearance).
- `Menu`: which apps the menu lists. Untick an app to hide it, or use `Hide all`, `Show all` and `Choose a profile` (Home, Work, School…); `Find an app` narrows the list. Hidden apps still open from the menu search, links, the desktop and the Dock. See [The menu](help:t/menu).
- `Notifications`: the `Notifications` switch, `Send a test`, `Notifications with orOS closed` (optional, per device), position, style, sound, `Quiet hours` and `Which apps may notify`. See [Notifications](help:t/notifications).
- `Sync`: `Connect to Dropbox` or `Use WebDAV / Nextcloud` (with `Sign in with Nextcloud` and `Through the orOS relay`), your `Passphrase`, `Get from cloud`, `Send to cloud`, `Sync automatically`, `Unused files in the cloud` and `Disconnect`. See [Sync across devices](help:t/sync).
- `Screen lock`: a PIN or a passphrase that orOS asks for when it starts and when you leave it alone, on this device only. See Screen lock below.
- `Backups`: `Automatic backup`, `Backup folder`, `Back up now`, `Export backup file` and `Import backup file`. See [Backup and restore](help:t/backup).
- `Language & search`: the `Language` of orOS, the `On-screen keyboard` (on touch screens: when it shows and which layouts it has, see [Appearance](help:t/appearance)) and which apps the menu search looks in. `Help translate` opens a page where you can translate orOS into another language line by line: English and Greek are shown next to each line, `Try it` shows your lines in orOS at once, and `Download file` or `Send on GitHub` sends them for review. Your lines stay on this device until then.
- `Opening files`: which orOS app opens which file type from your device (`Open with`), the choice for types more than one app opens, and how to make orOS the default on Windows, Linux and Mac.
- `Connected services`: platforms orOS works with: a WooCommerce shop (used in [Stock](app:stock)) a timologisi.online account (used in [Invoices](app:invoices) to send documents to myDATA), MailerLite, Mailchimp or Brevo accounts (used in [Newsletter](app:newsletter) to sync a list's subscribers) HubSpot or Pipedrive accounts (read by [CRM](app:crm) to bring in customers and deals) a Stripe account (read by [Budget](app:budget) to bring in payments) an Invoice Ninja account (read by CRM for clients and by Budget for payments) an Odoo (read by CRM for contacts and opportunities) a Todoist account (read by [To-Do](app:todo) for projects and open tasks) Trello or GitHub accounts (read by [Kanban](app:kanban) to bring in a board or a repository's issues, and GitHub also by [To-Do](app:todo) for the open issues assigned to you) a Raindrop.io account (read by [Bookmarks](app:bookmarks) for collections and bookmarks) and a Notion integration (read by [Notes](app:notes) for the pages shared with it and by [Spreadsheet](app:spreadsheet) and [Kanban](app:kanban) for its databases). `Connect a service` asks first for the platform. For WooCommerce: the shop address and the two REST API keys (WooCommerce › Settings › Advanced › REST API › Add key, Read/Write). For timologisi.online: `Test (sandbox)` or `Live (real documents)`, the issuer's ΑΦΜ and the API key from its developer portal; `Check connection` shows the credits left. For MailerLite, Mailchimp and Brevo: the API key (MailerLite calls it a token). For HubSpot: the access token of a private app with read scopes for contacts, companies and deals (it starts with pat-). For Pipedrive: your personal API token (Personal preferences › API). For Stripe: a restricted key with Read access to Balance only (rk_live_…); a secret key (sk_) is refused. For Invoice Ninja: its address (invoicing.co for the hosted service, or your own install) and an API token (Settings › Account management › Integrations › API tokens). For Odoo (19 or Odoo Online): its address, the database only if the server has several, and an API key (your avatar › My Preferences › Account Security › New API Key). For Todoist: your API token (Settings › Integrations › Developer). For Trello: an API key and a token (trello.com/power-ups/admin › New › API key, then the Token link next to it). For GitHub: a personal access token, best a fine-grained one with read-only access to Issues of the repositories you want (Settings › Developer settings › Personal access tokens). For Raindrop.io: a test token (Settings › Integrations › For developers › Create new app › Create test token). For Notion: the secret of an integration of your own (notion.so/profile/integrations › New integration › Internal Integration Secret); Notion always goes through the relay. Each account shows its `Route` (straight from this browser, or through the orOS relay when the shop doesn't answer browsers), `Check connection`, `Enter the keys`, `Rename`, `Use these keys on all my devices` and `Disconnect`.
- `System`: the `orOS version`, `Check for updates`, `Install orOS as an app` (when your browser offers it), `Keyboard shortcuts`, links to [Device info](app:device) and more, and `Factory reset`.

### Screen lock
`Screen lock` is off until you turn it on, and it applies to this device only. It is for a shared family PC, a Portable stick or the Live USB: whoever sits down next can't open your mail, diary or invoices.

1. Open `Screen lock` and press `Turn on the lock`.
2. Choose `PIN` (4 to 12 digits) or `Passphrase` (at least 6 characters), type it twice and press `Turn on the lock`.
3. Under `When it locks`, pick `Lock after` (1 to 60 minutes of no use, or `Never`) and, if you like, `Lock when you leave the page`.

orOS then asks for it every time it starts, after the idle time, and with the second switch whenever you switch to another tab, app or window. Reopening the same tab within a few minutes (an update, the Dropbox sign-in) doesn't ask again. `Lock now` (also `Lock orOS` in the menu, under `Settings`) locks at once, in every orOS tab of this browser. Apps keep running behind the lock: music plays on and sync keeps syncing, but nothing can be seen or touched until you unlock. After five wrong tries the lock screen makes you wait, a little longer each time. `Change PIN or passphrase` and `Turn off the lock` ask for the current one first.

Forgot it? On the lock screen choose `Forgot your PIN?`. If sync is connected on this device, your sync passphrase opens it and the lock turns off; set a new one here. Otherwise `Reset this device` is the last resort: it erases orOS on this device only (changes not synced yet are sent first when sync can) and keeps your cloud data and backup folder, so connecting sync again brings everything back.

What it doesn't do: it protects the screen, not the disk. Anyone who can read this device's storage, or open the browser's developer tools, can get past it, and your data on the device is not encrypted by it. Only a slow, salted hash of the PIN is kept on the device; it is never synced and never goes into a backup file.

### Multitasking
`System` also has `Multitasking`, for this device only: on by default on computers, off on phones and tablets. With `Keep apps open when you leave them` on, an app you leave (with the menu button, the menu or a link from another app) stays open in the background and comes back exactly as you left it, half-typed text included. Music, Radio and Podcasts play on as always. How many stay open depends on your device's memory (the card says how many); heavy apps such as Atelier, Maps and Photo Lab count as two, and when there is no room the app you used longest ago closes. Mail, Calendar, Passwords, Settings and apps that use the camera or microphone close when you leave them, as before. Changing the language closes the ones in the background. Turning the switch off closes them all at once.

### This computer
In orOS Portable and in the orOS apps for Windows and Linux, `System` has `This computer`, for this computer only: `Show this computer's files in orOS` adds This computer to [Files](help:a/files) (your home folder and the computer's disks) and lets every app open and save there. orOS Portable asks once on each computer; the orOS apps have it on from the start. Nothing on the computer is synced. On the orOS USB stick the files always show, so the switch is not there.

While an app waits in the background, the top bar shows a button next to the menu button with the number of open apps; [[Ctrl+Alt+Shift+W]] does the same. It lists them with the time you left each one: choose one to go back to it, close one with its ×, or close them all. Use the arrow keys, [[Enter]], [[Delete]] and [[Esc]] on a keyboard; on a phone the list comes up from the bottom and you swipe a card sideways to close that app. In the menu and the Dock, a dot marks the apps that are open.

On a screen at least 1024 pixels wide you can put two apps side by side. In the list of open apps, press the split button next to an app in the background to open it beside the one in front, or the one next to the app in front to choose the other app from the menu. Drag the bar between them to share the width (or focus it and use the arrow keys); the keys go to the side you clicked last, which has a coloured outline. The × on the bar, going back to the desktop, or a narrower window ends side by side, and the app on the right waits in the background.

### Other ways in
- Tap the sync dot in the top bar while sync isn't connected or is locked: Settings opens at `Sync`.
- Type the name of an option in the menu search: the result opens Settings at that option.
- The `About orOS + shortcuts` window has a button that opens `System`, where the factory reset is.

## Sync and backup
Settings stores nothing of its own. Every option is saved by orOS in the same place as before, so your choices sync and back up exactly as they always did. Theme, colour, wallpaper, the apps the menu shows, screen pet, notifications, sync interval, automatic backup and language go to all your devices. The backup folder, your remembered passphrase, the menu search choices, the Dock and the accessibility options stay on this device.

### Connected services and your keys
The keys of a connected service never go into your synced data in clear. Each device keeps them sealed with its own key; a new device asks for them once. With `Use these keys on all my devices` on, they also travel inside your encrypted sync, sealed with your sync passphrase. The account itself (name, address) syncs to all your devices. `Disconnect` removes it and its keys everywhere.

## Keyboard shortcuts
- [[Esc]] on a phone-sized screen goes back from a section to the list.
- The orOS shortcuts are listed under `System` and in [Keyboard shortcuts](help:t/shortcuts).

## Tips
- Buttons that can't be undone, such as `Disconnect` and `Factory reset`, ask you to tap a second time. If you don't, they go back to normal after a few seconds.
- Some apps keep their own settings inside the app, for example the weather in the top bar, which you set in [Weather](app:weather). `System` links to them under `More`.

## Limits
- Opening files from your device works with orOS installed from Chrome or Edge on a computer. On Android, use Share › orOS from Files, Gmail or any app. Firefox and Safari can't hand files to orOS yet: use the Import button inside the app.
- Settings works only inside the orOS desktop. Opened on its own, it asks you to open it from the orOS menu.
- The `Backup folder` options appear only in browsers that can write to a folder, such as Chrome and Edge on a computer.
