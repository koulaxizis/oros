# Household
Share lists with the people you live with: family, a partner, flatmates. Each person uses their own orOS and nobody needs an account. Everything is encrypted on your devices before it leaves them.

## Getting started
- Tap `Create a household`, give it a name (for example "Home") and write your name as the others will see it.
- Tap `+ Invite`. You get a link and a QR code. Send the link, or let the other person scan the code.
- The other person opens the link and taps `Ask to join`. You (or anyone already in the household) see the request in the household and in your notifications. Tap `Let in`.
- A household holds up to 12 people. You can be in several households.

## Sharing a list
- In [To-Do](app:todo), open a list's settings (the pencil on its tab). Under `Household`, pick the household and tap `Share`.
- The list moves into the household: everyone sees it in their own To-Do, with a small house on its tab, and can add, tick and change tasks.
- Changes reach the others in a few seconds while orOS is open, and when they next open orOS otherwise. Offline changes are sent when you are back online; two people changing different things at once both keep their change.
- A task changed last by someone else shows their initials.
- Labels stay yours: they are not shared.
- `Stop sharing (keep my own copy)` takes the list out of the household for everyone and leaves you a copy of your own. `Delete list` removes it for everyone.
- [Meal Planner](app:meals) can send a shopping list straight to a shared list: pick it in the `Add to To-Do` window.

## Invites
- `The link works for` one day up to 30 days, and `For` one person or several.
- `Password` is optional: with it, the link alone is not enough. Tell the password in person, not in the same message as the link.
- `Withdraw` under `Open invites` stops a link at once.
- Nothing is shared with someone who asks to join until a member accepts them.

## People
- Tap your own name to change it or your colour.
- An admin can rename the household, make someone else an admin and remove a person. Removing someone changes the household key, so what is added after that can no longer be read with their old copy.
- `Leave this household` takes the shared lists off your devices; To-Do keeps your own copy of each.
- `Delete the household` (admins) removes it for everyone; everyone keeps their own copy of the lists.

## Removed lately
- A list someone stopped sharing stays here for 30 days. `Share again` puts it back into the household.

## What the others changed
When someone in the household adds, changes or removes shared things, the bell gets one line per household once they are done (after ten quiet minutes, or an hour at most), for example `Shopping: Nikos +3 ~1, −1`: + added, ~ changed, − removed. Today shows the same for the whole day under the household's name. Your own changes, also from your other devices, are left out. This list stays on this device, and only what reached it while orOS was open counts.

With orOS closed: tick `Tell me about changes when orOS is closed` at the bottom of a household's page (Settings › Notifications › `Notifications with orOS closed` must be on for this device). A few minutes after someone else changes something shared, this device gets one notification, at most every 15 minutes. Only "something changed" travels, encrypted on this device for itself; the relay never sees what changed or who changed it.

## In your own folder (Nextcloud, WebDAV)
A household can live in a folder on your own Nextcloud or WebDAV server instead of the orOS relay. When you create it, open `Keep it in a shared folder (Nextcloud, WebDAV)` and write the folder's WebDAV address (on Nextcloud: Files › Settings › WebDAV, plus the folder's name), your user name and an app password (Nextcloud: Settings › Security › Devices & sessions). orOS first checks that the server answers and never lets two people overwrite each other; if your server does not answer browsers, tick `Through the orOS relay` (it passes the requests on and keeps nothing).

Invites, approval, removing someone and the key change work the same. The invite link carries the folder's address and sign-in, so give it a password or send it only to the person it is for. Everything in the folder stays end-to-end encrypted: the server sees only scrambled files. Changes arrive when orOS opens, comes to the front or goes online, and every 10 minutes (there are no instant updates or notifications with orOS closed for a folder). Someone removed from the household can no longer read what is written after the removal, but still has the folder's sign-in: change the app password when that matters.

## A copy on your orOS disk
Every household you are in is also kept on your orOS disk, in the folder `Household backup` (one file per household and device). Your own sync takes it to your cloud encrypted with your passphrase, like every file of the disk, so your other devices have it too. orOS writes it after it has reached the household, at most every 10 minutes and only when something changed.

If a household's box is gone (deleted, or nobody opened it for 180 days), its page says that nothing is lost: what was shared comes back from this copy, even on a device that had lost it. `Start it again` makes a new household with all of it and you as its admin; then invite the others again (do it on one device only). `Remove from this device` brings the lists back to your apps as your own copies.

Households you are no longer in are listed under `Copies on your orOS disk`: `Bring back to my apps`, `Start again` or `Delete` (from your disk, on all your devices).

## Privacy and safety
- The orOS server (a small Cloudflare worker) keeps only scrambled data it cannot read, and forgets a household nobody has opened for 180 days.
- The key stays on your devices and travels with your own sync (Dropbox, WebDAV), so your other devices are in the household too.
- A household is meant for people you trust: everyone in it can change or delete the shared lists.

## Limits
- Up to 64 shared lists per household, 300 KB each, 5 MB per household.
- Reminders for due dates come only from lists of your own, and shared lists are not in the search yet.
