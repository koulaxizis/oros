# Calendar
Calendar keeps your events, with times, places, labels, repeats and reminders. It also shows dates from your other orOS apps, such as birthdays from Contacts and due tasks from To-Do, so you see everything in one place.

## Getting started
Calendar opens on the current month with today selected. Tap or click a day to see its events below the grid, then press `Add` to create an event on that day. Use ‹ and › to move back and forward, and `Today` to jump back.

Switch between `Day`, `Week`, `Month`, `Year` and `Agenda` with the buttons under the title:
- `Day` shows one day as an hour grid, with all-day events in a strip at the top and a line at the current time. Tap or click an empty hour to add an event at that time.
- `Week` shows seven days side by side.
- `Year` shows the 12 months small, with a dot under days that have events. Tap a day to open it in `Day`, or a month's name to open that month.
- `Agenda` lists what's coming in the next 30 days; `Show more` adds more days.

## Quick add
`Quick add`, next to `Add`, takes one line written the way you'd say it, in English or Greek, and opens the event window filled in, so you can check it before you save. For example:
- "Coffee with Nikos Thursday 6pm at Syntagma"
- "Καφές με Νίκο Πέμπτη 6μμ στο Σύνταγμα"
- "dentist 15/11 9:30"
- "every Monday gym 7am"
- "αύριο 10-12 σύσκεψη"

It understands days ("today", "tomorrow", "Friday", "next Friday", "in 3 days", "15/11", "15 Nov", «αύριο», «την άλλη Τρίτη», «3 Μαΐου»), times and ranges ("6pm", "18:30", "10-12", "at 7", «στις 9 το βράδυ», «6μμ»), repeats ("every Monday", "every weekday", "every 2 weeks", "monthly", «κάθε Δευτέρα», «τις Τρίτες», «καθημερινά»), and a place after "at", "@" or «στο / στη / στην». What is left becomes the title. While you type, a short preview shows what it understood. Dates are day first (15/11 is 15 November). A bare hour from 1 to 6 counts as the afternoon ("at 3" is 15:00); write "3am" or «3 το πρωί» for the morning.

## Add and edit events
The event window has:
- `Title`. It's the only field you must fill in.
- `All day`, or a `Time`: pick a start and, if you like, an end (`No end`). You can type a time or choose one from the list.
- `Starts on (day)` and `Ends on (day)`: set a later end day for an event that lasts several days, such as a trip or a conference.
- `Time zone` (timed events only): leave it on `This device` or pick the zone the event is in, for example a call with someone in New York. The Calendar shows the event at your own local time, with a small ◷ mark and the original time underneath.
- `Repeat`: daily, weekly on the event's weekday, every weekday (Mon–Fri), every 2 weeks, monthly on the same date or on the same weekday (such as "the 2nd Tuesday" or "the last Friday"), or yearly. `Custom…` lets you choose every N days, weeks, months or years, several weekdays, and for months either the day of the month or the weekday. `Repeat ends`: `Never`, `On a date`, or `After` a number of times. A short summary under the choices says what you've picked. A repeating event can also last several days, such as a 3-day course every month.
- `Reminder`: from 5 minutes up to 5 days before.
- `Location`, a label and a `Note`.

Tap or click an event to edit it. For a repeating event you're asked first: `This occurrence` or `Entire series`. `Delete event` asks to confirm, and an `Undo` button appears for a few seconds afterwards. `QR code` in the same window opens [QR Generator](app:qr) with a code for the event, which phones can add to their own calendar. `Email` (when [Mail](app:mail) has an account) opens a new message about the event: its title as the subject, the date, time, place and notes as the text, and any email addresses written in the event as recipients. `Send…` sends the event to someone as a link: they see it in any browser and can add it to their calendar (a single occurrence of a series goes as that one day). `Track time` (when [Timesheet](app:timesheet) is installed) starts a timer there named after the event; if a timer is already running, it's left alone.

When [Weather](app:weather) has a forecast for your city, the days it covers show a small weather icon (the week and the day show the high and low too). It comes from what Weather already downloaded on this device; the Calendar never looks it up online.

On a computer you can drag an event onto another day in the `Month` view, or onto another day's column in `Week`, to move it. An event that lasts several days moves as a whole. For a repeating event you're asked whether to move `This occurrence` or the `Entire series`; `Undo` puts it back.

Tap or click a location to open it in [Maps](app:maps). Maps can also send a planned route to Calendar as a new event.

## Labels and feeds
The chips above the calendar are your labels. You start with `Personal`, `Work` and `Family`. Tap or click a chip to hide or show those events. `＋ Manage labels` lets you add, rename, recolour and delete labels; a label still used by events can't be deleted. On a phone the chips are folded behind one `Labels & feeds` button that shows how many are on; tap it to open or close the list. Your device remembers whether you left it open.

Filters:
- `No label`: your events without a label have their own chip. Turn everything else off to see only them.
- Only one chip: long press a chip (phone), or right-click or Alt+click it (computer), and every other chip turns off. Do it again on the same chip and they all come back.
- `All` turns every chip on. `Moon & Sky`, `Medicines` and `Doctor visits` stay as they are; they turn on only from their own chip.
- `None` turns every chip off, so you can turn on just the ones you want.
- `My events` shows only your own events (every label and `No label`) and hides all feeds.
Filters only hide rows from view; they never change or delete anything.

Other apps add read-only rows, each with its own chip so you can hide them:
- `Birthdays`, `Anniversaries` and `Custom Feed` from [Contacts](app:contacts).
- `To-Do`: unfinished tasks on their due date, from [To-Do](app:todo).
- `Kanban` cards with a due date, `Habits` you completed, `Mood` entries, `Cycle` periods, `Workouts`, `Plants` care, and `Screen Pet` moments.
- `Garage`: renewals and service due dates from [Garage](app:garage).
- `Budget`: upcoming recurring entries from [Budget](app:budget).
- `Pet health`: vaccines, deworming, visits, next due dates and birthdays from [Pet health](app:petcare).
- `Customers`: open follow-ups and the expected close day of open deals, from [CRM](app:crm).
- `Travel`: trip days and timed itinerary entries from [Travel](app:travel).
- `Baby`: milestones, vaccines, doctor visits and monthly age from [Baby](app:baby).
- `Together`: anniversaries, every 100 days, fun numbers, moments and special days from [Together](app:together).
- `Chores` from [Chore Wheel](app:chores); only yours if you picked who you are there.
- `Work time`: time per project and day from [Timesheet](app:timesheet).
- `Meals` planned in [Meal Planner](app:meals), such as "Dinner: Fasolada".
- `Death anniversaries` from [Family Tree](app:familytree), such as "Maria · 10 years since death", for people with an exact date of death.
- `Invoices`: unpaid invoices on their due date, from [Invoices](app:invoices).
- `Media Shelf`: the day you finished a book, film, album or game, and release dates on your wishlist, from [Media Shelf](app:shelf).
- `Publishing`: the release date of your titles and the open steps of their release plan, and the social posts you planned, from [Publishing](app:publish).
- `Holidays` (Greek public holidays), `Name days` of your contacts, and `World days` such as international observances.
- `Moon & Sky`: moon phases, equinoxes and solstices, meteor showers and eclipses from [Moon & Sky](app:sky). This one starts off: tap or click its chip to turn it on.
- `Medicines`: the first and last day of a medicine course and the day a medicine you count runs out, from [Health](app:health). Health data is private, so this one starts off too.
- `Doctor visits`: your doctor's appointments at their time and the day a vaccine's next dose is due, from the `Record` tab of [Health](app:health). It starts off too.

Tapping a feed row opens the app it comes from. To change it, edit it there.

## Subscribe to a calendar
`More` › `Subscribe to a calendar…` adds a calendar that someone publishes as a .ics address (it starts with webcal:// or https://), such as a team calendar, school holidays, sports fixtures, or a Google or Outlook calendar's "secret address in iCal format". Paste the address, give it a name and a colour if you like, and press `Subscribe`. Its events show read-only, with their own chip, like the other feeds. Tap one to see it in full; `Copy to my calendar` opens it as a new event of your own.

The Calendar reads a subscribed calendar when you open it (if it's been more than half an hour) and every 3 hours while it stays open; `Refresh` reads it at once. Offline, you see the last copy. Many servers let the browser read the calendar directly. Those that don't are read through the orOS relay that [Reader](app:feeds) uses, with its address from Reader's `Settings` or from [Mail](app:mail); without one, the window tells you so. In the same window you can rename a subscription, tap its dot to change its colour, or `Remove` it (`Undo` brings it back).

## Share a calendar with your household
Once you're in a household (the [Household](app:household) app), a label can become a calendar you share. In `Manage labels`, tap the house icon next to the label and press `Share`. Its events leave your own calendar and go to the household: everyone there sees them with the label's colour and can add, change, move and delete them from their own orOS. Shared calendars are listed under `Shared with a household`, where a new name or colour changes it for everyone. A small circle with initials on an event shows who in the household changed it last.

Reminders stay yours: each person picks their own in the event's `Reminder`, and orOS reminds you even when the Calendar is closed. The events are encrypted on your devices before they leave, and they don't go into your own sync copy or your .oroscal file (an .ics export and `Send calendar to someone` include them). `Stop sharing` takes the calendar out of the household for everyone and leaves you an own copy with its events; `Delete` leaves you none. Either way, the others can bring it back from [Household](app:household) for 30 days.

## Share free times
`More` › `Share free times…` makes a link with the times you're free, so someone can pick one, like a booking page. Choose what it's for, the length (15 minutes to 2 hours), how many days ahead (3 to 30), the hours of the day, and whether Saturday and Sunday count. Your name and email come from your [Mail](app:mail) account when there is one. The window says how many free times it found, and `Make the link…` opens [Send to someone](help:t/share), where you copy, share or email the link.

Free times are worked out on this device from your own events with a time and from the subscribed calendars you show; all-day events (birthdays, holidays) don't block time, and times already past are left out. The other person opens the link in any browser, sees the times in their own time zone, picks one, and gets `Add to calendar (.ics)`, `Add to my orOS`, and an email back to you, written for them, with the time they chose. Nothing is booked by itself: the time is yours to confirm when the email arrives. Like every shared link, the times travel inside the link; no server keeps them.

## Send your calendar to someone
`More` › `Send calendar to someone…` puts your own events in a link: all of them or one label's, whole or only some dates (`This month`, `Next 3 months`, `This year`, or `Choose dates…`). The window says how many events go; `Next` opens [Send to someone](help:t/share), where you copy, share or email the link, add a password, or choose how long it lasts. Events from subscriptions and from other apps (birthdays, cycle, habits) stay here. A calendar with many events goes as a link with an expiry. The other person sees it month by month in any browser and can add it to their calendar as an .ics file or to their orOS.

## Print
`More` › `Print…` prints the view you're looking at, `Day`, `Week`, `Month`, `Year` or `Agenda`, on a clean white page: the month as a table with times and titles, the week and agenda as lists per day, the year as 12 small months with days that have events in bold. To make a PDF, pick "Save as PDF" as the printer.

## Import from Google Calendar
`More` › `Import from Google Calendar…` copies your events from Google once. Sign in to Google in the small window; orOS reads your calendars (read only), lists them with how many events are new or already here, and gives the access back to Google right away: nothing is kept, no password and no access, and nothing changes at Google. Your own calendars start ticked; holidays, birthdays and other people's calendars start unticked. Press `Import` to save the ticked ones in one go; the message that follows has `Undo`.

Events come in the way an .ics import brings them: times and time zones, all-day and multi-day events, repeats (with skipped and moved dates), places, notes and the first reminder that matches the Calendar's choices. Each calendar becomes a label of its name, or uses your label with the same name. Importing again adds only what is missing; events already here are left as they are. Google must have this enabled for orOS; if it answers 403, the service is not switched on yet. To use your own Google app id, set it in [Contacts](app:contacts) › `From an account` › `Use my own app id`.

## Search and statistics
The search box looks through the title, note and location of your own events and lists every match by date. `Stats` shows how many events you have, how many fall in the next 30 days, how many repeat, and a breakdown by label.

## On this phone (Android app)
In the orOS Android app, `On this phone` puts your events, with their reminders and label colours, in the phone's own Calendar as an "orOS" calendar, so other calendar apps and widgets show them too. Turn on `Show on this phone` and tap `Allow access` (Android asks once). Repeating events stay repeating, and every change you make here follows. It works both ways: events you add, change or delete in the phone's "orOS" calendar come into orOS the next time you open orOS (the newest change wins when both sides changed). Changing or cancelling one occurrence of a repeating event on the phone skips that day in the series and, for a change, adds it as a single event. Feed rows go to a separate, read-only "orOS feeds" calendar (holidays, name days and the other apps' dates, from two weeks back to six months ahead), except labels you have hidden here; they follow each time you open the Calendar. "Add to calendar" in other apps and .ics links can also open orOS (pick orOS when Android asks): a new event opens filled in, or the import asks first. Turning the switch off removes the orOS calendar from the phone again. The button appears only in the Android app, and the choice is kept on this phone only.

## Save and open an orOS file
Press `More`, then `orOS file (.oroscal)`, to save all your own events and labels as one `.oroscal` file, for example to move them to another device without sync or to keep a copy. The file is a plain ZIP: unzipped it holds the events as `content.json` plus `preview/calendar.ics`, which other calendar apps open. It is not encrypted, so keep it somewhere safe. Subscribed calendars, the Google sign-in and this device's settings are not in the file, and feed rows never are.

`Open orOS file…` (or `Unzipped orOS folder…`, or "Open with" orOS on the computer) shows how many events and labels are new and how many are already here, then `Add` adds only the new ones, with their own ids: opening the same file again adds nothing. An event already here keeps what you entered here, and an event you deleted here stays deleted; a file never deletes anything. Tick `Update with newer versions` to let events that were changed later in the file take the file's copy. `Undo` in the message removes what was added.

## Sync and backup
Your events, labels and subscriptions (address, name, colour) sync between your devices through [orOS sync](help:t/sync). The events of a subscribed calendar are not synced: each device reads them from the address itself and keeps its own copy (on this device only). Which of your own labels (and `No label`) you've hidden is not saved: they all show again next time. The `Holidays`, `Name days`, `World days`, `Work time`, `Moon & Sky`, `Medicines` and `Doctor visits` chips remember whether they are on or off, on this device only. Feed rows are never stored in Calendar; they come from the other apps' own data.

`Export` saves your own events as a .ics file that other calendar apps can open, with repeats, labels and reminders. Feed rows and subscribed calendars aren't included.

`Import`, next to `Export`, reads a .ics file from Google Calendar, Outlook, your phone or another orOS Calendar. Before anything is saved you see how many events are new, updated or already in the Calendar, and you choose `Import` or `Cancel`. Importing the same file again updates those events instead of adding copies. A category in the file is matched to one of your labels with the same name; no new labels are made. Cancelled events are skipped. With orOS installed on a computer you can also open a .ics file straight from your device with `Open with`; see [opening files](help:t/install).

For a full copy, see [backup](help:t/backup).

## Keyboard shortcuts
- [[←]] / [[→]] go to the previous or next day, week, month, year or agenda page.
- [[Esc]] clears the search.
- In the time boxes, [[↑]] / [[↓]] move through the list and [[Enter]] picks a time.

## Tips
- Reminders show in orOS even when Calendar itself is closed, as long as orOS is open. See [Notifications](help:t/notifications).
- Opening a reminder takes you straight to the event.

## Limits
- Reminders can't fire when the browser or the orOS tab is closed.
- Subscribed calendars are read while the Calendar is open (when it opens and every 3 hours), not in the background. A server that blocks browsers needs the orOS relay. A subscription keeps up to 3000 events; one-off events that ended more than a year ago are left out, and its events have no reminders.
- Quick add reads English and Greek, with dates day first. When it gets something wrong, fix it in the event window before saving.
- Free times come from this device's calendar only and don't know about other calendars you haven't subscribed to here. The person who picks a time answers you by email; nothing updates by itself, and the link keeps the times it had when you made it.
- Dragging works with a mouse only, not by touch. On a phone, open the event and change its days instead.
- On a phone the days in `Year` are small; tap the month's name to open it larger.
- When importing, a repeating event whose rule the Calendar can't hold (for example "the 1st and 3rd Monday", several days of the month, or by week number) comes in with only its first date, and an hourly rule likewise. The preview tells you how many.
- Exported time zones are written by name only. Most calendar apps understand them; a few older ones may show the time in their own zone.
- On an older orOS version that hasn't updated yet, the newer repeat choices and time zones show in a simpler form until it updates.
- An event with a time zone that you edit in the phone's own Calendar keeps its zone only after orOS reads the change back. A file can bring in at most 5000 events and be at most 5 MB.
