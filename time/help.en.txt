# Time

A big clock in five styles, a world clock with a meeting planner, sunrise, sunset and the moon phase, plus alarms, kitchen timers, a stopwatch with laps, a Pomodoro timer and a stage timer for conferences. Tap the clock in the orOS top bar to open it.

## Getting started

Pick a clock style under the big clock: `Digital`, `Analog`, `Binary`, `Flip` or `Neon`. Below the clock you'll see today's date and your UTC offset. Everything uses the 24-hour format.

## World clock

- Tap a quick chip (`+ Athens`, `+ London`, `+ New York`, `+ Tokyo`), or `Add` and type a city or a time zone, in English or Greek (accents don't matter). More than 500 cities are built in, plus every time zone your browser knows. Tap a result to add it.
- Each row shows the time there and how it compares with yours, for example "Today, +3 h" or "Tomorrow, −7 h".
- Double-click a name to change it (`Change zone`). The × deletes it (`Delete zone`). A row is one time zone: picking another city in the same zone renames the row.

## Meeting planner

Find an hour that suits people in different places.

- Tap `Show` next to `Meeting planner`. It starts with your own time zone and the first places of your world clock. `Add place` adds more (up to 6), and the × removes one (at least 2 stay).
- Each place gets a 24-hour strip for the `Date` you pick, lined up so that a column is the same moment everywhere. Green is working hours (`Work from` … `until`, Monday to Friday), plain is awake (07:00–22:00), grey is night. A ★ above a column means everyone is at work.
- Tap an hour, or use [[←]] and [[→]], to pick the start, and choose the `Length`. The box below shows the slot in every place.
- `Add to Calendar` opens a new event in [Calendar](app:calendar) with the start and end in your time and the times of every place in its note. Nothing is saved until you save the event there. `Copy` copies the same lines for an email or a chat.
- The places, the working hours and the length stay on this device.

## Sun & Moon

This section shows `Sunrise`, `Sunset`, `Solar noon`, `Day length`, where the sun is now, `Moon age`, the moon phase and its `Illumination`. It's all calculated on your device, so it works offline.

It uses the top-bar weather city: the city pinned in [Weather](help:a/weather), or your first city there. To use another place, tap `Set location`, type `Latitude` and `Longitude` or tap `Use device location`, then `Save`. `Use weather location` goes back to the weather location. If you use your device location without saving it, it's only kept until you close the app.

## Alarms and timers

- **`Alarm`**: set a time, add a `Label…` if you like, tick `Daily` for every day, then `Add alarm`. A time that has already passed today is set for tomorrow. The × deletes an alarm.
- **`Timer`**: give it a name if you like (for example "Pasta"), set hours, minutes and seconds, pick a sound (`Beep`, `Chime`, `Bell`, `Digital` or `Gentle`, or one of your melodies from [Ringtone Composer](help:a/ringtones)) and tap `Start`. A chip such as `+ 5 min` starts one at once. Several timers can run together, each in its own card with a progress bar: `Pause` and `Resume`, `+1 min`, `Reset`, and the × deletes it. Tap the name to rename it, or change its sound in the card. When a timer ends it rings with its own sound and its card counts how long ago that was; `Restart` runs it again. Timers keep running when you close Time or orOS. In the last minute of a timer of 2 minutes or more, the countdown fills the screen. Tap it or press [[Esc]] to hide it (the timer keeps going).
- **`Stopwatch`**: `Start` and `Stop`, `Lap` while it runs, `Reset` when it's stopped. Laps are listed newest first with the lap time and the total, and the fastest and slowest are marked. `Copy laps` copies them as text and `Save CSV` saves them for a spreadsheet. The stopwatch keeps counting when you close Time or orOS, and its laps stay.
- **`Pomodoro`**: set `Work` and `Break` minutes and tap `Start`. When a `Focus` leg ends, a `Break` is ready to start, and so on. The counter shows how many sessions you've finished. `Reset` goes back to `Focus`. With `Log focus in Timesheet` set to a project (or `No project`), each `Focus` leg is added to [Timesheet](app:timesheet) as the task "Pomodoro", even if you close Time; stopping early logs the minutes you focused. The choice stays on this device.

When an alarm, a timer or a Pomodoro leg ends, orOS shows a box with `Snooze 9m` and `Dismiss` and plays a sound (a timer plays its own). It stops by itself after 30 seconds and also lands in your [notifications](help:t/notifications). It rings in any app, not only in Time. In the orOS app for Android, with reminders through the app turned on, alarms and timers also ring while orOS is closed (see [notifications](help:t/notifications)).

## Stage timer for conferences

The `Stage` tab is a timer for talks, panels and shows, with a rundown and a big countdown the speaker can read from far away.

- **Event:** tap `New event`, give it a `Name`, a `Date` and, if you like, the time it `Starts at`. Set when the clock turns yellow and red (`Yellow at (min left)`, `Red at (min left)`) and whether it should `Flash when time is up`. `Event settings` changes them later; `Duplicate event` copies an event with its rundown (handy as a template).
- **Rundown:** `Add item` adds a talk or break with a `Title`, a `Speaker` and a `Length`. The `Type` is a `Countdown`, a `Count up`, `Until a clock time` (for example a coffee break until 11:00) or the `Time of day`. Each item can have its own yellow and red times and `Notes (only you see them)`. Tick `Start the next item by itself at zero` for breaks that should flow on. Tap an item to edit it, ↑ and ↓ to move it, ▶ to start from it.
- **Running:** `Start`, `Pause` and `Resume` run the clock of the current item, `Next ▶` moves on (and starts the next one if the clock was running), `◀ Previous` goes back, `−1 min` and `+1 min` change the time left (hold the button for five minutes), and `Restart item` starts the current item again. When the time is up the clock keeps counting in red with a minus sign, so you see how far a speaker ran over.
- **Schedule:** with a start time, each item shows when it was planned and, in colour, when it is now expected to start. The line above the clock shows the planned and expected end of the day and how many minutes you are behind or ahead.
- **Stage view:** `Stage view` fills the screen with only the big clock, the title, the speaker, a progress bar, the next item and the time of day, always on black. Move the mouse or tap to show `Black screen`, `Full screen` and `Close`.
- **Keys and clickers:** [[Space]] start or pause, [[→]] or [[Page Down]] next, [[←]] or [[Page Up]] previous, [[+]] and [[−]] one minute, [[B]] black screen, [[M]] message on or off, [[F]] stage view, [[Esc]] close it. Presenter clickers send Page Up and Page Down, so they work as previous and next.
- **Message to the speaker:** type a message and press `Show` (or [[Enter]]), or tap a ready one such as `Please wrap up` or `Questions now`. Pick a `Colour` and tick `Flash` to make it stand out; `Hide` takes it away. `Save` keeps your own messages as chips (they sync); the × deletes one.
- **Stage display on a second screen:** `Open stage display` opens the speaker's picture in a window of its own. On Chrome or Edge with two monitors it moves to the other screen by itself (the browser asks once); elsewhere drag it there. Double-click it or press [[F]] for full screen. It keeps going even if you close Time or open another app, and `Black screen` blanks it.
- **Stage output:** `Layout` is `Clock, titles and next`, `Clock only` or `Clock and rundown`. `Mirror (for a screen seen in a reflection)` flips the picture. `Logo…` puts your logo in the corner; it stays on this device. The box above the buttons shows the message and the black screen as the stage sees them.
- **Sound:** tick `Sound at yellow, red and zero (this device)` for a short beep at each change. It is off by default, because the laptop on stage is often connected to the speakers.
- **Another device:** under `Other devices`, `Connect a device` pairs a phone, tablet, laptop or smart-TV browser with this computer. Choose what it will do: `Remote control` (start, pause, next, ± minute, black screen and messages from a phone), `Stage display` (the speaker's picture on a screen on stage) or `Moderator` (sees the clock and the rundown and can send you requests such as `1 more minute please`, but changes nothing). Then `Show a code`. On the other device open orOS › Time › Stage › `Join with a code` and type the 6-digit code, or scan the QR code. Each device needs `Allow` here, and each gets its own code; up to six at a time. A moderator's request shows above the buttons: `Show to speaker` puts it on stage, `OK` dismisses it, and the moderator sees that you read it. Paired devices come back by themselves after a reload; `Leave` on the device or × here ends a pairing.
- **Quick add:** under the rundown, `Quick add` adds ready-made items to the end: `Talk 20`, `Keynote 20 + Q&A 10`, `Panel 45`, `Coffee break 15` and `Lunch 60`. Rename them by tapping them.
- **Import:** `Import…` adds a rundown to the end of the open event, never replacing what is there. `From a CSV file` reads the columns title, speaker, duration and start, in that order or under a header row that names them (in English or Greek; a spreadsheet saved as CSV works). A duration is minutes (`20`), `mm:ss` (`12:30`), `h:mm:ss` or `1h 30m`; a row without one lasts until the next row's start. `From a Calendar day` takes that day's timed events of the orOS Calendar in order and turns the gaps between them into breaks. You see what was found before `Add`.
- **Export and report:** `Export CSV` saves the rundown (it reads back with Import). `Report` compares each item's planned and actual start and length, with the total over or under; `Download CSV` saves it and `Print / PDF` prints it (choose "Save as PDF" in the print dialog for a file). The actual times are what this device recorded while running the clock.
- **Hard stop:** in `Event settings`, `Must end by (hard stop)` is the time the day cannot go past, for example when the room must be free. The line above the clock then says how many minutes have to be cut to end on time.
- **Search and Today:** the orOS search finds events, talks and speakers and opens the Stage tab on them. On an event's day, the Today page shows the next session and when it is expected to start (later if the day runs behind); events on the days ahead show at their start time.

## Sync and backup

These sync through [orOS sync](help:t/sync): the clock style, world clock zones, Pomodoro lengths and session count, the manual Sun & Moon location, and your alarms (they travel with the orOS settings). Stage events and their rundowns sync too; the item that is running, its clock and the sound choice and what the stage screen shows (message, black screen, layout, logo) stay on this device, so a phone at home never moves the clock on stage. Timers, the stopwatch and its laps, the meeting planner's places and a Pomodoro leg in progress stay on this device; a timer rings only on the device where you started it. Only the Stage rundown has an import and an export (CSV), and the stopwatch laps a CSV export; the rest of Time has none.

## Tips

- A Pomodoro leg that ends while Time is closed still counts when you open it again.
- Daily alarms are good for routines like medicine or a stretch break.
- In the kitchen, name each timer after the dish and give it its own sound, so you know which one is ringing.
- In the meeting planner, the columns with a ★ are the easy choices.

## Limits

- In a browser, alarms and timers ring only while orOS is open (in a browser tab or as an installed app). If orOS was closed at that time, they ring as soon as you open it again. The orOS app for Android, with reminders through the app turned on, rings them with orOS closed, using the phone's alarm sound instead of the timer's own sound.
- The city list is built in (about 550 cities). Any other place is found by its time zone, for example "Jujuy" or "America/Jujuy". The list of time zones comes from your browser.
- The meeting planner counts Monday to Friday as working days everywhere and uses the same working hours for every place.
- Up to 20 timers and 999 laps.
- The Stage clock runs while Time is open. If you close Time, the clock goes on from the right time when you open it again, but sounds and "start the next item by itself" only happen while it is open.
- The stage display window needs pop-ups allowed for orOS. It shows what this device stores, so it belongs on a screen connected to the same computer.
- Paired devices need Time open on the computer and an internet connection for the first contact (the orOS relay). On the same Wi-Fi they then talk directly; otherwise the relay carries everything, encrypted with a key from the code. The logo is not sent to other devices.
- The report and the Today card use the times this device recorded. If the clock ran on another computer, its report is on that computer.
