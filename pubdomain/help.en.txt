# Public Domain Calculator

Find out when a book, a song, a painting or a film becomes free to use, in Greece and in 20 more countries.

## Getting started

Type an author or a work in the search box (`Author or work, e.g. Kazantzakis`) and pick a result. The app reads the dates from Wikidata and shows:

- the author or the work, with years of birth and death and links to `Wikipedia` and `Wikidata`;
- a card for `My country` with the answer;
- the list `In other countries`.

The country list and the rules are stored in the app, so working out the answer needs no connection. Only the search does.

## Look up an author or a work

- Pick a person to see when everything they wrote or made is free. Pick a work (a novel, a film, a song) to see when that work is free. For a work, the app finds its creators and the last one to die counts.
- Search in Greek or in English. Results show the Wikidata description, which helps you tell people with the same name apart.
- Your last 10 lookups appear under `Recent`. Tap one to look it up again, or tap `Clear` to forget them.

## Enter the dates yourself

Tap the pencil button (`Enter dates yourself`) to open the form. It works offline.

1. Choose `One author`, `Several authors` or `Anonymous`.
2. Enter each `Year of death`, or tick `Still alive`. With `Several authors`, use `+ Add author`. For a film, add the director, the screenwriter, the dialogue writer and the composer of its music.
3. Optionally enter the `Year of first publication`. Anonymous works need it, and it matters in the United States.

The answer updates as you type. Tap the pencil again to close the form.

## Reading the answer

Each country shows one of four answers:

- `Public domain` (✓), with the date it became free. Works become free on 1 January after the term ends.
- `Protected` (©), with the last protected day and how many years are left.
- `Possibly free` (?), when the answer depends on something the app doesn't know: an exact date of death, or in the United States the year a work was published.
- `Cannot tell` (–), when a date is missing. The card says which one.

Under the answer you see the rule (for example `Rule: 70 years after death`), the law and article, notes for that country, and a `Source` link to the text the rule was checked against.

>! A rule marked `This rule has not yet been checked at its source.` is based on secondary sources. Check it before relying on it.

## Other countries

- Choose your country in `My country`. Greece is the default, and your choice stays on this device.
- `A–Z` sorts the list by name, `Free first` puts the countries where the work is free at the top.
- Tap a country to see its rule, notes and source.

## Sync and backup

Nothing in this app is synced or backed up: your country, the list order and your recent lookups stay on this device. See [orOS sync](help:t/sync) for the apps that do sync.

## Keyboard shortcuts

- [[/]] jumps to the search box. [[Esc]] clears it.
- [[↓]] moves from the search box into the results, [[↑]] / [[↓]] move through them and [[Enter]] picks one.

## Tips

- If Wikidata shows only the decade of death, or more than one date, the card warns you and the answer becomes a range.
- A person with no date of death who was born less than 110 years ago is treated as living.

## Limits

- This is information, not legal advice. The answer is about the original work: translations, new editions with notes, arrangements, recordings and photographs of a work have rights of their own.
- Some special cases are explained but not counted: France's wartime extensions, Russia's extra 4 years for war veterans, Japan's wartime extension, and the US notice and renewal rules.
- The search sends what you type to [Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org/), which needs no account.
