# Install orOS as an app
You can use orOS in a normal browser tab, but you can also install it. Installed, orOS gets its own icon and opens in its own window, without the browser's address bar. It works offline either way.

## Android
In Chrome on Android:
1. Open orOS and tap `orOS` to open the menu.
2. If you see `Install orOS`, tap it and confirm.
3. If you don't, open the browser's own menu and choose the option to install the app or add it to the home screen.

orOS then appears with your other apps.

## iPhone and iPad
On iPhone and iPad, install orOS from Safari:
1. Open orOS in Safari.
2. Tap the Share button.
3. Choose **Add to Home Screen**, then **Add**.

Open orOS from the new icon on your home screen. It opens full screen, like any other app.

## Computer (Chrome or Edge)
1. Open orOS and click `orOS` to open the menu.
2. Click `Install orOS` and confirm.

You can also use the install icon in the browser's address bar, or the browser's menu. After that, orOS opens in its own window and you can pin it to your taskbar or dock.

> `Install orOS` shows only when your browser can install it right now. Once orOS is installed, the row disappears. The same button is in `Settings` under `System`, as `Install orOS as an app`.

## Open files from your computer
With orOS installed from Chrome or Edge on Windows, Linux or Mac, orOS appears in your system's `Open with` for the file types its apps can read. Right-click a file, choose `Open with` and pick orOS; the file opens in the right app:
- [Writer](app:writer): .docx, .odt, .rtf, .md, .txt and its own .orosdoc files
- [Spreadsheet](app:spreadsheet): .xlsx, .xls, .ods, .csv
- [Atelier](app:atelier): .pptx; [Slides](app:slides), [Layout](app:layout) (also .sla and .idml) and Atelier: their own orOS files
- [Reader](app:feeds) and [Podcasts](app:podcasts): .opml
- [Mind Map](app:mindmap): .mm, .xmind, and on request .opml, .md and .txt
- [Family Tree](app:familytree): .ged, .gedcom
- [Ringtone Composer](app:ringtones): .rtttl, .rtx
- [Photo Lab](app:photolab): .png, .jpg, .webp, .gif, .bmp, .avif, .psd, .ora
- [Library](app:library): .epub, .pdf
- [Maps](app:maps): .gpx, .kml, .geojson
- [Chess](app:chess): .pgn
- [Montage](app:montage): .mp4, .m4v, .webm, .mov
- [Notes](app:notes): .txt, .md, as a choice next to Writer
- [Text Tools](app:texttools): .txt, .csv, .tsv, as a choice next to the apps above
- [Calendar](app:calendar): .ics, with the import preview first
- [Contacts](app:contacts): .vcf, with a question before importing

The file is imported as a new item, or through the app's own preview; orOS never writes back to the original file. When more than one app can open a type, orOS asks which one, and `Always use this app` remembers your choice on this device. One file opens at a time.

In [Settings](app:settings) under `Opening files` you see the list, pick the app for shared types, and find how to make orOS the default for a type on Windows, Linux or Mac. Only you can do that, in your system's settings.

On Android, with orOS installed from Chrome, use `Share` › orOS in Files, Gmail or any app: the file opens in the matching orOS app. orOS can't be the default for opening files on Android yet.

> Firefox, Safari and iPhone can't hand files to orOS yet. There, open the app and use its `Import` or `Open` button.

## Single apps
Some orOS apps can also be installed on their own: **orOS Notes**, **orOS Calendar**, **orOS Bookmarks** and **orOS To-Do**. A single app gets its own icon and window, opens straight into the app and works offline, like orOS itself.
1. Open [Settings](app:settings) and choose `Single apps`.
2. Press `Install` next to the app. Its page opens in a new tab.
3. Install it there: `Install` in the address bar of Chrome or Edge, or `Add to Home screen` in Chrome's menu on Android, or Share › `Add to Home Screen` in Safari.

Inside a single app, the button at the top left carries the app's name. It opens a small menu: `Sync and cloud`, `Settings`, `Help` and `Open the whole orOS`. An app opened from there shows `Back`, which returns you to the single app.

- **Your data:** on a device that also has orOS, the single app and orOS share the same data at once. On a device with only the single app, the data stays on the device until you turn on sync in `Sync and cloud`, with the same Dropbox and passphrase as orOS. Then it joins your encrypted orOS cloud, both ways.
- **Icon shortcuts:** long-press the icon (Android) or right-click it (Windows) for `New page`, `New event`, `Add a bookmark` or `New task`.
- **Open with:** on Windows and Linux, orOS Notes is in `Open with` for .txt and .md files and orOS Calendar for .ics files.
- **Share:** on Android, `Share` › orOS Notes makes a new page, › orOS To-Do a new task, › orOS Calendar a new event and › orOS Bookmarks a new bookmark. Nothing is saved until you confirm.
- **Badge:** orOS To-Do shows on its icon how many tasks are due today or overdue, where the system supports badges.
- **Notifications:** each single app shows only its own reminders. orOS keeps showing all of them.

> A single app updates by itself, like orOS, while its window is in the background.

## How updates arrive
You never need to download updates yourself. orOS checks for a new version every time it starts, every hour while it stays open, and when you come back to it or get your connection back.

A new version downloads in the background. orOS then reloads itself at a quiet moment, so it never interrupts you:
- you are on the desktop, with no app open;
- the menu and any windows are closed;
- no alarm is ringing and the radio isn't playing;
- no changes are waiting to be synced.

After the update, a notification says `orOS was updated to` and the new version number. You can always see the version you have in `About orOS + shortcuts`.

> To check for an update right away, press [[Ctrl+Alt+Shift+U]]. You'll see `Checking for updates…`.
