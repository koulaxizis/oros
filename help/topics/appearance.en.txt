# Appearance
Make orOS look the way you like: language, light or dark theme, colour skin, wallpaper, seasonal decorations, a screen pet and the weather in the top bar. Most of these are in the [Settings](app:settings) app, under `Appearance`: open the menu and choose `Settings`. The top-bar weather is set in the Weather app.

## Quick settings
The button at the right end of the top bar (the network and power symbols) opens Quick settings, as on a Linux desktop. [[Ctrl+Alt+Shift+M]] opens it too.

- The round buttons at the top: the battery (on a laptop or phone running on it), the language, `Settings`, `Lock orOS` (when the screen lock is on) and the power button with `Restart orOS`. On orOS USB the power button also restarts or shuts down the computer.
- `Network` shows whether you are online. `Sync` shows its state; tap it to sync now, or the arrow for the sync settings.
- `Dark style`, `Large text`, `High contrast` and `On-screen keyboard` switch on and off with one tap. Settings has the full choices for each.
- `Do Not Disturb` keeps notifications quiet: they still arrive in the bell's list, without a pop-up or a sound. The arrow sets it `For one hour`, `Until 8 in the morning` or `Until you turn it off`. It is set on this device only, and the top-bar button shows a crossed bell while it is on.
- `Playing` lists the music, podcast, radio station or Sound Mixer mix that plays, with Play / Pause; tap its name to open the app.
- On orOS USB, the panel also has the volume and brightness, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth.

On a narrow screen, when the top bar is full, the language button moves into Quick settings.

## Desktop widgets
Cards on the desktop that show your day at a glance: `Clock`, `Calendar` (a month; tap a day to open it in the Calendar), `Today` (what is late and what is due today, from the [Today](app:today) summary; tap a line to open it), `Weather` (the place of the top-bar weather), `Playing` (Play / Pause for music, podcasts, radio or the Sound Mixer) and `Sync` (its state and Sync now).

- On a computer they sit in a column at the right of the desktop; on a phone or a narrow window, in a row at the top that scrolls sideways.
- Right-click an empty spot of the desktop and choose `Widgets…` to tick the cards you want and set their order. `Settings`, under `Appearance`, has the same ticks.
- Right-click a card (long press on a touch screen) or use its `⋯` button to move it up or down or remove it. With a mouse you can also drag a card by its title.
- A computer starts with Clock, Calendar, Today and Weather; a phone starts with none. Your choice stays on this device.

### First steps
When you start orOS for the first time, a `First steps` card waits on the desktop: write your first note, add a task, choose a wallpaper or a Living land, pick an icon pack, turn on sync, meet the Assistant and, where your browser offers it, install orOS as an app. Tap a line to go there. Each line ticks itself once you have really done it, so there is nothing to tick by hand. `Hide` takes the card away; when everything is done it says so and leaves the next day. You can bring it back with `Widgets…`.

## Greeting at the start
The first time you start orOS each day, a short greeting appears over the desktop: good morning, afternoon or evening (with your name, if you set one under `Your name`), the date and, when [Today](app:today) has something, how many things are due and late. Tap that line to open Today. The greeting goes by itself after a few seconds, or when you tap anywhere, press a key or open an app. It never shows over an app or on your very first day. Turn it off in `Settings`, under `Appearance`, with `Greet me when orOS starts`; this stays on this device.

## Language
orOS speaks English and Greek. The `EL` / `EN` button in the top bar switches to the other language, and so does [[Ctrl+Alt+Shift+L]].

If an app is open when you switch, it reopens in the new language. Your data isn't touched.

## On-screen keyboard
On a touch screen without a keyboard of its own (orOS USB on a touch laptop or tablet PC, a touch PC, a kiosk) orOS has its own keyboard at the bottom of the screen. It types into the text field you are in, in the shell and in every app, and the app moves up so the field stays in sight. It never shows on phones: they have their own.

- It opens when you tap a text field with a finger or a pen. A mouse click does not open it, and while you type on a real keyboard it stays away (a real key press closes it).
- The keyboard button in the top bar shows or hides it at any time. With no text field in use, its keys go to the app as key presses, for games.
- `⇧` gives one capital letter; tap it twice for Caps Lock. `?123` and `=\<` show numbers and symbols, `abc` the letters again. `⌫` deletes (hold it to keep deleting), `↵` is Enter, the arrows move the cursor.
- The key next to `?123` (`EN`, `ΕΛ`, `DE`) goes to the next layout. In Greek, `΄` puts the accent on the next vowel (`΄` then `α` gives `ά`) and `⇧` `¨` the diaeresis (`ϊ`, `ϋ`).
- In `Settings`, under `Language & search`, `On-screen keyboard` sets when it shows (`When I tap a text field`, `Only from its button` or `Never`) and which layouts it has: English, Greek and German. These choices stay on this device.

## Light or dark theme
In `Settings`, under `Appearance`, choose `Dark` or `Light` next to `Theme`. orOS starts in dark mode.

## Skins
The coloured dots under `System colour` are skins: each one gives orOS and its apps a different accent colour. They are named after Linux systems, such as `Ubuntu`, `Fedora`, `Mint` or `Pop!_OS`; hover over a dot to see its name. The default is `Oros`, in gold.

> Every skin comes with a suggested wallpaper, marked with a ★ under `Wallpaper`. Choosing a skin also switches to its wallpaper, as long as you still have the default wallpaper (`Desert Sand`) or the one suggested for your previous skin. Once you pick another wallpaper yourself, skins leave it alone, and you can still choose any wallpaper with any skin.

## Wallpapers
Under `Wallpaper`, tap or click a picture to make it your desktop background. You can choose from `Adwaita Dusk`, `Midnight`, `Aubergine`, `Forest Night`, `Ember`, `Nordic Frost`, `Aurora`, `Desert Sand`, `Monochrome`, `Nebula`, `Nordic Aurora`, `Hex Grid`, `Obsidian Veil` and `Retro Terrazzo`. `None` uses the plain background of your theme.

### Your own wallpaper (Mine)
The first tile, `Mine`, is a wallpaper you make yourself.
1. Until you've made one, the tile shows a **+** (`Make your own in Wallpaper Generator`). Tap or click it to open [Wallpaper Generator](app:wallpaper).
2. Create a design there and press `Set as wallpaper`.
3. It becomes your `Mine` wallpaper and you can pick it again in `Settings` any time.

Each device draws your wallpaper to fit its own screen, so it stays sharp on a phone and on a big monitor.

### Icon packs
Settings › Appearance › `Icons` changes how app icons look on the desktop, in the Dock, the menu and search. `Classic` is the orOS look. `Colourful` gives every menu category its own colour, `Glass` makes see-through frosted tiles, `Thin` drops the tiles for a fine line, `Paper` puts a dark ink icon on a cream card, and `Round` uses circles in your system colour. The pack you pick is the same on all your devices. High contrast always shows `Classic`.

### Living land
`Living land` (the second tile) draws a small landscape around what is on your desktop: your shortcuts, widgets and sticky notes stand on islands, and the gaps between them become water. Add or remove a shortcut, move a sticky note or change your widgets, and the land re-forms and fades in. Its colours follow your skin and your light or dark theme.
- Under the wallpapers, `Style` picks `Islands` (mostly water), `Hills` (meadows rising to terraces, with a winding river) or `Garden` (a lawn with flowers, hedges and ponds), and `New shape` draws another land. Your choice and its shape follow you to your other devices; every device draws the land for its own screen.
- The land is still: it only changes when your desktop does, so it costs no battery while you work. With reduced motion it changes without fading.
- `Gentle motion` (off at first) adds glints on the water and a drifting cloud shadow. It stops while an app is open, with reduced motion, on a low battery (under 20 % and not charging) and on a device too slow for it.
- High contrast shows the plain desktop instead.

## Seasonal decorations
At some times of the year your desktop dresses up on its own: snow, a string of lights and a tree at Christmas, gold sparkles and a pomegranate at New Year (with fireworks from 23:55 to 00:10 on New Year's Eve), pumpkins, bats and a spider's web at Halloween.

The Greek holidays have their own:
- `Epiphany` (5-6 January): a gold cross over the sea.
- `Valentine's Day` (13-14 February): hearts floating up.
- `Carnival` (from Tsiknopempti to the Sunday before Clean Monday): confetti, coloured flags and a mask.
- `Clean Monday`: kites in the sky.
- `25 March` and `28 October` (and the day before each): blue and white flags and the Greek flag.
- `Easter` (Holy Thursday to Easter Monday): red eggs, an Easter candle, flowers and butterflies.
- `May Day` (30 April - 1 May): a flower wreath and falling petals.

Carnival, Clean Monday and Easter move every year; orOS works out their dates from the Orthodox Easter. When two events fall on the same day, one wins: 28 October over Halloween, Valentine's Day over Carnival, Easter over May Day.

### Extras you can turn on
These are off until you choose them:
- `My birthday`: balloons, flags and confetti on your birthday. Pick the day and month under the event; nothing shows until you do. It wins over any other event that day.
- `Seasons`: petals and tulips in `Spring`, the sun and waves in `Summer`, falling leaves in `Autumn`, light snow in `Winter`. A holiday always wins over its season.
- `Easter date`: `Orthodox` (the default) or `Western`, for those who keep Easter on the Western date. It moves only Easter; Carnival and Clean Monday stay Orthodox.

The decorations sit behind your desktop icons, never take a click and stop moving while an app is open.

They are on from the start. In `Settings`, under `Appearance` › `Seasonal decorations`:
- `Decorate the desktop in season` turns them all off or on again. While it is off, the options below it and the list of events are hidden and a decoration you are trying or kept stops; your choices stay and come back when you turn it on again.
- `Motion`: `Moving`, or `Still only` for the decorations without snow or anything that moves.
- `Amount`: `A little`, `Normal` or `Lots` of snowflakes and sparkles.
- `At Christmas`: a `Tree` or the Greek Christmas `Boat`.
- `Hide for today` appears while an event is on; `Show again` brings it back.
- Under `Events`, each event shows its next dates and has its own switch. The list starts with what is on now or comes next.

### Try one before its time
Press `Try it now` next to an event. Settings closes and the decoration appears on your desktop with a small bar:
1. `Keep it` keeps it until the event's period ends (for Halloween, until 31 October).
2. `Stop` takes it away, and your desktop follows the calendar again.

A kept decoration shows in `Settings` with its own `Stop` button.

If your device asks for less motion, or the battery is low, only the still pieces show. Autumn and Winter are made only of falling leaves and snow, so they then show as a still picture of them. Slow devices get fewer snowflakes on their own; a device that stays too slow shows still pieces, and changing `Motion` or `Amount` tries moving again.

## Screen pet
In `Settings`, under `Appearance`, the `Screen pet` switch turns on a tiny companion that lives on your desktop. Feed it, pet it and let it sleep. It never dies: if you forget it for a while, it just gets grumpy. You can do more with your pet in [Pet World](app:petworld).

## Weather in the top bar
A small icon with the current temperature shows in the top bar from the start. To change it, open [Weather](app:weather), then the `Cities` dialog, and look under `Taskbar`:
1. `Show the weather in the taskbar` shows or hides it on this device.
2. The pin next to a city picks the city it shows. With no city pinned, it shows your first city.

Tap or click the icon to open [Weather](app:weather); inside Weather, it takes you back to the desktop.

When you are offline, or the reading is more than 3 hours old, the icon shows a crossed-out cloud and no temperature. orOS never shows a temperature it doesn't have. Weather data comes from Open-Meteo (see [Privacy](help:t/privacy)).

## Dock
The Dock is a bar of your favourite apps along the bottom of the desktop. Add an app with the ☆ next to it in the menu. In `Settings`, under `Appearance` › `Dock`:
- `Show the Dock` turns it on or off.
- `Size` is `Small`, `Medium` or `Large`.
- `Style` changes its look: `Classic`, `Glass`, `Frosted`, `Smoke`, `Transparent`, `Solid`, `3D shelf` or `Neon`.
- `Magnify on hover` makes icons grow under the mouse (only with a mouse or trackpad).
- `Hide automatically` tucks it away until you move the mouse to the bottom edge, or tap the small handle on a touch screen.
- `Show over open apps` keeps it visible while an app is open. It is off until you turn it on, so apps get the whole screen.

## Accessibility
In `Settings` › `Accessibility`, for orOS and every app:
- `Text size` makes text and everything around it larger: `100%`, `115%`, `130%` or `150%`. It works in every app, in Settings, in Help and in the menu, like zooming the page; the top bar and the desktop keep their size (the Dock has its own `Size`). On a narrow screen orOS goes only as far as every app still fits (at least 320 pixels across), and the card says so.
- `High contrast` switches orOS and the apps to black and white with one strong colour, clear borders, underlined links and a plain desktop without a wallpaper. `Automatic` (the default) turns it on while your device asks for more contrast, for example with Windows high contrast; `On` and `Off` decide yourself. When Windows forces its own contrast colours, orOS follows them and marks chosen options with an outline.
- `Always show the focus ring` draws a thick outline around whatever has the keyboard focus when you move with the [[Tab]] key, also in apps that show only a faint one.

These three stay on this device: a phone and a large screen often need a different size.

## Your name
In Settings › Appearance › `Your name` you can type what orOS should call you. It is empty at first. With a name, [Today](app:today) and the Start page greet you with it, for example `Good evening, Maria`. The name stays on your devices: with [sync](help:t/sync) on it goes to your other devices encrypted, like your other settings. It never goes to the [Assistant](app:assistant) or anywhere else. Empty the field to remove it.

## The Start page
The Start page is a home page for the web: the time, a search field, your favourites from [Bookmarks](app:bookmarks) and a link to orOS. Open it at [useoros.online/startpage/](https://useoros.online/startpage/) and make it your browser's home page, on a phone too.
- Type words and press [[Enter]] to search with DuckDuckGo, or type an address such as `wikipedia.org` to go there.
- Start with `oros ` (for example `oros dentist`) to search your apps and data in orOS.
- Your favourites are the bookmarks with a star in Bookmarks. `All bookmarks` opens the app.
- On orOS Portable and the orOS USB, `New Tor window` opens Tor Browser for private browsing.
- With the [screen lock](help:t/privacy) on, the page shows neither your name nor your favourites.

The page only reads what orOS already keeps on this device. Nothing you type goes anywhere until you search, and then only to DuckDuckGo.

## What follows you to other devices
With [sync](help:t/sync) on, your language, theme, skin, wallpaper (including `Mine`), your name and the pinned weather city go to your other devices too.

The seasonal decoration settings go too. The Dock and its settings, the accessibility options, a decoration you are trying or kept, and `Hide for today` stay on this device.
