# Newsletter
Newsletter writes and sends e-mail newsletters to your own subscriber lists, like Mailerlite or Buttondown, but from your own e-mail account or a sending provider. Subscribers live in orOS, not on someone else's service, and nothing is tracked: there is no open or click counting.

## Getting started
1. On `Lists`, choose `Add sending account`. Pick a `Mail account` you already set up in [Mail](app:mail), or `Provider (SMTP)` for a sending provider such as Brevo, Amazon SES, Mailgun, Postmark, SendGrid or MailerSend. `Test login` checks it. MailerLite itself offers no SMTP: its sending service is MailerSend.
2. Make a `New list`: its name, the account it `Sends from`, the `From address`, and `Your name and postal address (footer)`, which the law asks every newsletter to show.
3. Add subscribers on `Subscribers`, then write your first issue with `New` on `Issues`.

## Lists and sending accounts
A list has its own look (`Classic`, `Warm`, `Dark` or `Plain`), colour, footer language and the text that says `Why they receive it (footer)`. `Check domain` looks up the SPF, DKIM and DMARC records of your From address: without them newsletters often land in spam.

`Seconds between messages` and `Messages a day (at most)` keep you under your provider's limits. A provider's password or API key stays sealed on the device where you typed it and is never synced: enter it once on each device that sends.

## Sending through a provider's API
For Brevo, Postmark, SendGrid and Mailgun, set `Connection` to `HTTP API` in the sending account and paste the provider's API key (Mailgun also needs its sending domain). Then:
- Up to 10 messages leave in one call instead of one at a time.
- Every 10 minutes, Newsletter reads the provider's hard bounces, spam complaints and unsubscribes and marks those subscribers on your lists.
- A refused address counts as bounced; a refused key pauses the issue until you fix it.

The key needs permission to send and to read bounces or suppressions. Brevo's API can't carry pictures inside a message: for issues with pictures, use Brevo's SMTP. Payloads follow each provider's public API guide, so try a test send first.

## Subscribers
`Add`, `Paste addresses`, `Import CSV`, or pick people `From Contacts` or `From CRM` (your CRM customers with an e-mail; their CRM status and tags become tags). `From the shop` brings a WooCommerce shop's customers, as existing customers. From Shopify, export the customers (Customers › Export) and use `Import CSV`: only those who accept e-mail marketing come in. Each person has a status (`Active`, `Waiting to confirm`, `Unsubscribed`, `Bounced`), tags, and the basis you have for writing to them:
- `Gave consent`: they agreed, for example on a form.
- `Existing customer`: news about similar products or services.
- `Confirms by e-mail`: they get a confirmation request and receive issues only after they confirm.

An import never brings back someone who unsubscribed. `Delete for good` removes a person and keeps only a fingerprint of the address, so it can't be imported again by mistake. `Their data (JSON)` saves everything kept about one person.

## Clean up the list
`Clean up…` keeps a list from growing with people who will never read it. Newsletter doesn't track opens, so it deletes only people who never confirmed (30 days after the confirmation request, by default) and people who left: unsubscribed, bounced or marked as spam (90 days after they left). Active subscribers are never deleted. Those who left are deleted for good, like `Delete for good`; those who never confirmed can be invited again. It runs once a day while orOS is open, on one device per list: the one that sends the list (`Run it on this device` picks another). More than 200 people, or more than a fifth of the list, at once wait for you: the dialog shows who would go, `Export them (CSV)` saves them first and `Clean up now` deletes them. Active subscribers with no sign of life for 12 months (6, 12, 24 or never; a sign of life is signing up or confirming, since opens are not counted) get one e-mail, "Do you still want it?", from the same device, at most 20 a day (or 5% of the list), oldest first. Replying with the prepared e-mail keeps them on the list, even after the time is up; whoever doesn't answer within 30 days is unsubscribed, and later deleted like anyone who left.

## Email platforms (MailerLite, Mailchimp, Brevo)
If you also keep subscribers on MailerLite, Mailchimp or Brevo, connect the account first in [Settings](app:settings) › `Connected services`. Then, on `Subscribers`, `Email platforms` links the list to a group (MailerLite), an audience (Mailchimp) or a contact list (Brevo). `Sync` reads everyone there and shows what would change before anything does; `Apply` makes the changes.
- `Bring their subscribers here`: people on the platform who aren't on this list are added, with the tag of the platform and the basis `Gave consent`.
- `Send our confirmed subscribers there`: only `Active` people go; people still waiting to confirm don't.
- Unsubscribes always travel both ways: someone who left on the platform leaves here too, and someone who left here (or was deleted for good) is unsubscribed there. Nobody who left on one side is ever brought back on the other.
- `Unlink` only removes the link: nobody is deleted here or there.
- Mailchimp only talks to servers, so it always goes through the orOS relay; the relay keeps nothing. Your keys stay in Connected services and never come into Newsletter.

## Writing an issue
Write in `Blocks` (heading, text, list, quote, picture, buttons, line, space, two columns) or in `Markdown`. `{first}` and `{name}` put in the subscriber's name; `{first|friend}` uses "friend" when there is no name. Under `Goes to` you send to everyone active, to subscribers with some tags, or to a saved segment. `Segments…` makes segments: subscribers with some tags (any or all of them), a language, or both. Pictures are shrunk and sent inside the e-mail.

Move a block by dragging its handle (with a mouse or a finger), or focus the handle and use the arrow keys. The copy icon duplicates a block, the gear icon opens its alignment, the space above and below it and its background colour (the text turns white or dark to stay readable). A line can be solid, dashed or dotted, thicker, shorter or in its own colour; a button can be filled or outlined, full width, or have up to two more buttons next to it. `Undo` and `Redo` at the top of the content (or Ctrl+Z, and Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z, outside a text box) step through your changes.

A `Header` block holds your logo (or none), a name, a line under it and up to four links. `Social icons` shows round icons for the networks you add (in their own colours, dark, or light for dark backgrounds, at three sizes); they are drawn on your device and sent inside the e-mail, so they show even when pictures from the web are blocked. A `Footer` block holds your own text and small social icons; your address and the unsubscribe link are always added below it. `Use in every new issue of this list` on a header or footer makes every new issue of the list start or end with it. `More blocks…` adds the rest: `Picture + text` side by side (the picture left or right, with an optional button), `3 columns`, a `Gallery` of up to nine pictures (two or three a row), a `Video` (pick a picture from it: a play mark is drawn on it and it opens the video's link; orOS fetches nothing from the video site), a `Table` of up to six columns and an `Event` card with day, time, place and button, which can carry a calendar file (.ics) the reader opens to add the event. On a phone the columns stack. `Save block` in a block's settings keeps it under `More blocks…` for every issue and every device. `New issue` also offers three ready layouts: weekly news, announcement and product offer.

`Products` (under `More blocks…`) brings products from your own shop into the issue: connect a WooCommerce or Shopify shop in Settings › Connected services, then choose up to nine products by searching or by category. Each becomes a card with its picture (copied into the issue like your other pictures), name, price, the old price when it is on sale, and a button to the product page; one, two or three a row. orOS only reads the shop. `Refresh prices` reads them again; before sending, the check reminds you when the prices are more than a day old.

Each list's look is set on the list card (`Edit`): theme and colour, and also the font, the heading size, the shape of the buttons, a link colour and an e-mail background. Every issue of the list uses them.

`+ Greek version` (or `+ English version`) adds a second language to the issue: subscribers who read that language get it, everyone else gets the main one. Switch between the two versions at the top of the editor.

`Save as template` keeps an issue's content; `New` then offers `Blank issue` or one of your templates.

`Preview` shows the issue on a phone and a desktop (and in each language), `Send a test` mails it to you, and `Check` lists what must be fixed before sending.

## Writing with the Assistant
Tell the [Assistant](app:assistant) what goes in, piece by piece as it reaches you: "add the interview from this link", "a short note that the fair starts on Friday, with a button to the programme". It answers with a card listing the blocks (heading, text, quote, button, list, divider, space, picture, picture and text, products; a banner is a picture with a link). `Open in Newsletter` asks which draft they go into, or starts a new one; the open draft takes them at once. Each block lands in its place: the top story first, then news, products, banners and offers, and a closing note last, all before the footer; blocks you placed yourself stay where they are. `Undo` in the message takes them out again. Pictures are copied from their web address when the site allows it; when it does not, the message says so and you pick that picture in its block. "Our latest release" comes from your own shop (Settings › Connected services): Newsletter reads the newest products itself when they are added, with their prices and pictures. Other publishers' products come from their pages, with their own links. Nothing is added before you choose, and the Assistant never sends: you send from here, as always. When you tell it the issue is ready, its card `Open the check` opens the draft's check here: what is missing, and when nothing is, the send screen, where you press Send.

## Sending
`Send…` shows how many messages will go out and how long it takes; type the number to confirm. Choose `Now`, or `Later` and a day and time to schedule it. Sending goes one message at a time while orOS is open on this device, even with Newsletter closed, and the bar on top shows progress with `Pause`. If the daily limit is reached it goes on the next day. Messages that fail are listed under `Not delivered`. orOS lets you know when an issue has gone out or paused, and [Today](app:today) shows issues being sent and scheduled.

A scheduled issue goes out from the device that scheduled it, where the passwords are. Open it to `Cancel the schedule` or `Send now`.

>! orOS has to be open: with orOS closed nothing is sent, and a scheduled issue starts the next time you open orOS on that device. A tab in the background sends more slowly.

## RSS to e-mail
On a list card, `RSS to e-mail` turns a blog or news feed into issues. Pick one of your feeds in [Reader](app:feeds) or type any feed address, choose every day, every week or every month and the time, and how many articles go in. Each issue lists the new articles with their picture, a few lines and a `Read more` button.

Choose `Make a draft for me to check` and you get a draft and a notice, or `Send it by itself` and it goes out on its own, but only if the check before sending finds no errors; otherwise it stays a draft and orOS tells you. `Make one now` builds a draft from the newest articles at once.

>! A feed runs on the device where you set it up, while orOS is open. If orOS was closed at that time, it runs the next time you open it.

## Past issues from another service
`Settings` › `Import past issues…` brings in what you sent with Buttondown, MailerLite or another service: their .zip export, or Markdown, HTML, CSV or JSON files. Import them as past issues, which show among your issues as already sent (to save as a web page or `Duplicate`) without anyone getting an e-mail, or as drafts to send again. Text, headings, links and pictures come over; scripts and tracking pixels are dropped, and pictures stay at their web address. Importing the same file twice adds nothing new.

Moving from Substack or Buttondown: both `Import CSV` and `Import past issues…` read their exports and show one preview with the subscribers and the past issues, side by side; untick what you don't want.
- Substack: `Settings` › `Exports` › `Create new export`, then download the .zip. Readers whose e-mail is disabled or whose subscription has ended stay out; a paid plan becomes a tag. Only published posts come over, with their subtitle as the preview line.
- Buttondown: `Subscribers` › `Export` for the CSV, and `Emails` › `Export` (CSV, or the .zip of Markdown files) for the archive. Only regular, premium and gifted subscribers come over; drafts and scheduled e-mails stay out.

Subscribers follow the same rules as any import (nobody who left is brought back), and `Undo` on the message right after removes everything that import added.

## Subscribe and unsubscribe by e-mail
Every issue has an unsubscribe link that opens a ready e-mail. `Subscribe link` gives a link and HTML for your site that does the same for new subscribers. Newsletter reads these e-mails from the Inbox of the list's account (`Check Inbox`, every few minutes while orOS is open, and before each send), applies them, and files them in a folder called Newsletter. Delivery-failure reports mark addresses as bounced.

## Sign-up form on your site
In `Subscribe link`, `Set up the form` makes a form for your own website: copy its HTML into any page. People type their e-mail and name, and orOS's relay keeps the sign-up, encrypted so only your Newsletter can read it, until orOS collects it, every few minutes while it is open. Each new subscriber then gets a confirmation request. You can set a `Thank-you page` on your site where people land afterwards.

With the form on, every issue also has a one-click unsubscribe link, and Gmail and other mail apps show their own Unsubscribe button.

>! Sign-ups are collected on one device, the one that set the form up. Use `Collect here` to change it. `Turn the form off` deletes what the relay holds, and the one-click links in issues already sent stop working; the e-mail way keeps working.

## Save and open an orOS file
In `Settings`, under `Data`, `orOS file (.orosnewsletter)` saves your lists, subscribers, issues (with their pictures), segments, templates, RSS feeds and sending accounts as one `.orosnewsletter` file, for example to move them to another device without sync or to keep a copy. Passwords, API keys, SMTP user names, the sign-up form's keys, the email-platform links and the sending progress stay on this device; an issue that was scheduled or going out is saved as a draft, and RSS feeds as off. The file is a plain ZIP: unzipped it holds everything as `content.json`, the pictures in `media/`, and `preview/issues.md` and one `preview/subscribers-….csv` per list for other programs. It holds your subscribers' e-mail addresses and is not encrypted, so keep it somewhere safe.

`Open orOS file…` (or `Unzipped orOS folder…`, or "Open with" orOS on the computer) shows how many records are new and how many are already here, then `Add` adds only the new ones, with their own ids: opening the same file again adds nothing. A record already here is kept as it is, one you deleted here stays deleted, and an address you deleted for good is not brought back; a file never deletes anything. Tick `Update with newer versions` to let records that were changed later in the file replace your copy. Opening a file never sends anything: no confirmation requests, no issues. A list added from a file gets new unsubscribe and confirm codes (they are never in the file): unsubscribes answering issues sent earlier are handled on the device that sent them, and the list's sign-up form is set up again here. `Undo` in the message removes what was added.

## Sync and backup
Lists, subscribers and issues sync through orOS sync, and pictures through the orOS disk. Passwords, the daily counter and when the Inbox was last read stay on each device. `Settings` has `Back up (JSON)` and `Restore from backup`, and `Export CSV` saves a list's subscribers.

## Keyboard shortcuts
- [[N]]: new issue, subscriber or list (on the tab you are on).
- [[/]]: search subscribers.
- [[Esc]]: back to the list.
- [[Ctrl+Z]]: undo the last delete.

## Names from online profiles
`Fill in names online` in Subscribers looks up the subscribers shown that have no name, using the public Gravatar profile each person set for their own e-mail (only a coded fingerprint of the address is sent). You see every name found, tick the right ones and tap `Apply selected`; `Undo` takes them away again.

## Tips
> Send a test to your own phone and a Gmail address before each big send.

> Ask your provider for SPF and DKIM before your first send: it matters more than the subject line.

## Limits
- Sending happens only while orOS is open, and the daily limit is counted per device.
- Up to 5,000 subscribers in all and 20 lists.
- Up to 20 RSS feeds and 500 issues.
- No open or click statistics.
- The relay holds at most 2,000 sign-ups and unsubscribes per list waiting to be collected.
- Each call to a provider's API sends up to 10 messages (about 1 MB); bounces older than 7 days before the first read are not fetched.
