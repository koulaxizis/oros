# Media Shelf
Media Shelf is your wishlist and tracker for books, films, series, music, podcasts and games. Keep what you want to try, follow your progress, rate what you finish and see your year in numbers.

## Getting started
Tap or click `Add` (or `Add your first title`). Choose the `Type`, then fill in the `Title` and, if you like, the creator (`Author`, `Director`, `Artist`…), `Year`, size (pages, episodes, hours…), `Tags (comma separated)`, `Where I heard of it`, a `Release date (optional)` and a `Cover colour`. Books also have a `Format`: `Print`, `E-book` or `Audiobook`; games a `Platform`. Pick the `Status` and press `Save`. Each title gets a generated cover with its initials.

## Tabs, search and sort
- `Now`: what you're reading, watching, listening to or playing.
- `Wishlist`: what you want to get to. Open a title and tap `High priority` to flag it; flagged titles come first when you sort by `Recent`.
- `History`: finished and dropped titles.
- `Statistics`: your year in numbers.

The chips filter by type. `Search title, creator, tag…` also looks in platform and source, and ignores accents. Sort by `Recent`, `Title`, `Creator`, `Rating` or `Year`.

## Progress
Tap a title to open it. Start it with a button such as `Start reading` or `Start playing`. Then log where you are: the page for books, minutes for audiobooks, episodes for series and podcasts, hours for games. Press `Update`, or use `+1 episode` or `+1 hour`.

You'll see a progress bar and percentage, and, once there's enough to go on, how long you'll take at your pace. When you reach the end, the app offers `Mark done`. You can also `Drop` a title, put it `Back to wishlist`, `Resume` it, or start it again (`Read again`, `Watch again`…). The history inside a title lists each start, progress and finish; you can remove an entry.

Books you add from [Library](app:library) with `Add to Media Shelf` are linked: their pages follow how far you've read there each time you open Media Shelf, finishing the book in Library marks it done here, and `Read in Library` opens it at your place.

## Rating and review
Rate with five stars in half steps: tap the left or right half of a star. Mark a `Favourite` with the heart and write a `Review / notes`.

## Statistics and goals
Pick a year with the arrows. `Statistics` shows what you finished per type, `Pages read` (print and e-books), `Average rating`, a chart `By month`, `Top creators` and `Top tags`. With `Set goals` you set a target per type for the year, and the app tells you if you're ahead of or behind schedule. Under each goal you also see how many are left and how many days remain, the pace you need (about so many a week and a month, and for books about so many pages a day, based on the length of the books you read), and where you'll end up at your current pace.

`Reading in` (the year) adds up the pages you logged in your books: `Pages read`, `Pages a day`, `Days you read`, `Days in a row`, your `Best day` and a small chart of `The last 30 days`. It counts what you log with `Update`, so log your page now and then.

When you have a book goal for this year, it also shows at the top of `Now` (`Reading goal`… with a bar); tap it to open `Statistics`.

## Find details online and the Calendar
For books and albums, `Find details online` in the form looks up the title you typed (and the creator, if any) on Open Library (books) or MusicBrainz (music). It runs only when you tap it, and the first time it tells you what is sent: just that text. Pick a match to fill in the title, creator, year, pages or tracks and, for albums, the release date; check it and press `Save`.

Films and series are looked up on Wikidata, with no account and no key. Pick a match to fill in the title (in your language when Wikidata has it), the director or creator, the year, the runtime or the number of episodes, and a cover picture from Wikimedia Commons when there is one. The details are CC0; every picture keeps its own free licence, and the title's page shows who made it, the licence and a link to its Commons page (`Cover:` … · `Wikimedia Commons`). `Remove cover` in the form goes back to the generated cover.

If you have a free TMDB account, you can also search TMDB, which has more posters: `More` → `Film and series details (TMDB key)`, paste your own API key (v3) or read access token and press `Save`. A `Search on` switch (`Wikidata` / `TMDB`) then appears above the results. The key stays on this device: it doesn't sync and is sent only to TMDB, only when you search. Titles filled in from TMDB show "This product uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB."

Podcasts and games are filled in by hand.

In [Calendar](app:calendar), the `Media Shelf` chip shows the day you finished each title (rewatches and rereads too) and the release date of titles on your wishlist. Tap a row to open that title here.

## Reading reminder
`More` → `Reading reminder` turns on one gentle nudge a day, from the hour you pick (20:00 to start with). It comes only when something is in `Now` and you haven't logged anything that day, and it names the title you were on with your progress, such as "Dune · page 212 of 604". Tap it to open that title. It's off until you turn it on, it's set per device, and it arrives while orOS is open; quiet hours and the `Media Shelf` switch in the notification settings still apply.

## Import from another app
Bring your existing lists in with `More` → `Import from another app`. Pick the export file; you can pick several at once, and a ZIP works as it is.
- **Goodreads:** on the Goodreads website, My Books → Import and export → Export library; pick the CSV.
- **Letterboxd:** on the Letterboxd website, Settings → Data → Export your data; pick the ZIP, or its CSV files (`watchlist`, `watched`, `ratings`, `diary`, `reviews`).
- **StoryGraph:** on the StoryGraph website, your profile menu → Manage account → Export StoryGraph library; pick the CSV.
- **IMDb:** on IMDb, open Your ratings or your Watchlist, then the ⋯ menu → Export; pick `ratings.csv` and / or `watchlist.csv`.
- **Trakt:** on the Trakt website, Settings → Data → Export your data; pick the ZIP or its JSON files (`watched-movies`, `watched-shows`, `ratings-…`, `watchlist-…`, `history-…`).
- **TV Time:** TV Time has no export button; ask for your data (a GDPR request from the app's settings or its support) and pick the CSV files you get, or their ZIP.
- **MyAnimeList:** on MyAnimeList, Profile → Export (or Import / Export) → Export My List, for the anime list and the manga list; pick the `.xml` or the `.xml.gz` file.
- **Discogs:** on Discogs, your Collection (or Wantlist) → Export; pick the CSV it emails or downloads.
- **BoardGameGeek:** on BoardGameGeek, your Collection → Export (the CSV download at the bottom of the collection page); pick the CSV.

Before anything is added, a preview shows where the titles come from, how many were found per type and status, the span of the dates, what is left out and how many are already on your shelf. Titles already there are skipped (same title and creator, for films and series also same title and year, or imported before), so importing the same file twice adds nothing, and nothing of yours is changed. Press the import button to add the rest, or `Cancel`. Right after an import, `Undo` takes it all back.

What comes in:
- Goodreads shelves become the status (`read`, `currently-reading`, `to-read`; a shelf such as `did-not-finish` becomes dropped), with your rating, review, pages, format, the date you finished and your other shelves as tags. From Letterboxd you get the watchlist, every logged watch with its date (rewatches count), your rating, review and tags.
- StoryGraph: read → Read, currently reading → Reading, to read → Want, did not finish → Dropped, with the dates of each read, your stars (quarter stars round to half stars), format, review and tags.
- IMDb: what you rated becomes Watched on the day you rated it, your 1–10 as half stars (8 = four stars); the watchlist becomes Want. Films, series, video games and podcasts come in; single episodes are left out.
- Trakt: watched films become Watched; a show with watched episodes is in progress with the number seen (Watched when every aired episode is seen); ratings become stars; the watchlist becomes Want. A film rated on IMDb and Trakt is one title.
- TV Time: followed shows become Want, shows with seen episodes are in progress with the number seen, watched films become Watched.
- MyAnimeList: Completed → done, Watching / Reading / On-Hold → in progress, Plan to Watch / Read → Want, Dropped → dropped. Anime films become films, other anime series (with episodes), manga books; they get the tag `anime` or `manga` and your MAL tags.
- Discogs: your collection becomes Music, listened on the day it joined the collection, with your rating and the format (Vinyl, CD…) as a tag; a wantlist becomes Want.
- BoardGameGeek: games you played or rated become finished games with the platform `Board game`; owned, wishlist and want-to-play games become Want.

## Import from Google Books
`More` › `Import from Google Books` copies the books on your Google Books shelves once. Sign in to Google in the small window; orOS reads your shelves and gives the access back to Google right away: nothing is kept, and nothing changes at Google. Google Books has no read-only permission, so Google asks to "manage" your books; orOS only reads them. The same preview as the Goodreads import shows what comes in and what is already on your shelf; `Import` saves it, with `Undo`.

`Have read` becomes Read, `Reading now` Reading and `To read` Want; `Favorites` gives the ♥, `My eBooks` marks the format as e-book, and your own shelves become tags. Each book comes with its title, authors, year and page count; covers and ISBNs are not kept. A book already on your shelf (same title and author) is skipped, so importing again adds only what is missing. Google must have this enabled for orOS; if it answers 403, the service is not switched on yet. To use your own Google app id, set it in [Contacts](app:contacts) › `From an account` › `Use my own app id`.

## Sync and backup
Your titles, progress, ratings, reviews and goals sync between your devices through [orOS sync](help:t/sync). Covers and their credits sync with the title. The open tab, type filter, sort and your TMDB key stay on each device.

In the `More` menu, `Export (JSON)` saves your shelf to a file and `Import (JSON)` merges a file in, without replacing anything.

## orOS file (.orosshelf)
In the `More` menu, `orOS file (.orosshelf)` saves every title (books, films, series, music, podcasts, games), its history (starts, progress, finishes, drops) and your yearly goals as one `.orosshelf` file, for example to move them to another device without sync or to keep a copy. `Open orOS file…` (or opening the file from Files with "Open with") adds only the titles, history entries and goals this device does not have yet: what is already here, or was deleted here, stays as it is, unless you tick "Update with newer versions". A window shows the counts first, and `Undo` takes the added records back. `Unzipped orOS folder…` opens the same file after it has been unzipped. Your tab, filter and sort, the reading reminder and your TMDB key stay on each device. The file is a plain ZIP: unzipped it holds the shelf as `content.json` plus `preview/shelf.md` and `preview/shelf.csv` for other programs. It is not encrypted, so keep it somewhere safe.

## Keyboard shortcuts
- [[/]] jumps to the search box; [[Esc]] there clears it.
- [[N]] adds a new title.
- On the stars: arrow keys change the rating by half a star, [[1]] to [[5]] set whole stars, [[0]] clears it.

## Tips
- Use tags such as `summer` or `book club` and find them later with search.
- Fill in `Where I heard of it` to remember who recommended a title; search finds it too.

## Limits
- The reading reminder needs orOS open; there is no push server.
- Cover pictures come only for films and series you look up online (Wikimedia Commons or TMDB) and load from those sites when shown; offline, or for books, music, podcasts and games, you see the generated cover.
- The online lookup has no source for podcasts or games.
- TMDB works only with your own key; orOS has none to share.
- Pages per day count the pages you log in books, plus the rest of a book when you mark it done; audiobooks don't add to them.
