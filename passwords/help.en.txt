# Passwords
Passwords keeps your logins in an encrypted vault, shows two-factor codes, and makes random passwords, PINs and passphrases. It can also check how strong a password is. The vault is locked with a master password that only you know.

## Getting started
The first tab, `Vault`, asks you to create your vault: type a master password of at least 12 characters twice and press `Create vault`. A passphrase of five or six random words is a good choice.

Next you see your recovery key. Write it down or press `Save as a file`, keep it away from this device, tick `I saved my recovery key` and close the window. It is the only way back in if you forget the master password, and it is shown only once.

The other tabs, `Password`, `Passphrase` and `Check`, work without a vault.

## The vault
Press `New` to add an entry: a title, a username, a password, websites (one per line), a two-factor secret, notes, tags and `More fields` of your own. Tick `Hidden` on a field to hide it like a password. `Favourite` puts the entry at the top.

Next to the password, `Generate` fills in a `Password` or a `Passphrase` made with the options of the Password and Passphrase tabs. On the Password and Passphrase tabs, `Save to vault` starts a new entry with the shown result.

Press an entry to see it. Passwords and hidden fields show dots until you press `Show`. `Copy` copies a value, and `Open` opens a website in a new tab (only `http` and `https` addresses). `Edit` changes the entry, and a password you replace goes to its `Password history` (the last five). `Delete` removes it on every device; `Undo` in the message brings it back.

Search finds words in titles, usernames, websites, tags, notes and fields that are not hidden. The buttons under the search show `Favourites`, `2FA codes`, `Weak` passwords and passwords `Reused` in more than one entry, and the tag list shows one tag.

## Cards, identities, addresses and notes
`New` asks what you want to keep: a `Login`, a `Payment card`, an `Identity`, an `Address`, a `2FA code` or a `Secure note`. Each has its own form, and the list shows an icon and a short line for each one. `All types` above the list shows one type only.
- Payment card: cardholder name, card number, expiry date (MM/YY), security code (CVV), PIN, bank and the bank's website. The number is checked (check digit) and its network is shown (Visa, Mastercard and others). The list shows only its last four digits, and `Expires soon` or `Expired` near its expiry date. `Copy in order` copies the number, then the expiry, then the code, then the name, one per press, for a payment form.
- Identity: title, name, date of birth, email, phone, company, ID card, passport and driving licence with their expiry dates, tax number, social security number and insurance number. Documents that expire soon are marked. `Copy name` copies the whole name.
- Address: recipient, street, floor, city, postal code, region, country and phone. `Copy address` copies it as lines.
- Secure note: text that shows dots until you press `Show`, for Wi-Fi passwords, recovery codes or a lock combination.

Card numbers, codes, PINs and document numbers are hidden like passwords and leave the clipboard after 30 seconds. None of them ever appears in search results, Today, the Assistant or notifications.

A login made from a card or an identity of another app (fields such as `Number` and `Security code`, or `First name` and `Passport number`) shows `Convert`: it turns the entry into a card or an identity, with `Undo`. An identity with an address gives an identity and an address.

## Two-factor codes
Put the secret a website gives you for an authenticator app into `Two-factor secret or otpauth:// link`: the text code, or the whole otpauth:// link. `Scan QR` reads the QR code with the camera, or from a picture with `Choose a picture`. The entry then shows the current code and the seconds it has left; `Copy` copies it.

A `2FA code` is an account whose two-factor code you keep here without its password, as in an authenticator app: the service, the account and the secret. Scanning the service's QR code fills in the service and the account. Codes that you import from Aegis, 2FAS or Google Authenticator and that match no entry become 2FA codes.

`Codes` at the top shows every two-factor code of the vault in one list, with the seconds each one has left. Type in the search to find one, and tap a code to copy it. In its last 5 seconds a code also shows the next one, and a tap copies the next one, so it is still valid when you paste it. `›` opens the entry.

`QR code` next to a code shows the account as a QR code, for moving it to another authenticator app. Anyone who sees or photographs it can make your codes, so close it once the other app has read it.

Your codes and your passwords are in the same vault: one master password opens both. That is simpler, but a separate authenticator app keeps the second step apart; `Export` can copy your codes to Aegis or 2FAS.

Codes from a counter (HOTP) are not supported.

## Import and export
`⋯` then `Import` brings entries and two-factor codes from another app. Press `Choose file`: the app is recognised by itself. Entries that are already in the vault (same title, username, password and first website) are skipped, and you see what was found and how many are new before you import. `Undo` in the message after the import takes it all back.
- Bitwarden: export as `.json`, or as `.json (Encrypted)` with the type `Password protected`: then type that password. The `Account restricted` kind opens only in Bitwarden. Folders become tags; cards, identities and secure notes keep their type, and hidden fields stay hidden.
- KeePass / KeePassXC (`.kdbx`): type the file's password and, if it has one, choose its key file. Groups become tags. Attachments and the recycle bin are left out. KeePass 2 can also export as `KeePass XML (2.x)`, which is not encrypted.
- 1Password: File › Export, as `.1pux` (or CSV). Vaults and tags become tags; sections become fields.
- Proton Pass: Settings › Export, without encryption (a ZIP or CSV). Encrypted (PGP) exports can't be read. Vaults become tags; cards, aliases and identities come too.
- LastPass: Advanced Options › Export, a CSV. Secure notes become entries with notes, folders become tags.
- Dashlane: Settings › Export data, as CSV (a ZIP; `credentials.csv` alone works too). The encrypted `.dash` file can't be read.
- NordPass: Settings › Export items, a CSV. Enpass: File › Export, as `.json`. Apple Passwords (Mac): File › Export All Passwords, a CSV with the two-factor codes.
- Keeper: Settings › Export, as `.json` or `.csv`. Folders become tags; custom fields become fields.
- RoboForm: export as a CSV. Folders become tags.
- Zoho Vault: export your secrets as a CSV (not the encrypted kind). Folders and tags become tags.
- Chrome, Edge, Firefox, KeePassXC: their CSV export.

Two-factor apps: each code goes to the one entry with the same site and account that has no code yet; otherwise it becomes an entry of its own. A code whose secret is already in the vault is skipped. Only time-based codes (TOTP) come; counter-based (HOTP) and Steam codes are left out.
- Aegis: Settings › Import & Export › Export. If the backup is encrypted, type the Aegis password.
- 2FAS: Settings › 2FAS Backup › Export to file (`.2fas`). If it has a password, type it.
- Google Authenticator: Transfer accounts › Export accounts shows one or more QR codes. Under `Google Authenticator` choose a screenshot of each, scan it with `Use the camera`, or paste its `otpauth-migration://` link and press `Read`. Add them one after another, then import.

Delete an export that is not encrypted, and any picture of those QR codes, as soon as you have imported it. A file with a password is opened on your device only; the password is not kept. Nothing leaves your device while importing.

`⋯` then `Export` saves every entry with your master password as a check.
- `KeePass (.kdbx 4, encrypted)`: the file opens in KeePass and KeePassXC with the password you choose for it.
- `Bitwarden (.json, NOT encrypted)`: anyone who gets this file can read every password. You have to tick that you understand.
- `Aegis (.json, encrypted)`: only the two-factor codes, in a file Aegis imports with the password you choose for it (Aegis: Settings › Import & Export › Import, `Aegis`).
- `2FAS (.2fas, NOT encrypted)`: only the two-factor codes, for 2FAS (Settings › 2FAS Backup › Import). Anyone who gets the file can make your codes, so import it right away and delete it.

## Fill on websites (browser add-on)
- The orOS add-on for Chrome, Edge, Brave and Firefox fills logins, 2FA codes, cards, identities and addresses on websites. Get it with the install steps at [useoros.online/download](https://useoros.online/download/#addon).
- Open orOS in a tab, press the orOS icon in the toolbar (or Ctrl+Shift+L) and press `Allow access to orOS` once: the add-on keeps a copy of your vault, encrypted as in orOS. Unlock it with your master password; it locks again after 15 minutes without use (change it in the add-on's options).
- On a login page the add-on lists the logins saved for that site; choose one and it fills the name, password and the current 2FA code. Cards, identities and addresses can be filled on any site. When a card number sits in a payment frame from another site (Stripe, your bank), the add-on names that site and fills it there only after you press `Fill in the payment frame` and the browser allows it.
- It fills only for the exact site or its sub-domains, never an http page for an https login, and never a card PIN, ID numbers or notes.
- Saving a new login: after you type a name and password on a site (sign up, sign in, change of password), press `Save the login from this page` in the add-on and confirm. orOS opens with the login in the Passwords editor, as a new entry or as a new password for the entry of the same site and user name (the old password goes to the history). It is stored only when you press `Save` there.

## Autofill on your Android phone
- In the orOS app for Android, `Menu` › `Autofill on this phone` fills logins, 2FA codes, cards and addresses in other apps and in browsers that support Android autofill (Chrome 135 and newer with "Autofill using another service", Firefox, Brave, Samsung Internet).
- Turn it on, then press `Open Android settings` and choose orOS as the autofill service. orOS gives the app a copy sealed with a key that only this phone has; it is renewed each time you unlock or change Passwords on the phone.
- In a form, tap an orOS suggestion and confirm with your fingerprint, face or screen lock; only that entry is filled. In a browser, logins are offered for the same site or its sub-domains only.
- Apps: a login is offered in an app when one of its sites is `androidapp://` plus the app's package name. Otherwise choose `Choose a login from orOS` once and confirm; the phone remembers it.
- A new fingerprint or face on the phone turns autofill off for safety: open Passwords and turn it on again. Turning it off removes the phone's copy and keys.
- Saving: when autofill is on, Android offers `Save to orOS Passwords?` after you sign in or sign up somewhere new. A yes opens orOS with the login in the Passwords editor (unlock first if needed); it is stored only when you press `Save` there. Until then the phone keeps it sealed for at most a day.

## Locking and the clipboard
The vault locks itself after 5 minutes without use and as soon as you leave the app or switch to another tab. Change both in `⋯`, `Lock settings`. The padlock button locks it at once.

Copied passwords, hidden fields and codes are cleared from the clipboard after 30 seconds. If you are in another app at that moment, it happens when you come back.

## Master password and recovery key
`⋯` then `Change master password` asks for the current one and the new one. `New recovery key` makes a new key; the old one stops working.

Forgot the master password? On the lock screen press `Forgot it? Use the recovery key`, type the key and choose a new master password.

`Delete vault` removes the vault and every password in it on all your devices.

## Passwords and PINs
On the `Password` tab choose `Characters` or `PIN`.
- `Characters`: set the `Length` (4 to 128) and tick `Lowercase (a–z)`, `Uppercase (A–Z)`, `Digits (0–9)` and `Symbols`. `Avoid look-alikes (0 O o 1 l I)` leaves out characters that are easy to confuse.
- If a site accepts only a few symbols, type them in `Only these symbols`. Leave it empty for all symbols.
- `PIN`: only digits, with 4 to 12 `Digits`.

Under each result a meter shows its strength, from `Very weak` to `Very strong`, its entropy in bits and roughly how long a fast offline attack would need. Press `New` for another one and `Copy` to copy it.

## Passphrases
A passphrase is a few random words from the EFF Diceware lists: easy to remember, hard to guess.
- `Words`: 3 to 12.
- `Word list`: `EFF large (7,776)` or `EFF short (1,296)`.
- `Separator`: space, dash, dot or underscore.
- `Capitalize each word` and `Add one digit or symbol` for sites that demand them.

### Real dice
Choose `Real dice` instead of `This device` if you'd rather trust your own dice. Roll five dice for the large list (four for the short one), type the numbers in order and press `Add word`. `Remove last` and `Start over` fix mistakes.

## Check a password
On the `Check` tab type or paste a password. You see its strength, the `Time to crack (on average)` for an online attack and for slow and fast offline attacks, and `What makes it weaker`: common passwords, dictionary words, sequences, repeats, keyboard rows, dates and more. `Show` reveals what you typed. The box is wiped when you leave the tab or close the app.

## Sync and backup
With [orOS sync](help:t/sync) the vault reaches your other devices, where you open it with the same master password. Only the encrypted vault is synced: it is encrypted on this device with a key made from your master password (Argon2id) before anything is stored, and then the whole orOS sync copy is encrypted once more with your sync passphrase. Changes made on two devices at the same time are joined entry by entry when a device opens the vault.

Older cloud backups of your data still open with the master password you had then. Generated and checked passwords are never saved. See [Privacy](help:t/privacy).

## Keyboard shortcuts
- [[Enter]] or [[Space]] makes a new password or passphrase (not in a text box).
- [[Ctrl+C]] with nothing selected copies the shown result.
- [[←]] and [[→]] move between tabs and between option buttons.
- [[Enter]] in the dice box adds the word; in the master password box it unlocks.

## Tips
- A passphrase of six random words is a strong master password that you can remember.
- Look at `Weak` and `Reused` now and then, and change those passwords first.
- Export a KeePass file now and then and keep it somewhere safe, with your recovery key apart from it.

## Limits
- Nobody can reset your master password. Without it and without the recovery key, the vault cannot be opened.
- The browser add-on fills logins only in the page itself, not in a login box embedded from another site, and saves a new login only when you press its button.
- Attachments are not kept: a KeePass import leaves them out.
- KeePass files encrypted with Twofish cannot be imported; switch them to AES or ChaCha20 in KeePass first.
- While the vault is open, anything that controls this browser can read it. Lock it on shared computers.
- Clearing the clipboard is best effort; some browsers refuse it.
- The checker can't know personal facts such as names or birthdays unless they're in its lists.
