# Publishing
Publishing is a desk for a small publisher: every book you publish with its formats, ISBNs, prices, sizes, back-cover text and cover, your ISBN blocks with the next free number, and barcodes for the back cover. Musicians and labels keep their singles, EPs and albums here too: tracks with ISRCs, editions with UPC/EAN, and their own code prefixes.

## Getting started
1. Press `New title`, write the title and, if you like, the author.
2. Under `Formats` add each edition (paperback, e-book, audiobook…). Every format has its own ISBN and price.
3. Fill in the back-cover text, the people who worked on the book and the release date. The line at the top says what is `Still missing` before the release.

## Titles
The `Titles` tab lists your books with their cover, people, ISBNs and status (`Idea`, `In production`, `Released`, `Out of print`). Filter by status, search by title, person or ISBN, and sort by release date, title or last edit.

Tap a title to edit it. Every change is saved as you type. `Delete` removes a title; `Undo` brings it back right after.

### People
A title can have authors, translators, illustrators, editors, a cover designer, a narrator or others. Use `+ Person` and pick the role.

### Formats
Each format keeps its ISBN, price, pages, size (width × height in mm), spine, weight, binding and paper; an audiobook keeps its length in minutes. The price is in the currency of the settings (⚙), where you also type the `Publisher name` for covers and press texts.

The ISBN field checks the number as you type: a wrong check digit, a missing digit, or a number another title already uses. `Next free` fills in the next unused number of your blocks.

### Barcode
`Barcode` on a format with an ISBN shows the EAN-13 for the back cover, with "ISBN …" above the bars. `Download SVG` gives a vector at the standard size (about 37 × 29 mm), `Download PNG` a sharp picture. Leave a white margin around it when you place it.

### Cover
`Add a cover picture` keeps a copy of the front cover (up to 1600 px, without location or camera data) in [Files](app:files), folder Publishing/Covers. It reaches your other devices with Vault Drive. The print file of the cover itself is made in [Layout](app:layout).

### Release plan
With a release date set, `Make the plan` dates every step back from it: manuscript in, editing, proofs 1, 2 and 3, cover ready, ISBN and barcode, to the printer, and the presentation two weeks after the release. Tick a step when it is done, change any date, remove what you don't need, or add your own step with `+ Step`. The line above the list says what is late and what comes next.

- `Re-date from the release date` moves the open steps when the release date changes; done steps keep their dates.
- The release date and every open step show in [Calendar](app:calendar) under the `Publishing` chip.
- `Send to To-Do` adds the open steps to a new list named after the book, each with its date.
- When you make a new title with a release date, tick `Make the release plan from the release date` and the plan is there from the start. The Assistant can set it up too: "new book Night Sea by Ann Writer, out on 15 March".

### Review copies and reviews
In a title, `Review copies and reviews` keeps who got the book: name, outlet, e-mail and the day it went out (names you used before are offered again, with their outlet and address). Three weeks later with no review the copy is marked `follow up`; `Follow up by e-mail` opens Mail with a short, kind note and keeps the day. When a review comes, set the status to `Reviewed` and add its link and a quote: the two newest quotes go into the Newsletter blocks of the title, and `Copy the quotes` puts them all on the clipboard for the shop page or a post.

### Send to other apps
Under `Send to other apps` pick a format (when the title has more than one), then:
- `Draft in my shop` creates a draft product in your WooCommerce shop, connected with your own keys in [Settings](app:settings) › Connected services: name, SKU (the ISBN), price, back-cover text, weight and size in the shop's own units. If the shop already has a product with that ISBN, orOS links it and creates nothing. You add pictures and categories and publish it in the shop; `Open in the shop` takes you there.
- `Item in Stock` opens a new product in [Stock](app:stock) with the name, the ISBN as SKU and barcode, and the price. If Stock already has it, its sheet opens instead.
- `Block in Newsletter` adds the title, subtitle, author and back-cover text to a [Newsletter](app:newsletter) draft you pick, with a button to the book when it is in your shop.
- `Press release in Writer` opens a new [Writer](app:writer) document with a press release built from the title, the texts, the formats and your publisher name. Read it and edit it before you send it.
- `Release kit in Atelier` makes five designs in [Atelier](app:atelier), in a folder named after the book: an Instagram post, a story, a site banner, a bookmark and an A3 poster, each with the cover, title, author, release line, price and your publisher name (and the newest review quote on the bookmark and the poster). Atelier asks first; every design stays editable.

Nothing is saved in the other app until you save it there.

## Contracts
On a title, `Contracts` shows each contract that [CRM](app:crm) keeps for it, one line per format: until when it runs, how many days are left, or that it has ended with no decision yet. The list of titles shows the one that most needs a look as a badge. When a format's contract has ended but that format is still linked to a shop product, a note asks you to check whether it should still be sold (nothing is changed in the shop). `Renew`, `Put off`, `Email`, `End` and `More…` open that contract's actions in CRM, which asks before it changes anything. Link a contract to its title in CRM (the contract's `Publishing title`); a title with the same name is found by itself.

## Royalties
On a title, the `Royalties` section says who is paid for it: the name, the percentage, `On` the list price or on what came in (net), which formats (or all) and the advance paid on signing. Several terms per title are fine (the paperback at 10 % of the list price, the e-book at 25 % of net, the translator's 2 %).

The `Royalties` tab lists everyone with terms. `New statement…` takes a period (last half-year, last year, last quarter or your own dates) and fills in the units:
- they come from Stock: every sale recorded there (the till, shop orders, invoices, consignment settlements) of a product whose SKU or barcode is the format's ISBN, less customer returns; voided movements don't count;
- "net" is what came in without VAT; a consignment settlement counts after the shop's discount;
- sales Stock never saw (e-books, a book fair) are typed in, and every figure can be corrected before you issue.

The advance is recouped first: what earlier statements earned on the same terms is taken off it, and only the rest is payable. An issued statement is a record that doesn't change when terms or sales do. From it: `Print or PDF`, `E-mail` (Mail opens with the text), `Add to Budget` (an expense you confirm there), `Mark paid`, or `Void` (it stays in the list, crossed out, and no longer counts towards the advance).

## Cost & result
What each book cost and what it brought in, read from your WooCommerce shop: Production Ledger (a Noxpress plugin) keeps the sales and the costs, Revenue Splitter who gets a share. Connect the shop once in [Settings](app:settings) › Connected services (a read key is enough); nothing is written to the shop and nothing of these figures is kept in orOS: each reading shows the shop as it is now. All amounts are without VAT.

A title counts once one of its formats is in the shop: sent from `Send to other apps` › `Draft in my shop`, or a shop product with the same ISBN as its SKU (hyphens or not).

On a title, the `Cost & result` section shows:
- income: shop sales and bookshop (trade) sales with their copies, sales outside the shop (a fair, a presentation) and the shipping customers paid;
- costs: each category (printing, editing, design…) with its invoices (date, supplier, invoice number, note), then the book's share of each month's courier invoices, consumables and payment fees (by the orders it was in), and the creators' shares;
- the result, the result since the ledger began and, while it is negative, how many more copies cover it;
- VAT on sales and on costs and the difference owed to the tax office: VAT is neither income nor cost, so the result is without it.

Creators' shares are what Revenue Splitter pays; shares to the publisher are yours, not a cost (the publisher name in Settings, and `Other names you go by` for other spellings). When Revenue Splitter pays no one for a book and the ledger has no «Creators» cost for it, the royalties are worked out from the title's own terms (`Royalties` section) and the copies sold, the advance counting in full until it is earned.

The `Cost & result` tab lists every title with its copies, income, costs and result, worst first, then the shop's other products and the general expenses no book carries: the last line is the shop's whole result, the same as the ledger's report. Choose the period (since the ledger began, this year, last year); `Read again` reads the shop anew.

The full picture needs Production Ledger 1.1 or later on the shop; with 1.0 only the book's own costs and sales show.

## Submissions
Manuscripts people send you. `+ Submission` adds one by hand; in [Mail](app:mail), open the message and choose `Other apps` › `Manuscript submission in Publishing` to file it with the sender, the subject and the text filled in.

Each has a status (`New`, `Being read`, `Shortlisted`, `Accepted`, `Declined`). The answers open Mail with a ready text: `Thank for it`, `Decline` or `Accept`; the day you answered is kept. A submission still new or being read with no answer after 90 days is flagged (change the days in Settings › `Answers and contract…`). `Make it a title` starts a title with the author and your notes.

### Answers and contract
In Settings › `Answers and contract…` write your own texts; {name}, {title}, {imprint} and {days} are filled in. On a title, `Contract in Writer` (in each set of royalty terms) opens a contract draft in [Writer](app:writer) with the names, formats, percentage and advance filled in, to finish and save as PDF. The built-in draft is a starting point, not legal advice.

## Posts
Plan your social posts once: the text, a picture and the day (a time if you like). `+ Post` in the `Posts` tab adds one; on a title, `Send to other apps` › `Plan a post…` starts one with the title, its people, the release line and a quote, and its cover as the picture. Pick the networks it is for: the count under the text tells you when it is over a network's limit.

On the day the post is listed as due here, in the [Calendar](app:calendar) and in [Today](app:today). Open it and post it from each network's own page: `Copy and open` copies the text and opens Mastodon, Bluesky, X, Threads or LinkedIn with the text already in it. Facebook and Instagram have no such page, so they open their home page and you paste the text. `Copy the picture` and `Save the picture` hand you the picture. Nothing is sent from orOS and no network account is needed here; for Mastodon write your server in Settings (for example mastodon.social). Then `Mark posted`.

## Music releases
In `New title`, pick `Type` › `Music: Single`, `EP`, `Album` or `Compilation`. A music release has the same status, release date, people, texts and cover as a book (the main person is the `Artist`), and instead of formats:

- `Release details`: the type, your label, the genre, and the ℗ and © lines that stores show (who owns the recording, who owns the artwork and texts).
- `Tracks`: `+ Tracks` takes one track or the whole track list at once, one per line; a length at the end (3:45) is read too. Tap a track to open it: its title, version (Radio edit, Remix…), the artist when it is not the release's, its ISRC, its length, the language of the lyrics and whether the lyrics are explicit. ↑ and ↓ change the order.
- `Editions`: digital, CD, vinyl, cassette or other, each with its UPC or EAN, catalogue number, price and distributor. `Barcode` gives the code's bars for the CD or sleeve.

Inside each track:

- `Credits`: who sang, played, wrote, produced, mixed or mastered it, with the instrument for a musician.
- `Songwriting shares`: who wrote the song (music and lyrics) and their percent, with their collecting society (free text; the list only suggests names) and IPI number when they have one.
- `Master ownership`: who owns the recording and their percent.

Each list shows what its shares add up to, in red when it is not 100%. A name that matches a CRM partner is linked to them. `Split sheet in Writer` writes a split sheet for the track (release, ISRC, shares, credits and a line to sign for each person) as a new Writer document, to print or send for signing. `Same credits and splits on every track` copies the open track's three lists to the others (Undo puts theirs back).

The ISRC and UPC/EAN fields check the code as you type and warn when another track or edition already has it. If your distributor gives you the codes, type them in. If you have your own prefixes (Codes tab), `Next free` fills in the next unused one. `Still missing` lists what a release still needs: artist, tracks, ISRCs, lengths, shares that make 100%, an edition, UPC/EAN, release date and cover.

A release has its own `Release plan`, dated back from the release day: master ready (6 weeks before), artwork 3000 × 3000 px, metadata and codes, upload to the distributor (4 weeks before; most stores want 3 to 4 weeks), editorial pitch (3 weeks before; Spotify for Artists needs at least 7 days), pre-save link and posts (2 weeks before), and first-week posts. Move, rename, add or remove steps as in a book plan; the steps also show in Calendar, Today and To-Do.

## Codes
Your national ISBN agency gives a publisher a block of numbers, for example 978-618-5321 for 100 books. In the `Codes` tab, `Add a block` and write the prefix with its hyphens exactly as the agency gave it.

For every block you see how many numbers are in use, the next free one (`Copy` or `New title with it`) and the list of numbers in use with their titles.

- `Used outside this app`: numbers you gave to books before you used orOS, or spoiled ones. orOS will not offer them again.
- `Numbers used twice` appears at the top when two formats have the same ISBN.
- `ISRC prefixes`: your country and registrant code as the agency gave them (for example GR-A1B). Each prefix shows the codes in use and the next free one for this year (CC-XXX-YY-NNNNN).
- `UPC / EAN prefixes (GS1)`: your GS1 company prefix, as EAN-13 or UPC-A. You see how many codes are in use and the next free one with its check digit.
- `Release codes used elsewhere`: ISRCs, UPCs or EANs you used outside this app, or spoiled ones; they are never offered as free.
- `Check a code` tells you if any ISBN, ISRC, UPC or EAN is valid, shows an ISBN's ISBN-10 and whether one of your titles has it.

## Your data
- Everything stays on this device and is saved as you go.
- With orOS sync switched on in [Settings](app:settings), your titles and blocks follow you to your other devices, encrypted. Cover pictures travel with Vault Drive.

## Find it from anywhere
- The menu's search box finds a title by its words, its people or any of its ISBNs (with or without hyphens, ISBN-10 too); a music release also by its tracks, ISRCs and UPC/EANs. Choosing one opens the title.
- [Today](app:today) lists your posts and release-plan steps when they are due, the release days coming up, review copies to follow up and, in one line, the manuscripts waiting too long for an answer.
