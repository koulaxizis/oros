# Automations
Automations lets orOS do things for you at the right moment. A rule says when something happens and what orOS does then: show a notice, open a list of tasks, start a note, a timer, an e-mail or a Budget entry. Rules are plain settings, never code, and they follow you to your other devices, encrypted.

## Getting started
- Press `Ready-made` and pick a rule, for example `Plan the week` or `Monthly bills`. Change what you like and press `Save`.
- Or press `New rule`, give it a `Name`, choose `When` and add the steps under `Then`.
- The switch next to a rule turns it on or off. The line under its name says when it runs next.

## When a rule runs
- `Every day or on some days`: tick the days you want, or none for every day, and set the `Time`.
- `Every month`: a day of the month, or `The last day`. The 31st runs on the last day of shorter months.
- `Every year` and `Once`: pick the `Date`.
- `When orOS opens (once a day)`: the first time you open orOS each day.

## When something happens
A rule can also follow what happens instead of a time:
- `When an app posts a notice`: a notice from one app (for example Mail or Calendar) or from any app. With `Only when it says`, only notices with that word count (capitals and accents do not matter).
- `When something happens`: you finish a task in To-Do, tick a habit, reach your water goal or finish a workout.
- `Only on these days and hours` keeps it to, say, weekdays from 9:00 to 17:00.

In its texts, `{text}` becomes what the notice or the event said: the task, the habit, the workout or the notice's text. Such a rule runs once for each thing that happens, at most 20 times a day, and only on the device where it happened. Notices from Passwords never start a rule, and nothing of Passwords is ever read.

## Folders on your computer
With [orOS Bridge](https://github.com/koulaxizis/oros-bridge/releases/latest) (free, Windows and Linux) running on your computer, a rule can follow a folder there, for example the folder your scanner saves into or your Downloads.
- Press `Computer folders`, tick `Watch folders on this computer` and connect to Bridge with the code it shows. Then `Add folder…` opens the folder chooser on the computer; only you pick the folders, and `Stop watching` lets one go.
- The events `When a new PDF lands in a folder on my computer`, `… a new photo …` and `… any new file …` start a rule. `{text}` is the file's name and `Only when it says` looks at the name. Files that were already there are never reported, and files that arrived while orOS was closed come the next time it opens.
- Two steps work only after such an event: `Open the file in an app` hands it to Scan PDF, Files or Invoices, and `Print the file` sends it to the computer's printer. Both wait for your tap, and nothing is saved in Files or Invoices until you confirm it there.
- The ready-made rules `New scan into Scan PDF` and `New photo into Files` are a good start. Bridge only reads files in the folders you picked, never subfolders or links.

## Outside orOS
Two steps leave orOS by themselves when the rule runs:
- `Push to my phone`: a push through [ntfy](https://ntfy.sh) (free, no account; pick a topic nobody can guess, or your own server) or your own Gotify server. Install the ntfy or Gotify app on the phone and subscribe to the same topic.
- `Send to a webhook`: a small JSON message (`source`, `rule`, `at`, `text`) to your own n8n, Home Assistant (an automation with a Webhook trigger), Make or similar. The server must use https and accept requests from orOS (CORS; ntfy does).

Under each one you see exactly what is sent, and nothing leaves until you tick `Let this rule send this…` for that address; change the address and you tick it again. Only the device that runs the rule sends it, once. Keep the address secret: anyone who has it can post to your topic or webhook. The Assistant can suggest rules but never these two steps.

## Incoming webhook
`Turn on` under Incoming webhook gives this device a secret address. Any service that can send a web request (n8n, Home Assistant, a form, a script) can POST a small JSON object (up to 4 KB) to it, and a rule with `When a webhook arrives` runs here; `{text}` becomes the JSON's `text` or `message` field, or the whole JSON. orOS picks up what arrived every few minutes while it is open, so a message may wait until you open orOS.
- The relay encrypts what arrives at once with a key only this device has and keeps it until this device picks it up (at most 100 messages). It sees the message in plain text as it arrives and never stores it that way or logs it.
- `New address` stops the old one at once; `Turn off` deletes the box. After 7 days without orOS on this device the address stops by itself.
- The address works on this device only; turn it on on each device that should react.

## Ask the Assistant
Tell the [Assistant](app:assistant) what you want, for example "every Monday at 8 remind me to put the bins out" or "when an urgent mail comes, add it to To-Do". It writes the rule and shows it on a card; `Open in Automations` opens it here as a new rule, and nothing is saved until you press Save. Ask it "why did this run?" and, if you let it read Automations, it looks at your rules and the recent runs on this device (never what a notice said) and explains, or suggests a better rule. It never sees Passwords.

## What a rule does
- `Show a notice` appears by itself in the notifications, like any reminder.
- Every other step needs your go-ahead: the notice says what a tap does, and the tap opens that app filled in (To-Do, Calendar, Notes, Mail, Budget, Time or the Assistant). Nothing is saved in that app until you save it there.
- When a rule has several steps, the tap opens Automations with one button for each step.
- In texts, `{date}`, `{weekday}`, `{month}` and `{year}` become the day of the run, so "Bills {month}" turns into "Bills November".
- `Try it now` in the editor shows what the rule would do, without waiting for its time.

## Good to know
- Rules run while orOS is open on one of your devices. If orOS was closed at the time, the next time you open it a missed run comes once, as long as it is less than 36 hours old.
- A run happens once for all your devices: when one device has shown it, the others see the same notice and do not repeat it.
- A rule that needs an app you have not installed on this device is skipped there and marked in the list.
- `Recent runs on this device` shows the last runs and what happened.
- Notices follow your notification settings. If notifications are off, or turned off for Automations, rules run quietly and the list says so.
- Passwords is never part of an automation.

## Your data
- The rules are saved on this device and, with sync switched on in [Settings](app:settings), on your other devices too, encrypted. The list of recent runs stays on each device.
- The menu's search box finds your rules by their names and texts.
