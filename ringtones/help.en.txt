# Ringtone Composer
Write a melody note by note, like on the old phones, hear it, and take it with you as a ringtone.

## Getting started
Press a key on the phone keypad and the note is written and played. The melody shows on a small staff and, under it, as short notes like `8e6`: the length, the note and the octave.
- `Length` and `Octave` set what the next note will be.
- The play button at the top right plays the melody from the selected note, and stops it.
- The piano button at the top swaps the keypad for a piano.

## The phone keypad
- `1` to `7` write the notes C D E F G A B (do re mi fa sol la si). `0` writes a rest.
- `8` makes the selected note shorter and `9` longer.
- `*` moves the selected note one octave up, round from 4 to 7.
- `#` puts a sharp on the selected note, or takes it off. E and B have no sharp.
- On a computer keyboard the same keys work, and the letters C to B write notes too. `.` adds a dot (half as long again), `+` and `-` move a note a semitone, Space plays and stops.

## Editing
- Tap or click a note on the staff or in the list to select it. New notes go after the selected one.
- The arrows move the selection, and `Delete note` removes the selected note.
- Undo and Redo are at the top, and with [[Ctrl+Z]] and [[Ctrl+Y]].
- Give the melody a name at the top of the screen.

## Tempo and sound
- `Tempo` sets how fast the melody plays, in beats a minute.
- `Sound` picks `Phone beep`, `Soft` or `Bell`.
- `Play again and again` keeps playing the melody until you stop it.

## My melodies and ready tunes
- `Save melody` keeps it under `My melodies`. Changes to an open saved melody are saved as you go.
- `New melody` starts an empty one.
- In the list, the play button plays a melody without opening it. The copy button makes a duplicate, and the × deletes it; `Undo` brings it back.
- `Ready tunes` are old public-domain melodies to start from. Tapping one opens it as a new melody.

## Take it with you
- `Download WAV` saves the melody as a sound file you can set as a ringtone on your phone.
- `Download RTTTL` and `Copy RTTTL` give the melody as RTTTL text, the format of old phones.
- `Import RTTTL` reads a ringtone in RTTTL text, pasted or from a .txt or .rtttl file.
- A melody sent from `Make a ringtone` in Studio opens here with its text filled in `Import RTTTL`; press `Import` to keep it.
- `orOS file (.orostone)` saves the melody with its name, tempo and sound. An .orostone is an open ZIP file: unzip it and you find `content.json` (the melody), `preview/tone.rtttl` (RTTTL text) and, for a melody up to a minute long, `preview/tone.wav`. Open one with `Open a file` in `Import RTTTL`, or bring back an unzipped one with `Unzipped orOS folder…`; it is added to `My melodies` as a new melody.

## Sync and backup
- `My melodies` follow you to all your devices through orOS sync.
- The keypad or piano, the note names, the volume and the next length and octave stay on this device only.

## Tips
> Under `Note names` choose `C D E` or `Do Re Mi`, whichever you learned.
- RTTTL ringtones from the internet can be imported and then changed.

## Limits
- Up to 200 saved melodies, each up to 255 notes, as on the old phones.
- Notes go from octave 4 to octave 7, and one note sounds at a time.
- When the same melody is changed on two devices before they sync, the most recent change wins.
