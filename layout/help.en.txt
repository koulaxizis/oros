# Layout
Layout is a page-design app for print: flyers, newsletters, posters, cards and booklets. Place text, images and shapes on pages and export a PDF ready for the printer.

## Getting started
Press `New document` and choose a `Page size` (such as A4, `Business card` or `Custom`), `Orientation`, `Pages`, `Margins`, `Columns` and `Bleed`. Tick `Facing pages (spreads)` for a booklet. You can change all this later in `Document setup…`. Under `Start from`, pick `Blank` or one of 7 ready templates, such as a flyer or a newsletter.

Your documents appear as cards on the home screen, where you can search, rename, duplicate or delete them (with undo right after).

## Place things on the page
Pick a tool on the left and drag on the page: `Text frame (T)`, `Image frame (F)`, `Rectangle (R)`, `Ellipse (E)` or `Line (L)`. `Select (V)` moves and resizes objects; `Hand (H)` moves the view.

With something selected, the `Properties` panel sets position and size, `Rotation`, `Opacity`, `Fill`, `Gradient` (for shapes: `Linear` or `Radial`, start and end colour, angle), `Stroke`, `Arrange`, `Align`, `Group` and `Lock position`. Right-click (or long-press) an object for a quick menu. `Hide` hides an object; with nothing selected, the page panel has `Show hidden objects`.

Blend: select two shapes (rectangles, ellipses or paths) and the `Properties` panel shows `Blend`. Set `Steps` (1 to 50) and press `Blend`: new shapes appear in between, changing evenly in place, size, rotation, outline and colours. Each step gets a colour swatch of its own when its colour is new. Changed one of the two ends? Select a step and press `Update`, or change `Steps`. `Release` keeps the steps as plain shapes.

Drag from a ruler to add a guide, and drag a guide back onto the ruler to remove it. The `Zoom` menu has `Fit page`, `Fit spread`, 50, 100 and 200 %.

### Text
Double-click a text frame, select it and press [[Enter]], or click it with the `Text frame (T)` tool, and type straight into it: the caret, the selection and the reflow follow the frame through its columns, its threads and its rotation. A new text frame is ready for typing at once. Drag or [[Shift]]+arrows select, a double click picks a word and a triple click a paragraph; [[Enter]] starts a paragraph, [[Shift+Enter]] breaks the line and [[Esc]] finishes. Paragraph and character styles in the `Styles` panel apply to the selection.

`Edit text` (right-click menu or `Properties`) opens the `Story Editor` with the whole story in one place, where you can also insert the page number or count. Numbering starts at 1; to start at another number (a book whose page 1 is a cover), set `First page number` in `Document setup…`.

If the text doesn't fit, the status bar shows `Overset text`. Use `Continue in a new frame` and then tap or click another frame or an empty spot, or `Autoflow: add pages until it fits`. `Import text (.txt)…` in the `More` menu brings in a text file.

### Images
Double-click an image frame, or use `Place image…`, to choose a picture. Pick `Fit` or `Fill` and adjust `Scale`; the panel warns when the resolution is low for print. `Text wrap` lets text flow around a frame.
A picture sent from another app with `Send to…` (for example from [Files](app:files)) lands on the current page in a new frame that fits the margins, or in a new document when none is open.

## Pages, masters, styles and colours
- `Pages`: add, duplicate, delete and reorder pages, and manage `Masters`. Anything on a master appears on every page that uses it; `Edit master` opens it. To change master objects on one page only, use `Detach master objects here` (or right-click one for `Detach this master object`); `Reset to master` undoes it.
- `Styles`: paragraph and character styles with font, size, leading, alignment, spacing and colour. A style can be `Based on` another.
- `Kerning` in a style uses the font's own spacing for pairs such as AV or Το, on screen and in the PDF alike. New documents, rebuilt PDFs and Scribus files have it on; older documents keep their line breaks until you switch it on. The built-in Noto fonts have no kerning pairs, so it matters with your own fonts and free fonts.
- `Book typesetting` in a paragraph style: `Hyphenate Greek` breaks Greek words at their syllables, with a hyphen (new documents have it on; older ones keep their line breaks until you switch it on). `Keep with next` keeps a heading with the paragraph after it. `Fewest lines before a column break` and `Fewest lines after a column break` stop a paragraph leaving one lonely line at the foot of a column or the top of the next. `Start` puts a paragraph in the next column, on a new page or on an odd (right-hand) page, for chapters. Pages are not added for you: threaded frames must be there.
- More fonts: in a style's `Font` pick `Other fonts…`. `Free fonts` searches the open-licence Fontsource collection (tick `Only fonts with Greek` for Greek text); `Add a font file…` takes your own TTF, OTF or WOFF and keeps it in Files › Fonts, where it syncs like any file. A font is downloaded once, then works offline; it is embedded in the PDF, and a package (`.oroslayout`) carries your own font files. Fonts with PostScript (CFF) outlines, WOFF2 files and fonts whose licence forbids embedding cannot be added yet. Until a font is on the device, Noto stands in.
- `Colours`: your `Swatches`, in RGB or CMYK. CMYK looks approximate on screen, but the PDF gets the exact values.

## Preflight and export
The `Preflight` badge in the status bar turns green, orange or red. Click it for the list of problems: `Overset text`, `Missing image`, low resolution, objects that reach the trim edge but not the bleed, text smaller than 6 pt and empty frames. Pick a problem to select its object.

`Export` makes a PDF of all pages, the current page or a range, with optional `Spreads`, `Include bleed` and `Crop marks`, and images at `Print (300 ppi)` or `Screen (150 ppi)`. `Save as package (.oroslayout)` saves one file with the document and its images; `Open file` › `A file (.oroslayout, .sla, .idml, .pdf, .svg)…` on the home screen opens it again. The package is an open ZIP file: unzip it and you find `content.json` (the document), `media` (its images) and a thumbnail. `Open file` › `An unzipped package folder…` brings such a folder back, even after you changed an image in it.

`Image (PNG / JPG)` exports one page as a picture at 72, 150 or 300 dpi (up to 16 megapixels).

## Book cover
In `New document`, press `Book cover…`. Choose the `Trim size`, the number of `Pages` and the `Paper`: Layout works out the spine, but ask your printer for its width and type their figure in `Spine (mm)`, because theirs is the one that counts. Add `Flaps (mm, 0 = none)` for a cover with flaps. Fill in the title, author, `Back cover text`, `ISBN` and `Publisher`, and press `Choose picture…` for the front. You get one page with the back cover, the spine and the front cover side by side, bleed all round, guides on the folds and a vector ISBN barcode in black on the back. Everything stays editable, and the PDF export works as for any document. The Publishing app opens this dialog already filled in with `Cover in Layout`.

## Data merge
Make many name cards, labels, badges or certificates from one design and a CSV file (from Spreadsheet, Excel or any address book export; separated by commas, semicolons or tabs, with the field names in the first row).
- In a text frame, type `{{Name}}` where a value goes, using the column names of the file (capitals and accents don't matter). Formatting stays: a bold `{{Title}}` gives a bold title. A line that only holds empty fields is left out, so labels have no blank lines.
- For a picture per card, select an image frame and type the column name in `Data field (merge)`; that column holds file names such as `anna.jpg`.
- `More` › `Data merge (CSV)…` (or `Export` › `Data merge`) opens the dialog: `Choose CSV file…`, check that every field says `found`, and step through the rows in the preview.
- Pictures are found by file name in a folder on the orOS disk (`/internal/Pictures` and its subfolders unless you type another) or with `Add pictures from this device…`. They are only read for the PDF: nothing is copied into the document.
- `One per page` repeats the current page (or `All pages`, for a front and a back) once per row. `Several per sheet` places as many cards as fit on A4, A3 or Letter (portrait or landscape, whichever holds more) with the `Gap` you choose and cut marks around them.
- `Create PDF` saves one PDF with real, selectable text.

## Automations
Steps you set once and run again and again, on the open document or on all documents. No code: you pick the steps from a list.
- `More` › `Automations…` (or `Automations` on the home screen) lists them. `New automation`, give it a name, then `Add a step`: `Find and replace text` (with `Match case` and `Whole words only`), `Paragraph style` (paragraphs in one style, or any, get another), `Master on pages` (all, odd or even pages), `Page margins` in mm, `Change a style` (size, leading, font, alignment or tracking of a paragraph style, by name), `Preflight check` and `Export PDF` (bleed, crop marks, spreads, print or screen pictures). Move steps with ↑ and ↓.
- `Run` shows the steps and asks `Run on`: `This document` or `All documents`. The open document changes as one step you can undo; for the others, `Undo` on the message that follows puts every document back.
- Styles and masters are matched by name, so one automation works in every document that has a style of that name. A document without it is left as it is.
- With `Do not export a document with errors`, a document whose preflight finds errors is not exported. One PDF is saved with the usual Save dialog; several go to Files, in the `Layout` folder, without overwriting older ones.
- Automations sync with your other devices, together with those of Atelier and Photo Lab.
- `Share` saves an automation as a `.orosrecipe` file to send to anyone with orOS; `Import` in the list reads one back. The file holds only the steps, never code: it is checked when it is read and opens in the editor, where you look at it and press `Save`. A file made for another app offers to open it there.

## Bring work from other programs
`Open file` on the home screen also opens, as a new document:
- Scribus `.sla` and compressed `.sla.gz` files: pages, masters, styles, colours, threaded text, shapes and free shapes (polygons, curves, arcs, spirals), linear and radial gradients, layers (their order; objects on hidden layers come in hidden, on locked layers locked) and embedded images.
- InDesign `.idml` files (in InDesign: File, Save As, InDesign Markup): pages, masters, paragraph and character styles, colours, threaded text frames, shapes, lines, groups and embedded images.
- PDF files, for example from Affinity Publisher: the text becomes editable frames, every picture becomes its own image frame, and drawings and backgrounds become one locked picture per page behind everything.
- SVG files, for example from Affinity Publisher or Designer (File, Export, SVG; leave text as text, not curves): shapes, gradients, text and embedded pictures. Pick several SVG files at once to get one document with a page per file, in file-name order.

The fonts a Scribus file names are looked for by name, first among your font files, then among the free fonts; a style whose font is not found keeps the closest Noto font. Linked images come in where and how Scribus placed them (scale, offset, fit or stretched to the frame) when you open the `.sla` together with its pictures: pick them along with the file in `Open file › A file…`, or open the folder Scribus makes with `File › Collect for Output` through `Open file › A folder…`. Pictures are matched by file name. Any still missing stay empty frames with their file name: `Find pictures…` in the message after opening places them later, or put one back with `Place image…`. Pick the book's print PDF too, and the lines break where they did in it: soft hyphens where it hyphenated, and a `Line fit` character style on the lines that need a little tracking. The message says how many lines match. Tables, text on a path and mesh or pattern fills become simple boxes or plain colours; the message after opening says how many.

## Rebuild a printed book or magazine
`Open file` › `Rebuild a print PDF (with its pictures)…` turns the PDF you sent to the printer back into a Layout document you can edit, with every line where it was. Pick the PDF and, if you have them, the original pictures and the text (.txt, .md or .html) in the same go.
- Made in Affinity? Pick the document's `.af` file with the PDF (experimental). Its original pictures, full size, are placed instead of the PDF's copies, its words join hyphenated line ends, and the report names its styles and fonts. Only the content is read from it; the page layout always comes from the PDF.
- The page size, bleed, facing pages, margins and paragraph styles are read from the PDF. The text becomes text frames with a `Body text` style and others named after their size. Before it starts you choose the `Text frames`: `Per page` (each page keeps its own frames, so every line stays exactly where it was) or `Going on across pages` (an article's text runs from frame to frame over the pages, so a correction flows on, but a line that comes out longer moves the lines after it). With the second, a frame whose last line runs to the column's end goes on in the next one when that one starts without an indent and no heading above it; a page that breaks after a full stop keeps two paragraphs.
- A picture that matches one of your originals uses the original file; the rest come from the PDF. Drawings, textures and pictures under a mask or blend become one locked picture per page, behind everything.
- Each of your PDF's fonts is looked for by name among your font files and the free fonts: one that is found is used as it is. The others are replaced with orOS fonts, with tracking that keeps each line as wide. For the closest result, add the book's fonts with `Add a font file…` before you rebuild. Page numbers become their own text frames with a page-number field, numbered from the PDF's first number (`Document setup` › `First page number`), so they follow when you add or move pages.
- Every line breaks where it did in the PDF. A word hyphenated at a line end gets a soft hyphen there (it shows a hyphen only when the line breaks there), and a line that would break elsewhere gets a `Line fit` character style with a little more or less tracking, the way you would fit a line by hand.
- At the end a report says how many lines are where they were, which pictures are originals, and lists the pages where lines moved: press a page to go there and check it before you print.
- The [Assistant](app:assistant) can do the same: attach the PDF with `For an app only…` and ask it to rebuild it in Layout. The report then has `Back to the Assistant`, and the Assistant can change styles for you through an automation and show you a page.


## Sync and backup
Your documents sync between your devices through [orOS sync](help:t/sync). Images are stored on the orOS disk and sync as separate files, so on another device a picture may show `Syncing…` for a while. The open document and zoom stay on each device. For a full copy with images, save a package; see also [backup](help:t/backup).

Text changed on two devices at once? The other version waits in `Recovered text` in the `More` menu.

## Keyboard shortcuts
- [[V]] [[T]] [[F]] [[R]] [[E]] [[L]] [[H]] pick a tool; [[W]] toggles preview.
- Arrow keys nudge the selection ([[Shift]] for bigger steps); [[Delete]] removes it.
- [[Ctrl+Z]] / [[Ctrl+Y]] undo and redo; [[Ctrl+C]], [[Ctrl+X]], [[Ctrl+V]], [[Ctrl+D]] duplicate, [[Ctrl+A]] select all.
- [[Ctrl+G]] groups, [[Ctrl+Shift+G]] ungroups; [[Ctrl+E]] opens Export.
- Typing in a frame or in the Story Editor: [[Ctrl+B]], [[Ctrl+I]], [[Ctrl+U]]; [[Esc]] finishes.

## Tips
- Large documents fill the storage that every app shares; Layout warns you when one grows big.

## Limits
- Hyphenation is for Greek words only; in other languages a word breaks where you type a soft hyphen.
- A PDF opens up to 300 pages; its text keeps its place and look, not its original styles or threads.
- A rebuild reads up to 400 pages.
- A document can have up to 999 pages and 5,000 objects.
- Data merge reads up to 1,000 rows (5 MB) and makes a PDF only: the merged cards are not kept as a document, and the CSV file is not saved with it. A text frame does not grow to fit a long value: the preview and the final message say when text does not fit.
- `Several per sheet` uses one page per card (no backs) and cuts each card at its edge, so bleed is not printed.
- A document made with a newer orOS shows `Needs a newer orOS` and does not open until this device updates. Syncing it is safe: nothing in it is lost.
