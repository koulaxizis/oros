# Studio
A small multitrack studio: put audio clips on tracks, write notes for built-in instruments in a piano roll, start from ready loops, record over a click, add effects, draw automation, play a MIDI keyboard, mix and export the song as WAV, MP3 or Opus.

## Getting started
- `Import audio` at the top (the down arrow) brings in audio files: WAV, MP3, OGG, FLAC, M4A, whatever this browser can play. You can also drop files from your computer onto the timeline.
- `+ Track` adds a track: an `Audio track` for recordings and audio files, or an `Instrument track` that plays notes with a built-in sound. A project has up to 8.
- The first import or track makes a new project. Tap or click the project name at the top to rename it, and the menu button at the top left lists your projects.

## Playing and the timeline
- `Play` starts from the white playhead and pauses; `Stop` stops and goes back to where you started; pressed again, it goes to the start (or to the start of the loop).
- Tap or click the ruler to move the playhead. Tapping an empty part of a track also moves it there.
- Drag along the ruler to mark a loop. `Loop` turns it on and off; with no loop yet it makes one of four bars from the playhead.
- Set the tempo (BPM) and the beats in a bar next to the clock. `Click` turns the metronome on.
- The zoom buttons, or [[Ctrl]] and the mouse wheel, make the timeline wider or narrower.

## Editing clips
- Tap or click a clip to select it. Drag it to move it, also up or down to another track.
- Drag the left or right edge of a clip to trim it.
- `Snap` makes moves and trims land on a bar or a beat; `Free` lets them land anywhere.
- With a clip selected, the bar at the bottom offers `Split` (at the playhead), `Duplicate` (a copy right after it), `Delete`, `Gain`, `Fade in` and `Fade out`.
- Undo and Redo are at the top.

## Tracks and mixing
- `M` mutes a track and `S` solos it. When a track is soloed, only soloed tracks are heard.
- The slider under a track's buttons is its volume. The dots button opens the track's settings: name, colour, volume, pan, move up or down, delete.
- `Master` sets the volume of the whole mix, and the meter next to it shows the level while playing.

## Effects
- `FX` on a track opens its effects; `FX` next to `Master` opens the effects of the whole mix. On a phone, the track's effects are in its settings (the dots button).
- `+ Effect` lists more than 70 effects (EQ, compressors, delays, reverbs, saturation and more), grouped and searchable. A chain has up to 8, and they play in order, top to bottom.
- Many effects show their own interface: drag its sliders and knobs, or use the mouse wheel over them. `All controls` lists every setting as a plain slider, list or file choice.
- The power button bypasses an effect, the arrows change the order, and `×` removes it.
- The play button in the effects window plays the song while you adjust. Changes are heard at once and can be undone.
- Convolution can use your own impulse response: set `Source` to `IR File` and choose a WAV file (up to 20 MB). It is kept in the `Studio/fx` folder and syncs like the rest of the audio.
- Some effects need a moment to look ahead. Studio delays the other tracks to match, so everything stays in time, also in the export.
- The effects are [NULL JSFX](https://nulljsfx.tech) (MIT License), running in the browser.

## Sidechain
- `Sidechain Ducker` turns a track down while another track plays: the classic pumping pad under a kick, or music that makes room for a voice.
- Add it to the track that should duck, then choose the other track in `Key input (sidechain)` under the effect. Its sliders set how loud the key must be, how far down the track goes and how fast it comes back.
- The effect hears the key track after its effects and before its fader, so you can mute the key track and still use it.
- A track cannot be the key of a track that already listens to it; such tracks are left out of the list. The export ducks exactly as the playback does.

## Automation
- `A` on a track (on a phone: `Automation` in its settings) opens a lane under the track. Choose what it moves in the list: `Volume`, `Pan` or any slider of the track's effects.
- Tap or click the lane to add a point, drag a point to move it, and double-click it (or select it and press `×` or [[Delete]]) to remove it. The arrow keys move a selected point, in finer or coarser steps with [[Shift]].
- Between two points the value moves in a straight line; before the first and after the last point it holds. Volume moves evenly in dB.
- While a lane has points, it drives that control: the track's fader dims for a volume lane, and moving it by hand does nothing. The power button in the lane turns it off for a moment, and the trash button clears it. A track has up to 16 lanes.
- Automation plays back, is heard in the export, and is saved and synced with the project.

## Recording
- `R` on a track arms it: new takes go there. With no armed track, Studio records on the selected one.
- Press the red record button. The browser asks once for the microphone. Studio plays from the playhead (with the click, if it is on) and records until you press it again.
- The take is saved as a WAV file and placed where it was played. If takes land late against the click, raise `Recording offset` in the settings.
- In the settings (the three dots at the top) choose the input and, where the browser allows it, the output of your sound card.

## Recording through your sound card (orOS Bridge)
- A browser records through the system's shared mixer: one stereo input and some delay. With orOS Bridge (a small free program for Windows and Linux, github.com/koulaxizis/oros-bridge, version 0.3.0 or newer) Studio records through your sound card itself.
- In the settings set `Record and play through` to `orOS Bridge`, type the 6-digit code it shows once, then pick the `Sound system` (Windows Audio, ASIO®, JACK, PipeWire / PulseAudio or ALSA), the card and the `Inputs`: one input, a stereo pair, or every input, each on its own track. On Windows, `Exclusive` gives the lowest delay while other programs go quiet.
- ASIO (Windows, orOS Bridge 0.4.0 or newer, with `oros-bridge-asio.dll` next to it): the card's own ASIO driver records and plays, so the card you pick is used for both, at the buffer size set in the driver's control panel.
- `Check the inputs` shows a meter for each input of the card, so you can see where your microphone or guitar comes in.
- Press record as usual. Studio first prepares the song from the playhead (a moment for a long song), then the card plays it and records at the same time, so the take stays in step even when the computer is busy. Listen through your card's direct monitoring.
- The take lands where it was played, minus the delay the bridge measures for the card; if it still lands late, raise `Recording offset`.
- `Listen through a plugin` (orOS Bridge 0.5.0 or newer): pick one of your effects (an amp for a guitar, a reverb for a voice) and you hear the inputs through it while you play and record, with only the card's buffers and the plugin's own delay in between. `Check the inputs` opens the card with the plugin, so you can set its sound in `Show its window`. A take records the inputs as they come and the track keeps the plugin with that sound: freeze it later in `Plugin on this computer`. Or choose `A take records` › `The plugin's sound`. Use headphones, and turn the card's direct monitoring off.
- An instrument plugin in `Listen through a plugin` (orOS Bridge 0.6.0 or newer) plays from the `MIDI keyboard` you pick there: the bridge reads the keyboard itself, so the notes reach the instrument with only the card's delay. Record on an instrument track: the take is a MIDI clip of the notes you played (velocity and sustain pedal too), and the track keeps the instrument and its sound, so `Plugin on this computer` freezes the notes through it. While it plays, Studio's own keyboard sound stays quiet.

## Keyboard and MIDI
- `Keyboard` in the transport opens a piano keyboard with a built-in synth: piano, electric piano, organ, strings, lead and bass. Play it with the mouse or with your fingers, and slide along the keys for a glissando.
- `Connect MIDI keyboard` asks the browser for MIDI and plays the synth from every connected keyboard, with velocity, the sustain pedal and pitch bend. Studio asks only when you press it, and reconnects by itself next time once you have allowed it.
- The keyboard plays the sound of the armed instrument track, or of the selected one; the panel shows which. Changing `Sound` there changes that track's instrument.
- On an instrument track, the record button records what you play on the keys or a MIDI keyboard into a new MIDI clip, in time with the click, with velocity and the sustain pedal. The notes can be edited afterwards in the piano roll.
- On an audio track, while the keyboard is open, the record button records the synth as an audio take instead of the microphone.

## Instrument tracks and MIDI
- An instrument track holds MIDI clips: notes with a pitch, a start, a length and a velocity, played by one of the built-in sounds (piano, electric piano, organ, strings, lead, bass or a drum kit). Its colour dot is round, and its clips show their notes.
- Double-click or double-tap an empty spot of an instrument track to make a new two-bar clip there; the piano roll opens on it. `New clip` in the track's settings (the dots button) does the same at the playhead.
- Change the sound with `Instrument` in the track's settings. The notes stay as they are.
- MIDI clips move, trim, split, duplicate and delete like audio clips, and only onto instrument tracks. The track's effects, automation, mute, solo and sidechain work as for audio, and every export includes the notes.
- When you change the tempo, MIDI clips keep their bars and beats; audio clips stay where they are.
- `Import audio` also takes MIDI files (.mid, .midi, type 0 and 1). A file with one part goes into the selected instrument track; otherwise each part gets its own new track, with a sound picked from its General MIDI program (channel 10 becomes the drum kit). An empty project takes the file's tempo.
- `Export MIDI` saves a standard MIDI file: in the piano roll for one clip (type 0), in the track's settings for the whole track (type 1, with the project's tempo and bars).

## Sampled instruments
- Besides the built-in sounds, `Instrument` (in the track's settings, or the sound list of the keyboard panel) offers a grand piano and 22 recorded instruments in groups marked `(download)`: upright piano, pipe organ, violin, viola and cello sections, contrabass, pizzicato violins, harp, flute, oboe, clarinet, bassoon, French horn, trumpet, trombone, tuba, glockenspiel, marimba, xylophone, timpani, tubular bells and orchestral percussion (bass drum, snares, cymbals, gong, tambourine, triangle and more, on the drum keys of a drum track).
- They come from VS Chamber Orchestra: Community Edition by Versilian Studios, under CC0 (free for anything, no credit needed). Studio downloads an instrument the first time a track uses it (0.1 to 4.5 MB) and keeps it on this device, so afterwards it also plays offline. Until it is here the track plays its built-in sound.
- The keys, a MIDI keyboard, the piano roll and every export play the instrument. An export waits until every instrument of the song is downloaded.
- `Grand Piano` is the Salamander Grand Piano by Alexander Holm (a Yamaha C5, 10 MB), under CC BY 3.0: when you publish a song with it, name the author. `Export` lists the instruments the song uses under `Credits`, with that line ready to copy.
- `Instruments on this device` (under `Instrument`) lists what was downloaded, with its size; `Remove` frees the space, and the instrument downloads again when a track needs it.
- `Import SFZ instrument` takes a folder with an .sfz file and its samples (WAV, FLAC, MP3, OGG; up to 200 MB). Studio copies them onto the orOS disk (Studio/instruments), where they sync to your other devices like audio files, and the track plays it. Studio plays the common SFZ settings (key and velocity ranges, tuning, volume, envelope, loops, round robin, release samples); others are left out. Check the instrument's licence before you publish with it.
- `Free sounds` (in `Add a track`) lists 34 single sounds from the same orchestra, in three groups: drums, cymbals and gong, small percussion. `Listen` plays one; `Add` puts it on an audio track at the playhead as a clip, with its licence card (CC0) already filled in.
- A recorded note lasts as long as its recording: a very long held note on strings or winds fades out after about eight seconds.

## Your own plugins (VST3, CLAP)
- On a Windows computer Studio can use the VST3 and CLAP plugins installed there, the ones you bought too, through orOS Bridge: a small free program (MIT) from github.com/koulaxizis/oros-bridge. It runs only on your computer and only orOS can talk to it.
- In a track's settings press `Plugin on this computer`. The first time, orOS Bridge shows a 6-digit code; type it in Studio once and this browser stays paired.
- Pick a plugin: on an instrument track an instrument plugin plays the track's notes, an effect plugin processes its sound; on an audio track you pick an effect. `Show its window` opens the plugin's own window on the computer, to set its sound. `Look for plugins` searches the usual folders again after you install new ones.
- `Freeze to audio` plays the track through the plugin into a new audio track, with the same volume, pan and effects, and mutes the original. It is one step that `Undo` takes back. The plugin's settings are kept with the new track, so `Freeze to audio` on it later starts from the same sound.
- The frozen track is ordinary audio: it plays, exports and syncs on every device, also without the computer. A track with a NULL instrument cannot go through a plugin yet.

## Piano roll
- Select a MIDI clip and press `Edit notes` in the bar at the bottom, or double-click the clip, or press [[E]]. On a phone the roll fills the screen.
- `Draw`: tap or click to add a note, and drag while adding to make it longer. `Select`: drag a box around notes, or tap them; with [[Shift]] they are added to the selection.
- Drag a note to move it, and drag its right end to make it longer or shorter. Double-click or double-tap a note to delete it. With a mouse, hold [[Alt]] to move off the grid.
- `Grid` sets the step for drawing, moving and quantizing: 1/4 to 1/32, triplets, or `Free`. `Quantize` moves the selected notes (or all of them, with none selected) onto the grid.
- The `Velocity` slider sets how hard the selected notes play; the lane under the notes shows each one and can be dragged too.
- Copy, paste, delete, select all, undo and redo have buttons and the usual keys. On a touch screen, drag an empty area to scroll and drag notes to move them.
- `Import MIDI` in the roll replaces the clip's notes with the notes of a MIDI file.
- The play button in the roll plays from the start of the clip. Every edit is heard at once and can be undone like any change in Studio.

## Loops
- `Loops` in the transport opens the loop library: 32 short patterns of drums, bass and keys in eight styles, made by Studio itself and free to use in anything you make.
- Filter them by style, tempo and kind. The play button lets you hear one at the project's tempo.
- Drag a loop by its handle onto a track, or press `+` to add it at the playhead. It lands on the selected instrument track when it fits (drums on a drum track, bass and keys on a pitched one), otherwise on a new instrument track.
- A loop becomes an ordinary MIDI clip: edit its notes, change its sound or repeat it with `Duplicate`. It follows the project's tempo.

## Sound info and credits
- Select an audio clip and press `Sound info` to write down where its sound comes from: its title, who made it, the licence and a link to its page. Every clip that plays the same sound shares the card, and it follows the sound to your other devices and into the .orosstudio file.
- Licences: `CC0` and `Public domain` ask nothing; `CC BY` asks you to name the author; `CC BY-SA` also asks that what you make from it carries the same licence; `Other` means follow the terms of the place you got it from. `My own sound` is for your own recordings.
- `Export` lists the sounds of the song that have a card under `Credits`. `Copy` puts the text on the clipboard and `Save as text` saves it as a .txt file, ready to paste where you publish the song.
>! Studio only keeps what you write. Check a sound's licence where you downloaded it before you publish a song with it.

## Remote control
Control Studio's mixer and transport from a tablet or phone while the sound plays on the PC.
1. On the PC, open the settings (the three dots at the top) and press `Connect a tablet or phone`, then `Show a code`. Studio shows a 6-digit code and a QR code.
2. On the tablet or phone, scan the QR code with the camera, or open the Remote for orOS Studio app (or Studio, `Control Studio on another device` in the settings) and press `Scan the QR code` or type the code.
3. On the PC, press `Allow`. Once a device is allowed, no other device can join with that code.
- The remote shows play, stop, record, loop, the click, the tempo and a position bar, and a strip per track with the fader, pan, `M` `S` `R`, effect on/off and a meter, plus the master.
- `FX` on a strip opens that track's effects, as on the PC: the effect's own interface with its meters, every control, on/off, order, adding and removing. Files for effects (such as Convolution's IR) are chosen on the PC.
- Drag a fader to move it from where it is; a double tap puts it back to 0 dB. Every move is a normal edit on the PC, so [[Ctrl+Z]] undoes it there.
- On the same network the two devices talk directly. Through a VPN or on different networks (for example a phone on 4G) they talk through the orOS relay instead, with a little more delay. Either way the link is encrypted with the code, and the relay only passes it on.
- `Disconnect` on the PC ends the link at once. If the link drops, the remote reconnects by itself while Studio stays open on the PC.
- The screen of the tablet or phone stays on while it shows the console, and a phone gives a short tick when you grab a fader, pass 0 dB or press a button.
- **Remote for orOS Studio** is the remote as an app of its own for Android (orOS's [F-Droid repository](https://koulaxizis.github.io/oros-fdroid/), Droid-ify, Obtainium or the orOS download page) and at useoros.online/studio/remote/ in any browser. It keeps the last code on that device only: after `Stop controlling`, or when you come back to the app, `Connect` joins the same PC again without a new `Allow`, as long as Studio there still shows that code. It stores nothing of your projects.
>! Press `Allow` only when it is your own device asking. If you did not just type the code somewhere, press `Deny`.

## Export
`Export` (the up arrow) mixes the song into one stereo file: the loop if it is on, else everything from the start to the end of the last clip. Instrument tracks, effects, sidechain and automation are all in it.
- `WAV`: 16 or 24 bit, 44.1 or 48 kHz. Lossless, the best choice for further work.
- `MP3`: 128 to 320 kbit/s. Plays everywhere; Studio's own encoder makes it on this device, and players start and end it without a gap.
- `Opus (OGG)`: a smaller file at the same quality, always 48 kHz. Offered where the browser can encode Opus (Chrome, Edge, Firefox).
- `Add to Music` makes the same file in the chosen format, puts it in the Music folder on the orOS disk and plays it in Music.
- `Make a ringtone` turns the notes of the instrument tracks (not the drum kit) into a one-voice melody and opens it in Ringtone Composer: where notes overlap the highest one wins, and you press `Import` there to keep it.

## Sync and backup
- Projects, tracks and clips, the notes of MIDI clips too, follow you to your other devices through orOS sync.
- The audio itself is kept as files on the orOS disk, in the `Studio` folder of the [Files](app:files) app. With sync on and [Vault Drive](help:t/sync) turned on, the files travel to your other devices too. Until a file arrives, its clips show that the audio is not on this device yet.
- The zoom, the snap, the click, the sound card choices and the armed track stay on this device.

## orOS file (.orosstudio)
- `Save as orOS file (.orosstudio)` in `Projects` saves the open project as one open file named after it: tracks, clips, the notes of MIDI clips, effects with their settings, automation, tempo, the loop and the audio itself. It is a ZIP: unzip it and you find `content.json` (the project), `media` (the audio and the effects' files) and `preview/tracks.txt` (what is on each track), `preview/song.mid` (the instrument tracks) and `preview/thumbnail.png`. Big projects are fine: the audio is never loaded into memory whole, and a bar shows how far it is.
- `Open orOS file…` opens one again as a new project, with its audio copied to the orOS disk; so does `Import audio` or dropping the file on the tracks, and opening the file with orOS from your system (`Open with`). Your existing projects never change. `Unzipped orOS folder…` brings back a .orosstudio that was unzipped, even after you changed a file in it.
- Audio that is not on this device yet (still on its way with Vault Drive) is left out of the file, and Studio says so.

## Keyboard shortcuts
- [[Space]] play and pause, [[R]] record, [[L]] loop, [[K]] click, [[Home]] back to the start. [[Space]] also plays while the effects window is open.
- [[S]] split, [[D]] duplicate, [[Delete]] delete the selected clip, [[E]] or [[Enter]] edit the notes of a MIDI clip, [[Esc]] deselect.
- In the piano roll: [[B]] draw, [[V]] select, [[Q]] quantize, [[Ctrl+A]] [[Ctrl+C]] [[Ctrl+X]] [[Ctrl+V]] [[Ctrl+D]] select all, copy, cut, paste, duplicate, [[Delete]] delete, the arrow keys move the selected notes (by a bar or an octave with [[Shift]]), [[Space]] play, [[Esc]] close.
- [[+]] and [[-]] zoom, [[Ctrl+Z]] undo, [[Ctrl+Y]] redo.
- On the ruler, the arrow keys move the playhead by a beat, and by a bar with [[Shift]].

## The Assistant
Ask the Assistant to change your songs in words, such as "turn the vocals down 3 dB and pan them a little left", "mute the drums", "move the chorus clip to bar 9", "fade the last clip out over 4 seconds", "transpose the keys up 2 semitones", "add a bass track", "set the tempo to 96" or "loop bars 5 to 8". It shows the changes on a card first; `Apply` makes them here and opens the project. `Undo` on the message here, or on the Assistant's card, puts the project back as long as it wasn't changed since. See the Assistant's help for everything it can do.

## Tips
> Use headphones when you record, so the click and the other tracks do not end up in the take.
- A browser on its own cannot use ASIO drivers: choose `orOS Bridge` in the settings for your card's own driver and the lowest delay.

## NULL Drum Machine
- Besides the built-in sounds, an instrument track can be played by `NULL Drum Machine`, the first instrument of NULL JSFX: ten synthesized drum voices (kick, snare, clap, rim, closed and open hat, two toms, cowbell, cymbal) and a 16-step sequencer. Pick it in `+ Track`, or as the `Instrument` in a track's settings or as the keyboard's `Sound`.
- Its notes follow the General MIDI drum map (36 kick, 38 snare, 42 closed hat, …), so drum loops and drum parts of MIDI files play on it as they are.
- Its sequencer plays the chosen `Pattern` (House, Techno, Boom Bap and eight more) in time with the song while it plays, on top of the clip notes; `Off` leaves only the notes. With no clips, turn on the loop: the loop is then the song, for playing and for export.
- `Effects` on the track shows it first, with its own interface: click a step to cycle it (hit, accent, flam, roll, off), right-click to clear it, set each track's length (1 to 16 steps), click a voice's name to hear it, and turn its knobs. A step you draw switches to the `User` pattern, which is saved with the track and syncs like the rest. Every control is also in `All controls`, and each one can be automated.
- The keyboard and the piano roll play it straight away; the song plays it through the track's effects.

## Limits
- Up to 50 projects, 8 tracks and 500 clips per project, one hour per project.
- An imported file can be up to 100 MB, and a take up to 30 minutes.
- Through orOS Bridge you hear the song through the card but not your own input, unless you pick `Listen through a plugin` (or use the card's direct monitoring), and the click stops two minutes after the end of the song. With ASIO only one Studio tab at a time can use the card.
- One remote at a time, and pairing needs internet on both devices.
- A MIDI clip has up to 2000 notes. The instruments are simple built-in synths and a synthesized drum kit; there are no sampled instruments or soundfonts.
- While you play the keyboard live, you hear the sound without the track's effects; the recorded clip plays with them. The synth recorded as audio is also dry until the take plays. Only Sidechain Ducker takes a key input.
- A MIDI file's tempo changes are not kept: Studio uses its first tempo. Controllers other than the sustain pedal (pitch bend, modulation) are not recorded into clips.
- Loops are in 4/4. Audio clips do not stretch with the tempo; only MIDI clips follow it.
- An older Studio on another device does not show MIDI clips, and when it saves an instrument track, the track plays as piano until its sound is chosen again. It plays a NULL Drum Machine track with the built-in drum kit, and when it saves the track the drawn `User` pattern is lost.
- Automation has straight lines only, and the master has no lanes. An older Studio on another device drops lanes and sidechain choices when it saves that track.
- The MP3 encoder is simple and has constant bitrate: at 128 kbit/s it sounds less clean than other encoders, so choose 192 or more.
- Effects reset when playback starts, as in a desktop studio.
- Audio of deleted clips stays in the `Studio` folder; deleting a project removes its files.
- When the same clip is changed on two devices before they sync, the most recent change wins.
