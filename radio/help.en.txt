# Radio
Radio plays thousands of internet radio stations from around the world. Browse by country or genre, search by name, and keep your favourites. Music keeps playing even after you close the app.

## Getting started
Radio opens on `Discover` with the top stations worldwide. Tap or click a station to play it. A player bar appears at the bottom with play/pause, the station's name, the sleep timer (☾), the station tools (⋯), stop (■) and a heart for favourites.

The tile `Surprise me` plays a random top station.

## Find stations
- `Countries` lists every country with its number of stations. Pick one to see its stations; the chips above the list filter them by genre. Tap a chip again to clear the filter.
- `Genres` lists genres such as jazz or news; pick one to see its stations.
- The search box (`Search stations…`) suggests stations once you type three letters. Use [[↑]] and [[↓]] to pick a suggestion, or press [[Enter]] to see all results. With the box empty, clicking into it suggests your recent stations.

Each station shows its bitrate, format and country code where known.

## Favourites and recent stations
While a station plays, tap the heart in the player bar to add it to `Favorites`; tap it again to remove it. `Recent` keeps the last 20 stations you played. The ✕ button in that view clears them, with `Undo` for a few seconds.

### Import stations
`Import stations` at the top of `Favorites` adds stations from a list file made by another player:
- **M3U / M3U8**: most players and radio sites (VLC: Media › Save Playlist to File; many stations offer a "listen" link as .m3u).
- **RadioDroid**: Settings › Backup / restore or the favourites menu › Save (export) as M3U.
- **PLS** and **XSPF** playlists (Winamp, foobar2000, VLC, Clementine…).
- **CSV**: one station per line, `name,url` (a header line is optional).

Before anything changes, a preview shows how many stations are new, which are already in your favourites and what is skipped and why: a station listed twice, an address that isn't a stream, or one that only streams over plain http. orOS plays https streams only; tick `Try the http-only stations (…) over https` to add those anyway, since many servers answer both. `Import` adds them all at once, with `Undo` for a few seconds. Importing the same file again adds only what's missing.

## Playing in the background
Playback runs in orOS itself, so it keeps going when you close the app. A chip in the taskbar shows the station; tap or click it for play/pause, stop, favourite, volume and `Open Radio`. Your device's media controls (lock screen, headset buttons) can also play and pause.

### Sleep timer
Tap ☾ and choose 15, 30, 60 or 90 minutes. The time left shows in the player bar; the sound fades out during the last minute and playback stops when it runs out. `Turn off` cancels the timer.

### Play on a speaker or TV
The button with the screen-and-waves icon in the player bar (`Play on…`) chooses where the station plays.
- **Bluetooth speaker or headphones:** pair them in your system's Bluetooth settings (on orOS USB: Quick settings › Bluetooth). orOS then plays through them like any other speaker. Where the browser lets a page choose the output (Chrome and Edge on a computer, once a page has had microphone access; Firefox through `Choose a speaker…`), the outputs are listed under `This device`. orOS never asks for the microphone just for this.
- **Chromecast, AirPlay or a TV:** `Chromecast, AirPlay or TV` opens the browser's own list of devices. Chrome on Android finds Chromecast and Cast TVs, Safari finds AirPlay. orOS loads no code from Google or Apple for this; the browser does the connecting. While a TV plays, the button turns gold; open it again and choose `Stop or change`.
- **Smart TV or network speaker (DLNA):** with [orOS Bridge](https://useoros.online/download/) 0.10.0 or newer running on a Windows or Linux computer at home, the same window lists the smart TVs, Kodi boxes and network speakers on your network. The first time, `Connect to orOS Bridge` asks for the 6-digit code the bridge shows (Studio and Scanner & PDF use the same pairing). Choose a device and it plays; the window then has pause, volume and `Back to this device`. The device fetches the stream itself, so only http and https addresses go. `Search again` looks once more.
The station keeps playing on the TV or speaker when you close Radio.

## Station tools
The ⋯ button in the player bar opens the tools for the station that's playing.

### Now playing and song history
When a station announces its songs, the title shows under `Now playing` and in the player bar (♪). `Song history` lists what was playing and when, newest first; `Clear history` empties it. The history stays on this device.

### Record
`Record` saves the stream to your orOS disk, in Files › Radio, named after the date, time and station. The player bar shows a red dot and the time recorded; `Stop recording` saves the file. A recording also stops and is saved after 4 hours or 200 MB.

Recordings are for your own personal use only. Radio programmes are protected by copyright: don't share, publish or sell them.

### Wake up to a station
Under `Wake up to this station`, choose a `Time` and whether it repeats (`Once` or `Every day`), then `Set alarm`. The alarm appears in [Time](app:time), and when it rings the station starts playing, softly at first. You can also pick an existing alarm from Time and press `Use`. `Remove` takes a station off an alarm.

## Sync and backup
Your favourites and wake-up stations sync between your devices through [orOS sync](help:t/sync). Song history, recent stations and the temporarily kept station lists stay on this device. Stations can be imported (see `Import stations` above), not exported; favourites are part of the orOS [backup](help:t/backup).

## Keyboard shortcuts
- In the search box: [[Enter]] searches or plays the highlighted suggestion, [[↑]] / [[↓]] move through suggestions, [[Esc]] closes them.

## Tips
- Station lists you've just browsed are kept for 30 minutes, so they open instantly. Playing always needs a connection.
- If a station won't play, you'll see `Stream unavailable`: try another, as streams come and go.

## Limits
- Stations come from the free community directory radio-browser.info, so names, logos and genres are only as good as that directory. Radio tries several of its servers and waits for a slow one (a VPN such as Tailscale or mobile data can make the first answer slow). If none answers (no connection, or a content blocker), lists you opened in the last 7 days still show from this device, and only a list with nothing to show says the directory isn't answering. Favorites and recent stations never need the directory, so they keep playing.
- Favourites can only be added from the player bar, the taskbar chip or `Import stations`, not from the station list.
- Song titles and recording need a station that lets web pages read its stream (CORS). Many don't: then `This station does not say what it plays.` shows, or recording explains it isn't allowed. The station directory itself has no song information.
- Recording downloads the stream a second time while it runs, so it uses double the data. Playlist-style streams (HLS) can't be recorded.
- A wake-up station only plays if orOS is open when the alarm rings, and the browser may keep the sound muted until you touch the page.
- orOS only plays stations over an encrypted (https) connection. A station listed with a plain http address is tried over https; if it doesn't answer there, `Unencrypted (http) stream only, not played` shows. An unencrypted stream would make the page insecure, and the installed app would then show the browser's address bar.
