# Maps

Find places, plan routes by car, bike, on foot or by public transport, follow spoken turn-by-turn directions, record and follow GPX tracks, and help improve OpenStreetMap with notes and place edits.

## Getting started

Type a place or address in `Search a place…`. Suggestions appear from the third letter, and places near the part of the map you're looking at come first. Tap or click one to drop a pin. You can also type coordinates, such as `37.9755, 23.7348` or `37°58'32"N 23°44'05"E`.

`Use my location` shows where you are (your browser asks for permission the first time). The layers button at the top right switches between `Standard`, `Humanitarian`, `Satellite` and `Topo (contour lines)` maps. `Topo` shows contour lines and hill shading, useful for hiking; its images stop at a medium zoom and are enlarged beyond that.

## Places

- A pin's popup has `Save` (star), `Route` (from your location to there) and `From here`.
- Right-click the map, or long-press on a phone, to use any point: `Go here`, `Start here`, `Add as stop` (once a route is set) and `Save`.
- A long-press also offers `Measure distance` and `I parked here` (see Measure and parking below).
- `Saved places` lists everything you starred. Tap a place to fly there. The pencil renames it (`Rename place`) and the × deletes it, with `Undo`.

### Favourite groups

- Put saved places in groups, as in OsmAnd: the folder button next to a place, or `Group` in a saved place's popup, picks a group or makes a `New group` with a name and a colour. The pin on the map takes the group's colour.
- The list shows each group with its places, then `No group`. Next to a group: the arrow folds it, the eye hides its pins from the map, the pencil edits it and the arrow down exports it as a GPX file.
- `Groups` at the top lists every group with how many places it has; tap one to rename it, change its colour or delete it. Deleting a group keeps its places, without a group.
- `Import` reads the waypoints of a GPX, KML or GeoJSON file (for example favourites exported from OsmAnd) as saved places, into a new group named after the file, a group you have, or none. A file with only waypoints opened in `My tracks` goes the same way. KMZ files and Google saved places and lists come in too, a group for each list (see Import from another app).

## Discover

The compass button opens `Discover`, which looks around the middle of the map. Long-press › `What's nearby` does the same for any point.

- **Places**: pick a kind (restaurants, cafés, bars, fast food, supermarkets, bakeries, pharmacies, ATMs and banks, fuel and charging, toilets, sights and museums, parks). Each place shows whether it's open now and when it closes or opens, read from its OpenStreetMap opening hours; `Open now` keeps only the open ones. Tap one for its hours, address, phone and website, `Go here` and `Save`.
- **Wikipedia**: articles about places around you, in the language of orOS, with a picture when there is one. The popup links to the article.
- **Photos**: street-level photos from Panoramax, the open alternative to Street View, once you zoom in close. The dot's arrow shows which way the photo looks; tap a photo to see it larger and open it in Panoramax.
- After you move the map, `Search this area` looks again. The tab, kind and `Open now` stay as you left them on this device.

## Plan a route

1. Tap `Plan a route`. A new plan starts `From` `My location`.
2. Type in a row to search. Your saved and recent places are offered too. Or tap `Choose on map` in that row and then tap the map.
3. Use `Add stop` for up to 3 stops. `Swap` reverses the whole trip.

The route bar shows distance and travel time (an estimate without live traffic), plus `Car`, `Bike`, `Walk`, `Directions` (the step list), `Edit route`, `Send to Calendar`, `Start navigation` and `Clear route`. `Send to Calendar` asks for a `Day` and adds a "Route to …" event to [Calendar](app:calendar) with the distance, time and mode.

## Route choices and sharing

- The time in the route bar is followed by the arrival time, if you leave now.
- When the server finds other good routes, they appear as grey dotted lines. Tap one to use it, or pick it under `Routes` at the top of `Directions` (with how much longer it takes). Routes with stops have no alternatives.
- By car, `Avoid` in `Directions` skips `Tolls`, `Motorways` or `Ferries`, one at a time. If the server can't, you get the normal route and a message. The choice stays on this device.
- `Share` (in the route bar, in `Directions`, on a place, or on a long-press) gives you a link that opens the place or the route in orOS Maps, `Open in another app` (a `geo:` link: on Android it offers OsmAnd, Organic Maps and others), `Open on OpenStreetMap`, `Copy coordinates` for a place, and `Send…` where the device can share.
- `Save to My tracks` keeps the route as a track (with the start, stops and destination as waypoints), so you can export it as GPX or follow it later.
- `Download GPX` saves the route as a .gpx file (for a GPS device, another maps app or a friend) without keeping it in orOS.

## Public transport

Buses, metro, tram, trains and ferries, with live times where the operator publishes them. The timetables come from [Transitous](https://transitous.org), a free community service built on open data.

- Open it with the bus button on the left, with the bus button in the route bar (same start and destination), or with a long press › `Public transport to here`.
- `From` and `To`: the target button sets `My location`, the pin button lets you tap a point on the map. The arrows swap them. `Use the route's points` takes the start and destination of the route you planned.
- Pick `Leave now`, `Depart at` or `Arrive by` and a time, then `Find connections`. `Earlier` and `Later` page through the times.
- Each connection shows its times, the lines in their colours, the changes and how far you walk. A delay shows as "+3 min", and a cancelled trip is marked.
- Open a connection to see it step by step and drawn on the map (walking dotted). Tap a step to zoom to it. `Share` sends it as text with a link, and `Save to My tracks` keeps it as a track.
- Tap a stop name in a trip, a stop on the map, or long press › `Departures nearby` for the departures board. It refreshes every minute while it is open. At Athens bus stops the board also shows `Live arrivals (OASA)`: the minutes until each bus arrives, from OASA Telematics through the orOS relay.
- `Show stops on the map` shows stops when you zoom in. `Show live vehicles` moves trains, metro and buses on the map by their timetable and live data.

Coverage depends on open timetables. In Greece it includes Thessaloniki city buses, Hellenic Train with live data, and bike sharing in some areas. Athens is listed too, but since mid-September 2026 its timetables at Transitous are out of date, so searches there find nothing until Transitous refreshes them; Maps tells you when the stops near your points show no departures at all. Intercity buses (KTEL) and ferries are not included yet.

## Navigation

`Start navigation` follows your GPS position and speaks each turn in the orOS language. If you're more than about 150 m from the start, the route is recalculated from where you are. Leave the route and it's recalculated automatically (when you're online).

- Move the map and it stops following you. `Recenter` brings it back.
- The compass button turns the map with you, so the road ahead is always up (`Turn the map with me`); tap it again for `North up`. Moving the map turns it back to north until you tap `Recenter`. This device remembers your choice.
- `Mute voice` silences the instructions.
- The instructions are spoken only with a voice installed on your device, never with a browser's online voice (such as Chrome's "Google …" voices), which would send them to its server. If your device has no voice for the orOS language, a note says so and you follow the screen; you can add a voice in your system's speech settings.
- By car, a speed limit sign appears next to your speed when OpenStreetMap knows the limit of the road you're on. It shows the number as written on the road (a square sign for mph) and turns red when you go more than 5 km/h over it. No sign means the limit isn't known; always follow the real road signs.
- The bottom bar shows the arrival time, the time left and the distance left. Before a turn, the lanes appear under the instruction: the bright arrows are the lanes to take.
- `Exit navigation` needs a second tap within 3 seconds.
- Where the browser allows it, the screen stays on while you navigate.
- If Maps reopens in the same tab within 30 minutes, navigation picks up again.

## Measure and parking

**Measure**: long-press a point › `Measure distance`. Each tap on the map adds a point, and you can drag a point to move it. The sheet shows the distance, the last part and the number of points. `Close the shape` joins the last point to the first and shows the area (m², ha or km²) and the perimeter. `Undo` removes the last point, `Clear` starts over. Nothing is saved; closing the sheet or opening another mode clears it.

**Parking**: long-press where you left the car › `I parked here`. Pick a reminder (`No reminder`, `In 30 min`, `In 1 hour`, `In 2 hours` or `At a time`) and, if you like, a note such as "level -2, spot 14". A blue P marks the car. Tap it for when you parked, the reminder, the note, `Walk there` (a walking route from where you are), `Change` and `I left` (removes it). Long-press anywhere › `Show my parked car` takes you back to it. The reminder is an orOS notification, so it comes even with Maps closed, as long as orOS is open; tapping it opens Maps on the car.

## OpenStreetMap notes

A note tells the volunteers who map [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org) that something is wrong or missing: a shop that closed, a new path, a wrong street name. They read it, fix the map and resolve the note.

1. Tap `Mapping (OpenStreetMap notes)` (the flag). The notes of the area appear on the map and in the panel (zoom in if you see `Zoom in to see the notes of this area.`). Red notes are open, green ones resolved in the last week.
2. To report something, long-press the map (right-click on a computer) and pick `Report to OpenStreetMap`, or use `New note at the map centre`. Describe it clearly: other people will read it. If an open note is already close by, Maps shows it first.
3. Tap a note to read it. You can `Comment`, `Resolve` or `Reopen` it.

Writing needs a free OpenStreetMap account: `Sign in with OpenStreetMap` opens the OpenStreetMap page in a small window, and you come back signed in. Reading works without one. `Mine` lists the notes you created or commented on, and Maps tells you when someone replies.

> Without a connection, or when OpenStreetMap doesn't answer, what you write goes to `Waiting` and is sent from this device as soon as it can. If you wrote it on another device, `Send from here` takes it over.

In `Maps settings` › `OpenStreetMap` you sign out, pick the `Test server` (for trying things out, it's not the real map) and set the `Upload comment`. That line, "Uploaded using orOS Maps" unless you change it, goes at the end of your new notes (you can edit it each time in `Ending line`) and will be the comment of your map edits.

## Edit places on OpenStreetMap

Fix shops, cafés and other places on the real map, the way the Every Door app does it: walk past, check, correct.

1. Zoom in close and tap `Edit places (OpenStreetMap)` (the pencil). The places around you appear as dots and in a list, nearest first.
2. Tap a place. `Edit details` opens a form with the fields that fit its kind: name, address, opening hours (tap the days, type the times), phone, website and more. `All tags` shows everything as key=value for experienced mappers.
3. `Checked today` records that the place is still as described. `Closed / gone` keeps the address and building and removes the business. `Move` puts it where the cross is, `Delete` removes it.
4. `Add place` shows a cross: drag the map so the cross sits on the spot, then pick the kind (search in English or Greek).
5. Your changes wait in the list until you tap `Upload`. You see the upload comment once more and can change it.

> If someone changed the same detail on OpenStreetMap meanwhile, their value stays and Maps tells you which one. Details you didn't touch always follow the map. If the upload fails, nothing is lost: the change stays in the list with the reason, and `↻` tries again. `Undo my change` in a place drops your change.

Changes made on another device show `From another device` and are uploaded by that device. `Upload from here` takes them over.

An orange ring on a dot means the place is missing something important (its name, or opening hours for a shop or café); the place shows what is missing.

`Add place` also has `Small things (one tap)`: a bench, litter bin, bollard, street lamp, post box, drinking water, fire hydrant, defibrillator, tree and more. Put the cross on the spot and tap one.

### Entrances and flats

`Entrances and flats` in the Places panel shows the buildings around you (zoom in to street level) with their entrances: green when they have flat numbers or a letter, orange when not.

1. Tap on a building's outline where the door is. A tap next to a corner uses that corner.
2. Pick the kind (main entrance, staircase, other, service, garage, emergency exit), type the letter on the door (for example Β), the flat numbers (for example 5-8, or 5;6;7) and whether there are steps.
3. Tap an existing entrance to change it.

Changes wait in the panel until you tap `Upload`; they go in one changeset with your upload comment. A new entrance becomes part of the building's outline. If the building changed on OpenStreetMap meanwhile, the entrance stays in the list with a message; remove it and put it again.

## Quests (StreetComplete style)

Help OpenStreetMap by answering small questions about what's around you. You don't need to know any tags.

1. Tap `Quests` (the pin with a question mark) and zoom in to street level. Pins show the open questions: 🪑 bench backrest, 🚏 bus stop shelter, 🚌 bench at the stop, 🚸 crossing type, 💡 street lights, 🛣️ surface, 🕒 opening hours, ♿ wheelchair access, 🚻 toilets fee, ♻️ recycling, 🛐 religion, 🏢 building floors, 🔢 house number, 🛝 playground access, 🚬 smoking, 🏧 ATM at the bank, 🚦 speed limit, 🏷️ street name, 🪜 number of steps, 🤚 handrail, 🦯 tactile paving at the crossing, 🪧 stop name, 🚧 level crossing barrier, 🅿️ parking fee, 🚗 kind of car park, 🚲 bicycle parking spaces, ☂️ covered bicycle parking, 🥗 vegetarian food, 🌱 vegan food, 💳 ways to pay, 📶 Wi-Fi, 🚾 wheelchair toilet, ❤️ defibrillator indoors, 🧯 fire hydrant, 📮 postbox collection times, 🏠 kind of building.
2. Tap a pin (or a row in the list) and tap the answer. The next question nearby opens by itself.
3. `Can't say` skips the question for now. `Hide here` hides it for that place on all your devices. `It's not there any more` opens a new note at the spot, so mappers can check.
4. Some answers get old: Maps asks again after some years (opening hours after one year) and `Still the same` confirms them.
5. Answers wait until you tap `Upload`. Each kind of question goes in its own changeset, with your upload comment (it starts as "Uploaded using orOS Maps"). If someone changed the same thing meanwhile, their value is kept.

`Settings` in the panel turns kinds of questions on or off. The counter shows your answers in total, today and the days in a row.

`Achievements` shows a level for each group of quests (shops and cafés, streets and paths, stops and crossings, parking, buildings and addresses, street furniture). Levels go up at 1, 10, 30, 100, 300 and 1000 uploaded answers, and each level unlocks a page to read more about that topic.

Team mapping: to map an area with friends without answering the same things twice, open `Settings`, everyone picks the same `People in the team` and a different letter. Each of you then sees only your share of the quests. No account or server is needed; pick `Just me` to see all of them again.

### Overlays

An overlay colours the streets or buildings around you by one thing, so the gaps stand out (red dashes mean "not known yet"), and a tap changes it. Pick one in `Overlay` at the top of the Quests panel:

- 💡 `Street lights`: lit or not.
- 🛣️ `Surface`: asphalt, paving stones, gravel, ground and more.
- 🚶 `Sidewalks`: on both sides, one side, a separate path or none. Arrows on the chosen street show its direction: left and right are as you see them walking that way.
- 🔢 `Addresses`: which buildings have a house number. Type the number or tap `It has no number`.
- 🏢 `Buildings`: floors and kind of building in one form (empty fields stay as they are).

Zoom in to street level (buildings need one step closer). Tap a street or building, then the answer. Changes wait with your quest answers until you tap `Upload`, and count for your achievements. While an overlay is on, the quest pins are hidden; pick `None` to bring them back. The overlay you picked stays on this device.

## My tracks (GPX)

Record where you walk, ride or drive, keep your tracks and follow them again, like OsmAnd does.

1. Tap `My tracks (GPX)` (the path button) and `Start recording`. You see distance, time, speed and elevation as you go. `Pause` stops adding points; `Resume` goes on with a gap in the line.
2. You can close the panel: a red chip at the top shows the recording, tap it to come back.
3. `Stop` asks for a name and saves the track. `Keep recording` goes back, `Discard` throws it away.
4. Tap a track for its statistics and a chart of elevation or speed. `Show all` fits it on the map. `Follow` draws it thick and tells you how far you are from it and how much is left. `Export GPX` saves a copy anywhere. `Name, colour, folder` organises it, and the eye button shows or hides it on the map.
5. `Import file` brings in GPX, KML or GeoJSON files, and KMZ, TCX, FIT and Google exports (see Import from another app below). On a computer with orOS installed, `Open with` › orOS works for .gpx, .kml, .geojson and .orosmap files too.

> Keep the screen on while you record. With the screen off, or with orOS in the background, the browser stops GPS and the track gets a straight gap. Maps keeps the screen awake while it records. If the tab closes or the device restarts, nothing is lost: next time Maps offers `Continue`, `Save` or `Discard`.

### Plan a track

Plan a hike, a ride or a drive before you go, like OsmAnd's route planner. Open it with long-press › `Plan a track from here` or `My tracks` › `Plan a track`.

- Pick how the next part goes: `Walk`, `Bike` or `Car` follow the roads and paths (the same free servers as Directions), `Straight line` goes straight, for example across a field or a beach. Each part keeps the choice it was made with.
- Tap the map to add points. Drag a point to move it; its parts follow the roads again. Tap a point to remove it. `Undo` removes the last one, `Clear` starts over.
- The sheet shows the length and, when you're online, the elevation profile with the climb, the descent and the lowest and highest point.
- Give it a name and tap `Save to My tracks`. It becomes a normal GPX track (with elevation and your points as waypoints), so you can follow it, edit it, export it or upload it.

Offline, new parts are straight lines and there is no elevation. The plan stays while Maps is open, even if you switch to another panel, but it is only kept for good when you save it.

### Edit a track

`Edit` opens the track editor. Move the two sliders to select part of the track (green start, red end on the map):

- `Keep only the selection` trims the rest, for example the drive to the trail.
- `Remove the selection` cuts out a part, for example a GPS jump, and leaves a gap.
- `Split at the start` makes two tracks at the start of the selection and saves both.
- `Simplify` drops points that add nothing to the shape. Pick the tolerance first; the line under it shows how many points stay.
- `Join with` adds another track to the end of this one.

`Undo` steps back. Nothing is saved until `Save changes`. If you close the panel, the editor waits for you with your changes; `Cancel` throws them away.

### Upload to OpenStreetMap

`Upload to OSM` sends the track to your OpenStreetMap GPS traces, where mappers use it to draw roads and paths. Sign in to OpenStreetMap first (as for notes). Choose the description (it starts as your upload comment), tags and the visibility: `Private` (only you see the trace; its points appear anonymously in the GPS layer, without order), `Trackable`, `Public` or `Identifiable`. OpenStreetMap needs a time on the points, so planned routes and exports without times can't be uploaded. After the upload, the track shows `View on OpenStreetMap`.

### Heatmap and statistics

`Heatmap and statistics` (in My tracks) shows where you've been:

- `Show my heatmap on the map` paints every track you have on the map. The colour goes from blue to red with the number of different tracks that passed there, so your daily paths stand out. It stays on when you close the panel.
- Choose a `Year` or a `Folder` to see only those tracks.
- The totals show distance, time and tracks, a chart per month and a table per year. `New ground` is the path through squares of about 40 m that you had not crossed in an earlier track: walk somewhere new and it grows, repeat your usual route and it doesn't.
- `OpenStreetMap GPS traces (everyone's)` adds the lines of GPS traces people uploaded to OpenStreetMap. It helps when you map paths and roads.

The heatmap is drawn on your device from your GPX files. A track whose file hasn't arrived yet counts with its simplified line.

Each track is a GPX file in Files › Maps › Tracks, so Vault Drive backs it up and brings it to your other devices. Until the file arrives, another device shows a simplified line and the statistics.

## Places shared with your household
With a [household](app:household) set up, `Household` in a place's popup puts the place in the household's places, with a name and a note (opening hours, which entrance, who to ask for). Everyone in the household sees them in their own Maps: in `Saved places` after your own, and on the map as orange pins. A pin's popup shows the note and who changed it last, with `Route`, `Edit` and `Remove` (`Undo` brings a removed place back). A place already in the household shows `In` plus the household's name on its button. Everything is end-to-end encrypted: the orOS relay only passes on what it cannot read. Nobody's location is shared, only the places someone put there.

`Stop sharing` next to the household's name takes the folder out of the household for everyone. Its places become your own saved places (the note goes after the name), and the others can bring the folder back from the Household app for 30 days.

## Import from another app

Bring your tracks and saved places over from another maps or sports app. Press `Import file` in `My tracks` or `Import` in `Saved places` and pick one or more files. Maps recognises them and shows what it found before anything is saved: how many tracks and places, where they go (My tracks and its folder, or Saved places with a group for each list), the dates, what you have already and what is skipped. Press `Import`; the message that follows has `Undo`.

How to export from each app:
- **Organic Maps**, **OsmAnd**: share or export a list or a track as KMZ (or KML, GPX). Tracks go to My tracks, places to a group named after the list.
- **Google My Maps**: the map's menu › `Export to KML/KMZ`. **Google Earth**: right-click a folder or place › `Save Place As` (KMZ). Pictures in the file are ignored.
- **Google Maps saved places**: Google Takeout (takeout.google.com), choose `Maps (your places)` and `Saved`. Pick the downloaded ZIP as it is, or `Saved Places.json` and the list files (`Want to go.csv`, `Favourite places.csv`…). Each list becomes a group. Google keeps only a link for many places in lists, without coordinates; those are counted as skipped (Maps does not look them up online).
- **Google Timeline**: on your phone, Google Maps › your picture › `Your Timeline` › the menu › `Location and privacy settings` › `Export Timeline data`; this gives `Timeline.json`. Older Takeout exports with `Records.json` or the monthly files of `Semantic Location History` work too.
- **Garmin**: in Garmin Connect, open an activity › the gear › `Export to TCX` or `Export Original` (a FIT file, sometimes in a ZIP). Other watches and apps that save TCX or FIT files work the same way.

Your Timeline is personal. Maps reads it on this device and tells you so in the preview; it leaves the device only if you sync Maps data. Nothing is imported until you pick the days with `From` and `To` (at most 366 days at a time). You then get one track for each day, in the folder `Timeline`, and, if you want, the places you visited in the group `Timeline places`, most visited first.

Importing the same file again adds only what's missing and never makes a second copy. Tracks and places you have already stay exactly as they are.

`orOS file` (in `Saved places` and in `My tracks`) saves the lists and tracks you tick as one .orosmap file, to keep or to share: your groups with their places and colours, and your tracks with their names, colours and folders. No map images and no OpenStreetMap sign-in go in it. An .orosmap is an open ZIP file: unzip it and you find `content.json` (your data) and `preview`, with `places.geojson` and `tracks.gpx`, which other map apps open. Open it with `Import` or `Import file`, or from your device with “Open with” orOS: its tracks and groups always come in as new ones next to yours, the places you have already stay as they are, and `Undo` in the message takes it all out again. `Unzipped orOS folder…` in the same window brings back an .orosmap that was unzipped, even after you changed its files.

## Offline maps

Download an area so the map and search work with no connection, as in Organic Maps and OsmAnd.

1. Move and zoom the map to the area you want. Open `Maps settings` › `Offline maps`. The dashed box on the map is what will be saved.
2. Give it a name and tap `Check size`. Maps reads the map's index and shows the size of each level of detail before anything big is downloaded: `Overview: towns, main roads`, `Roads and villages`, `Streets, shops and places` and `Full detail with buildings`. A larger area offers less detail; zoom in for more.
3. Tap `Download`. The bar shows the progress. `Pause` stops it and `Resume` goes on from where it stopped, even after you close Maps; if the connection drops it continues by itself when you're back online. `Cancel` stops it and deletes what was downloaded.

Each pack shows its size, its detail, when you downloaded it and the date of the map data. `Show` flies there and turns on the offline map, `Update` downloads the area again from the newest data (the old pack stays until the new one is ready), and `Delete` removes it. The list also shows how much space the packs use and about how much is free on the device.

- `Offline map (downloaded)` in the layers button shows your packs; outside them the map is hatched. When the device goes offline over a pack, Maps switches to it by itself, and back when you're online again. Past the pack's top detail the map is drawn larger, still sharp.
- Offline, the search finds names from your packs: towns and neighbourhoods, streets, shops and other places, parks and water, and addresses where the map data has them. Accents don't matter, and Latin letters find Greek names (`plaka` finds Πλάκα). The rows say `(offline)`.
- New routes still need a connection.
- `Map source (advanced)` takes the address of your own PMTiles file (vector tiles in the Protomaps layout) instead of the default.

## Online services and privacy

- **Map images**: OpenStreetMap (`Standard`), OpenStreetMap France (`Humanitarian`), Esri (`Satellite`) and OpenTopoMap (`Topo`).
- **Search and place names**: Photon by komoot. It gets your search text and the centre of the map, or the point you long-pressed.
- **Routes**: the OSRM demo server (car) and FOSSGIS (bike and walk). They get the coordinates of your route points.
- **Public transport**: Transitous (api.transitous.org). It gets the start and destination of your search, the time, the stop whose departures you open, and the map area when stops or live vehicles are on.
- **Live bus arrivals (Athens)**: when you open the board of an Athens bus stop, the orOS relay (the one Mail uses) passes that stop's code to OASA Telematics (telematics.oasa.gr). Nothing else is sent, and the relay keeps nothing.
- **Sharing**: links are made on your device. Nothing leaves it until you send or open one; a shared link carries the points' coordinates and the destination's name.
- **Notes**: the OpenStreetMap API. In Mapping mode it gets the area you're looking at. What you write is published under your OpenStreetMap name, with the point you chose. The sign-in key stays on this device.
- **Places and quests**: the Overpass API (overpass-api.de) gets the area you're looking at to list its places and questions; your edits go to the OpenStreetMap API under your name. Place kinds come from the iD editor's presets.
- **Speed limits**: while you navigate by car, the Overpass API gets the next few kilometres of your route (points along it) to read the speed limits of those roads.
- **GPS traces layer**: when it's on, the map images of public GPS traces come from OpenStreetMap (gps.tile.openstreetmap.org), like the standard map.
- **Discover**: the Overpass API gets the middle of the map and the kind of place you picked; Wikipedia (wikipedia.org) gets the middle of the map; Panoramax (api.panoramax.xyz) gets the map area you're looking at, and its photos load from the servers that host them.
- **Elevation**: when you plan a track, the elevation comes from AWS Terrain Tiles (Mapzen and others, on Amazon's open data registry); it gets the map tiles around your plan, not the plan itself.
- **Offline maps**: only when you check a size or download, the newest list of builds comes from Protomaps (build-metadata.protomaps.dev) and the map data from build.protomaps.com: parts of one big file, which tell the server the area you download. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL), vector tiles by Protomaps. A source you set yourself gets the same requests instead.
- **GPS traces**: only when you tap `Upload`, the track (points, times, elevation) goes to the OpenStreetMap API under your account, with the visibility you chose.

Your location stays on your device unless it's a route point. More in [Privacy](help:t/privacy).

## Sync and backup

Saved places, your track list (names, colours, folders, statistics), notes, place changes, entrance changes and quest answers waiting to be sent, hidden quests, quest settings, your answer counter, achievements and team choice, favourite groups and which group each place is in, and the upload comment sync through [orOS sync](help:t/sync). Your OpenStreetMap sign-in does not: sign in once on each device. These stay on this device: your 10 recent places, the `Avoid` choice, the public transport switches (stops, live vehicles), the quest overlay you picked, the Discover tab, kind and `Open now` choice, which favourite groups are hidden or folded, your parked car and its reminder, the last route (it comes back next time you open Maps), map images saved for offline use, your offline maps (download each one on every device that needs it) and the map source you set, and the map position and style.

`Maps settings` shows how many map images are stored (`Clear tile cache`) and the saved route (`Forget saved route`). GPX tracks are imported and exported in `My tracks`.

## Keyboard shortcuts

- [[↑]] / [[↓]] move through the suggestions, in the search box and in the route planner.
- [[Enter]] opens the highlighted suggestion, or the first one.
- [[Esc]] closes the suggestions, open panels, the planner or a "choose on map" pick.

## Tips

- [Bookmarks](app:bookmarks) and [Calendar](app:calendar) can open a place in Maps.
- Before a trip, download the area in `Offline maps`. Areas you only looked at while online also keep their map images, but only the parts and zoom levels you saw.

## Limits

- New routes need a connection. Offline you get your offline maps (map and search), areas you looked at before, saved places and your last route. Without an offline map, search needs a connection.
- Offline maps need a browser that can store files for a site (recent Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari 26). The browser may remove them when the device runs out of space. The offline map is drawn more simply than the online one: no house numbers or one-way arrows on the map, no hill shading, and names near the edge of a map tile may not show.
- Offline search finds only names in the map data, not every address; one area pack holds at most 600,000 map tiles, so a whole country comes with less detail.
- Navigation needs GPS, and Maps has to stay open.
- Speed limits come only from OpenStreetMap tags (no limits by zone, like "urban"), and are read online; offline or on an untagged road there is no sign.
- The parking reminder comes only while orOS is open in the browser.
- The routing servers are free public services and can sometimes be slow or down.
- Public transport needs a connection and shows only what operators publish as open data; times are not checked against the stop's own signs.
- Place editing needs a connection to load the places; changes made offline wait until you upload them.
- `Open now` is only as good as the opening hours mapped in OpenStreetMap. Hours it can't read for sure (by month, sunrise, holidays) show as unknown, and holidays are not known.
- Elevation in `Plan a track` is read from a terrain model about 20 m across, so short ups and downs (a bridge, a few steps) may not show.
- Recording needs the screen on and Maps open; the browser does not record in the background.
