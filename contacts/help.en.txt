# Contacts
Contacts is your address book: names, phones, emails, addresses, birthdays and the links between people. Birthdays and other dates show up in [Calendar](app:calendar) on their own.

## Getting started
Press `Add` to create a contact. You need at least a name or a `Company`; everything else is optional. Press `Save` when you're done.

Tap or click a contact in the list to open its card. From the card you can call, email or visit a website by tapping it (an email opens a new message in [Mail](app:mail) when Mail has an account on this device), tap an address to open it in [Maps](app:maps), and use `Edit contact` to change it. Favourites come first in the list, then everyone else in alphabetical order.

## Add and edit contacts
The contact window has:
- `First name`, `Middle name`, `Last name`, `Nickname`, `Company`, `Job title`.
- As many `Phones`, `Emails`, `Addresses`, `Websites` and `Instant messaging` entries as you like, each with a type such as `Mobile`, `Home` or `Work`. Use the `＋` buttons to add more.
- `Events`: `Birthday`, `Anniversary` or `Custom` (give it a name, for example a nameday). Type the date as year-month-day, like `1975-03-17`, or just month-day, like `03-17`, if you don't know the year.
- `Relations`: link to another contact as `Spouse`, `Parent`, `Friend`, `Colleague` and so on. The other contact shows the link too, under `Linked from other contacts`.
- `Labels`, `Favorite`, a `Photo` (JPEG or PNG, made smaller automatically) and a `Note`.

`Delete contact` asks to confirm, and an `Undo` button appears for a few seconds afterwards.

`Share` on the card opens `Send to someone`: send the contact as a link that opens in any browser (they can save it to their phone or add it to their own orOS), or pick `Send as text` for plain text as before. Photo, labels and relations are not sent.

## Search, labels and favourites
The search box finds contacts by name, nickname, company, job title, note, email or phone number. It ignores Greek accents and understands Latin spelling of Greek names, so `dionisis` finds “Διονύσης”. Three or more digits find a phone number however it's written. Searching for a name also finds people linked to that person.

Above the list, `All` and `★ Favorites` switch between everyone and your favourites. The label chips hide or show contacts with that label; you start with `Personal`, `Work` and `Family`. `＋ Manage labels` lets you add, rename, recolour and delete labels; a label still in use can't be deleted.

## Duplicates
`Duplicates` looks for contacts that are probably the same person: `Same phone` or `Same email` (listed as `Definitive matches`) and `Similar name` (`Possible matches`). Tap or click a contact in a group to open `Merge duplicates`: it shows the combined contact. Use `Flip primary` to pick which one to keep as the base, then `Merge now`. You can `Undo` straight afterwards.

## Import, skip and merge
Every import opens `Review import` first, whether it comes from a file, `From phone`, `From an account` or a .vcf opened with orOS. It sorts the contacts it read into three groups:
- `New`: nobody here matches. Choose `Add` or `Skip` for each.
- `Other details`: a contact here has the same email, the same phone (with or without the country code) or the same first and last name, and the import brings something new. You see what it adds and which values differ, for example `Company: Alpha Inc → Alpha Ltd`. `Merge, keep mine` adds the missing details and keeps your values; `Merge, use imported` adds them and takes the imported values where they differ. You can also `Add as separate` or `Skip`. `When a detail differs` sets the choice for all of them at once.
- `Already here, skipped`: identical contacts, left as they are.

A contact with the same name but other phones and emails is marked as probably another person and is added separately unless you choose to merge. Nothing is ever deleted, and `Undo` straight after the import puts everything back as it was.

## From an account (Google, Microsoft)
`From an account` copies your contacts from Google or from Microsoft (Outlook.com, Hotmail, Microsoft 365) once. Pick the service and sign in in the small window; orOS reads your contacts with their groups (they become labels), shows `Review import`, and ends the connection straight away. No password or access is kept: the Google access is withdrawn as soon as the reading ends, and the Microsoft one is forgotten and runs out by itself within the hour. Run it again whenever you want to bring in new contacts; the identical ones are skipped.

iCloud, Yahoo and other services: export your contacts there as a .vcf file and use `Import`.

If a service shows "not set up yet", orOS doesn't have its app id yet. Under `Use my own app id` you can register your own OAuth app with the address shown there and paste its client id; it stays on this device.

## On this phone (Android app)
In the orOS Android app, `On this phone` puts your orOS contacts in the phone's own Contacts, so the dialer and incoming calls show their names. Turn on `Show on this phone` and tap `Allow access` (Android asks once). Contacts arrive in an "orOS" account and follow every change you make here. It works both ways: when you edit or delete one of them in the phone's contacts app, or save a new contact to the "orOS" account there, the change comes into orOS Contacts the next time you open orOS. Labels, relations and instant messaging stay as they are in orOS, and a photo from the phone is used only when the contact has none here. Some contacts apps (Fossify Contacts, for example) let you pick the "orOS" account for a new contact; others only offer their own account. Turning the switch off removes them from the phone again.

Other apps can send contacts to orOS too. When a phone app offers "Add contact" or "Create contact" (after a call from a number you don't have, from Viber or another messenger) or opens a .vcf file, pick orOS. The contact opens in `Review import`, and nothing is saved until you confirm. The button doesn't appear in a browser, only in the Android app, and the choice is kept on this phone only.

## Sync and backup
Your contacts and labels sync between your devices through [orOS sync](help:t/sync). Which labels you've hidden is not saved.

- `Import` reads a vCard file (.vcf) or a Google Contacts CSV and opens `Review import` (see above). Afterwards you see a list of what was added, merged or skipped. With orOS installed on a computer you can also open a .vcf file with `Open with`; orOS shows the same review before importing it (see [opening files](help:t/install)).
- `From phone` (only in Chrome on Android) opens your phone's contact list. Pick the people you want; only those reach orOS, with their names, phones and emails, and addresses when the phone shares them. They go through the same `Review import`.
- `Export` saves all your contacts as a .vcf file that other address books can open.
- `Export JSON` saves a complete copy of your contacts. `Import JSON` restores it; it combines with what you have and keeps the newer version of each contact.

See also [backup](help:t/backup).

## Keyboard shortcuts
- [[Ctrl+F]] (or [[Cmd+F]]) jumps to the search box.
- [[Alt+N]] starts a new contact.
- [[Esc]] closes the contact card or the open window.

## Tips
- Birthdays, anniversaries and custom dates appear in [Calendar](app:calendar) every year. Tapping one there opens the contact here.
- `Family tree` on a contact's card opens that person in [Family Tree](app:familytree). If nobody in the tree is linked to the contact yet, it offers to build a tree from your contacts instead.
- `QR code` on a contact's card opens [QR Generator](app:qr) with a contact code (vCard) for that person, ready to save or print. The contact itself is not changed.

## Limits
- `Import JSON` only accepts files made by `Export JSON` in orOS Contacts.
- CSV import expects the Google Contacts format.
- `From an account` doesn't bring contact photos, and reads only your own contacts (not Google's "Other contacts" or a company directory).
- `From phone` appears only in Chrome on Android, because other browsers can't open the phone's contact list.
