# Contacts
Contacts is your address book: names, phones, emails, addresses, birthdays and the links between people. Birthdays and other dates show up in [Calendar](app:calendar) on their own.

## Getting started
Press `Add` to create a contact. You need at least a name or a `Company`; everything else is optional. Press `Save` when you're done.

Tap or click a contact in the list to open its card. From the card you can call, email or visit a website by tapping it (an email opens a new message in [Mail](app:mail) when Mail has an account on this device), tap an address to open it in [Maps](app:maps), and use `Edit contact` to change it. Favourites come first in the list, then everyone else in alphabetical order.

## Add and edit contacts
The contact window has:
- `First name`, `Middle name`, `Last name`, `Nickname`, `Company`, `Job title`.
- As many `Phones`, `Emails`, `Addresses`, `Websites` and `Instant messaging` entries as you like, each with a type such as `Mobile`, `Home` or `Work`. Use the `＋` buttons to add more.
- `Events`: `Birthday`, `Anniversary` or `Custom` (give it a name, for example a nameday). Type the date as year-month-day, like `1975-03-17`, or just month-day, like `03-17`, if you don't know the year.
- `Relations`: link to another contact as `Spouse`, `Parent`, `Friend`, `Colleague` and so on. The other contact shows the link too, under `Linked from other contacts`.
- `Keep in touch`: every so many weeks or months (see below). Leave it on `Off` if you don't want reminding.
- `Labels`, `Favorite`, a `Photo` (JPEG or PNG, made smaller automatically) and a `Note`.

`Delete contact` asks to confirm, and an `Undo` button appears for a few seconds afterwards.

`Share` on the card opens `Send to someone`: send the contact as a link that opens in any browser (they can save it to their phone or add it to their own orOS), or pick `Send as text` for plain text as before. Photo, labels and relations are not sent.

## Keep in touch
Each contact's card has a `Keep in touch` part. `Last contacted` shows when you were last in touch: the latest interaction you logged, or the day you pressed `Contacted today`.

`＋ Log` writes down an interaction: a `Call`, `Meeting`, `Message`, `Gift` or `Note`, with its date and a few words about it. They appear newest first under `Timeline` on the card. Tap one to change or delete it (`Undo` brings a deleted one back). An interaction dated in the future doesn't count as contact yet.

To be reminded, open `Edit contact` and set `Keep in touch` to every so many weeks or months. When that time has passed since the last contact, the person appears under `⏰ Reach out` above the list, with whoever has waited longest first, and a ⏰ shows next to their name. Someone you have never logged counts from the day you set it.

In `Reach out` you can also switch on `Remind me at` and pick an hour: from that hour, on days when someone is due, a notice appears in orOS. Tapping it opens the person's card, or the `Reach out` list when there are several. The reminder is off until you switch it on, and the switch and hour stay on this device. orOS has to be open for the notice to appear; if it was closed at that hour, it comes when you next open orOS that day.

## Scan a business card
`Scan card` turns a business card into a new contact. Press `Take a photo` (on a phone, the camera opens) or `Choose a picture`. orOS reads the text on the card on this device and shows it in `Text from the card`, where you can correct it. `Fill in a new contact` opens a new contact with the name, company, job title, phones, e-mails, website and address it found; check it and press `Save`. Lines it couldn't place go into the `Note`.

Reading the text needs the text recognition engine (about 7 MB, English and Greek). The first time on each device orOS says so and downloads it only when you press `Download and read`. It comes from orOS's own site, not from another company, and the picture never leaves your device.

You can also type or paste the card's text yourself (for example, from another scanning app) and press `Fill in a new contact`.

## Search, labels and favourites
The search box finds contacts by name, nickname, company, job title, note, email or phone number. It ignores Greek accents and understands Latin spelling of Greek names, so `dionisis` finds “Διονύσης”. Three or more digits find a phone number however it's written. Searching for a name also finds people linked to that person.

Above the list, `All` and `★ Favorites` switch between everyone and your favourites. The label chips hide or show contacts with that label; you start with `Personal`, `Work` and `Family`. `＋ Manage labels` lets you add, rename, recolour and delete labels; a label still in use can't be deleted.

## Duplicates
`Duplicates` looks for contacts that are probably the same person: `Same phone` or `Same email` (listed as `Definitive matches`) and `Similar name` (`Possible matches`). Tap or click a contact in a group to open `Merge duplicates`: it shows the combined contact. Use `Flip primary` to pick which one to keep as the base, then `Merge now`. You can `Undo` straight afterwards.

## Import, skip and merge
Every import opens `Review import` first, whether it comes from a file, `From phone`, `From an account` or a .vcf opened with orOS.

A CSV file from anywhere (Outlook, Excel, another address book, a list you made yourself) first opens `Match the columns`: for each column you see its name and an example, and orOS has already guessed what it holds from the column names, in English or Greek (`First name`, `Επώνυμο`, `Κινητό`, `Τ.Κ.`…). Change any guess, set a column to `Skip`, or pick the type of a phone, e-mail, website or address. Untick `The first row has the column names` if the file starts straight with contacts. When a column holds birthdays, `Dates` says whether they are written day first (the usual way in Greece) or month first. The preview shows the first contacts as they will be read; `Continue` takes you to `Review import`. A CSV exported from Google Contacts is read directly, without this step.

`Review import` sorts the contacts it read into three groups:
- `New`: nobody here matches. Choose `Add` or `Skip` for each.
- `Other details`: a contact here has the same email, the same phone (with or without the country code) or the same first and last name, and the import brings something new. You see what it adds and which values differ, for example `Company: Alpha Inc → Alpha Ltd`. `Merge, keep mine` adds the missing details and keeps your values; `Merge, use imported` adds them and takes the imported values where they differ. You can also `Add as separate` or `Skip`. `When a detail differs` sets the choice for all of them at once.
- `Already here, skipped`: identical contacts, left as they are.

A contact with the same name but other phones and emails is marked as probably another person and is added separately unless you choose to merge. Nothing is ever deleted, and `Undo` straight after the import puts everything back as it was.

## From an account (Google)
`From an account` copies your contacts from Google once. Tap `Google` and sign in in the small window; orOS reads your contacts with their groups (they become labels), shows `Review import`, and ends the connection straight away. No password or access is kept: the Google access is withdrawn as soon as the reading ends. Run it again whenever you want to bring in new contacts; the identical ones are skipped. The review counts how many were read; a person listed twice in the account (same name and the same phone or e-mail) is combined into one, so orOS can end up with fewer contacts than the account shows.

iCloud, Yahoo and other services: export your contacts there as a .vcf file and use `Import`.

Microsoft (Outlook.com, Hotmail, Microsoft 365) is not offered by orOS for now. Under `Use my own app id` you can register your own OAuth app (Google or Microsoft) with the address shown there and paste its client id; it stays on this device and its button appears.

## Find photos online
`Find photos` looks for pictures for your contacts that have none. It uses the profile picture a person set for their own e-mail address on Gravatar or Libravatar, so it only finds people who did that, and it only sends a coded fingerprint of the address, never names or numbers. An e-mail that several of your contacts share (an office address) is skipped, so a picture always belongs to that one contact. Tap `Search`, untick any picture that is not right and tap `Use the ticked photos`; existing photos are never replaced, and `Undo` takes the new ones away again.

## Update from online sources
`Update online` (top bar) checks the contacts in the list right now, so search or pick a label first to check only some; `Update from online sources` in a contact's window checks that one. orOS looks only where the contact already points: the public profile the person set for their own e-mail on Gravatar (only a coded fingerprint of the address is sent), and the website or link page (Linktree, bio.link, Carrd and similar) saved in the contact, read once like a visitor would, and the public GitHub, Bluesky or Mastodon profiles these point to (their open APIs, no sign-in). From there it can suggest links to their profiles, phone numbers, e-mail addresses, a birthday, a job title, a company and an address, where they are published.
Each suggestion shows where it came from and how sure it is: `sure` when it was found through something only this contact has (their own e-mail, a page that shows their own phone number or e-mail, a verified account) or in two places, `likely` when it comes from their own page and nothing disagrees, and `check it` when the name found there is someone else's. `check it` rows start unticked. Nothing changes until you tap `Apply selected`, nothing you already have is replaced, and `Undo` puts the contacts back.
Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, X and TikTok are never read, because their terms forbid it; a link to them found elsewhere is kept as a link. orOS never searches for a person by name alone or by face. An e-mail that several contacts share is not used. Pages that don't let a browser read them are fetched through the orOS relay, which keeps nothing.
## On this phone (Android app)
In the orOS Android app, `On this phone` puts your orOS contacts in the phone's own Contacts, so the dialer and incoming calls show their names. Turn on `Show on this phone` and tap `Allow access` (Android asks once). Contacts arrive in an "orOS" account and follow every change you make here. It works both ways: when you edit or delete one of them in the phone's contacts app, or save a new contact to the "orOS" account there, the change comes into orOS Contacts the next time you open orOS. Labels, relations and instant messaging stay as they are in orOS, and a photo from the phone is used only when the contact has none here. Some contacts apps (Fossify Contacts, for example) let you pick the "orOS" account for a new contact; others only offer their own account. Turning the switch off removes them from the phone again.

Other apps can send contacts to orOS too. When a phone app offers "Add contact" or "Create contact" (after a call from a number you don't have, from Viber or another messenger) or opens a .vcf file, pick orOS. The contact opens in `Review import`, and nothing is saved until you confirm. The button doesn't appear in a browser, only in the Android app, and the choice is kept on this phone only.

## Sync and backup
Your contacts and labels sync between your devices through [orOS sync](help:t/sync). Each detail of a contact syncs on its own: if you add a phone number on your computer and change the note on your phone at the same time, both changes are kept. The interactions you log sync one by one too, and one deleted on a device is deleted on the others. Which labels you've hidden, and the reach-out reminder switch, are not synced.

- `Import` reads a vCard file (.vcf) or a CSV file and opens `Review import` (see above). Afterwards you see a list of what was added, merged or skipped. With orOS installed on a computer you can also open a .vcf file with `Open with`; orOS shows the same review before importing it (see [opening files](help:t/install)).
- `From phone` (only in Chrome on Android) opens your phone's contact list. Pick the people you want; only those reach orOS, with their names, phones and emails, and addresses when the phone shares them. They go through the same `Review import`.
- `Export` saves all your contacts as a .vcf file that other address books can open.
- `Export JSON` saves a complete copy of your contacts. `Import JSON` restores it; it combines with what you have and keeps the newer version of each contact.

See also [backup](help:t/backup).

## Keyboard shortcuts
- [[Ctrl+F]] (or [[Cmd+F]]) jumps to the search box.
- [[Alt+N]] starts a new contact.
- [[Esc]] closes the contact card or the open window.

## Tips
- Birthdays, anniversaries and custom dates appear in [Calendar](app:calendar) every year. Tapping one there opens the contact here.
- `Family tree` on a contact's card opens that person in [Family Tree](app:familytree). If nobody in the tree is linked to the contact yet, it offers to build a tree from your contacts instead.
- `QR code` on a contact's card opens [QR Generator](app:qr) with a contact code (vCard) for that person, ready to save or print. The contact itself is not changed.

## Limits
- `Import JSON` only accepts files made by `Export JSON` in orOS Contacts.
- CSV import reads one address of each type (home, work, other) per contact, and a single birthday.
- Business card reading works best with a sharp, straight, well-lit photo of a printed card. It doesn't read QR codes, and its guesses (which line is the name, which the company) can be wrong, so check the form before saving. Greek capital letters come without accents.
- The `Reach out` reminder appears only while orOS is open (or when you open it later that day).
- A device still on an older orOS version doesn't know `Keep in touch`: if you edit a contact there, it keeps working, but a cadence changed on that device at the same time may be lost.
- `From an account` doesn't bring contact photos, and reads only your own contacts (not Google's "Other contacts" or a company directory).
- `From phone` appears only in Chrome on Android, because other browsers can't open the phone's contact list.
