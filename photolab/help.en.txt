# Photo Lab
Edit photos and pictures with layers, selections, brushes, retouch tools, filters and adjustments, in the spirit of Photoshop, GIMP and Photopea. Everything works offline.

## Getting started
- `New picture` starts an empty picture: pick a size and a background. `Open picture` opens a photo from your device (also a camera RAW file, or a Photoshop, OpenRaster or Krita file with its layers), and `Paste` opens a picture you copied. You can also drop a picture file anywhere on the app.
- Your pictures are under `Projects`. Tap or click one to open it; the pencil renames it and the bin deletes it (`Undo` in the message brings it back).
- In the editor, the button at the top left goes back to `Projects`. Your work is saved as you go.

## Tools
The tool bar is on the left (at the bottom on a phone). The bar above the picture shows the options of the current tool.
- `Move`: drag the layer. With a selection, it lifts and moves the selected pixels.
- `Marquee selection` and `Lasso`: select an area. Press the tool again to switch between `Rectangle` and `Ellipse`, or between a freehand and a point-by-point lasso. Hold Shift to add to the selection, Alt to subtract.
- `Magic wand`: selects similar colours; set the `Tolerance` and whether only `Contiguous` pixels count.
- `Quick selection`: paint over what you want and the selection grows out to its edges; Alt+drag takes away. [[Shift+W]] switches between it and the magic wand.
- `Brush` and `Eraser`: `Size`, `Hardness`, `Opacity` and `Flow`. A pen paints wider when you press harder.
- `Clone stamp`: Alt+click (or `Set source`, then a tap) where to copy from, then paint where the copy should go. `Healing brush` does the same but keeps the colour around the spot, for blemishes and dust; its `Red eye` kind turns red pupils dark.
- `Dodge / burn / sponge`: `Dodge` lightens and `Burn` darkens the `Shadows`, `Midtones` or `Highlights`; `Sponge` takes colour away or adds it. `Smudge` drags colour like a finger in wet paint.
- `Paint bucket` and `Gradient` (`Linear` or `Radial`) fill with the foreground colour.
- `Eyedropper`: click picks the foreground colour, Alt+click the background colour.
- `Text` and `Shape` add their own layers; click a text to change it.
- `Crop`: drag the frame, choose a `Ratio`, then `Crop` or [[Enter]].
- `Hand` and `Zoom` move around. Two fingers pinch to zoom on touch screens.

## Layers
`Show or hide panels` (the layers button at the top right) opens `Layers` and `History`.
- Each layer has an eye (show or hide), a lock and a name (tap it to choose the layer). `Blend` and `Opacity` apply to the chosen layer.
- The buttons below add, duplicate, move, merge down and delete layers. The `Layer` menu has more: `Layer via copy`, `Rasterize layer`, `Flatten image` and more.
- `History` lists your steps; tap one to go back to it.

## Masks, adjustment layers and styles
- A layer mask hides parts of a layer without erasing them: black hides, white shows, grey shows a little. `Add layer mask` (the mask button below the layers, or the `Layer` menu) shows everything; with a selection it shows only the selection. Paint the mask with the brush, gradient or fill; tap the layer's picture to paint its pixels again, and the mask's picture to go back to the mask ([[Ctrl+\]] switches). `Disable or enable mask`, `Apply mask` (into the pixels) and `Delete mask` are in the `Layer` menu.
- `New adjustment layer…` (the half-filled circle button) changes the colours of every visible layer below it without touching their pixels: `Levels`, `Curves`, `Hue / saturation`, `Colour balance`, `Brightness / contrast`, `Black and white`, `Photo filter`, `Gradient map`, `Invert`, `Threshold`, `Posterize`. Double-click it to change it; its opacity and mask choose how much and where.
- `Layer style…` (the fx button) adds a `Drop shadow`, `Outer glow`, `Inner glow`, `Stroke` or `Colour overlay` to a layer. Styles follow the layer's pixels and stay editable; a layer with styles shows an fx button in its row.

## Menus
- `Image`: `Image size…`, `Canvas size…`, rotate, flip, `Crop to selection`, `Trim transparent edges`.
- `Select`: `Select all`, `Invert selection`, `Subject…` (see below), `Colour range…` (every pixel near a colour), `Feather…`, `Grow…`, `Shrink…`, `Save selection…` and `Load selection…` (saved selections stay in the project).
- `View`: `Rulers`, `Guides`, `Grid` and `Snap` turn on and off; `New guide…`, `Clear guides` and `Grid size…`. Drag a guide out of a ruler; with the `Move` or `Hand` tool drag a guide to move it, or back onto the ruler to remove it. With `Snap` on, selections, crops, shapes and moved layers stick to guides, the grid and the canvas edges and centre.
- `Adjust`: `Light and colour…`, `Looks…`, `Hue / saturation…`, `Levels…`, `Lightroom preset (.xmp)…`, `Invert`, `Black and white`, `Threshold…`, `Posterize…`. Dialogs show a live preview; with a selection, only the selected area changes.
- `Filter`: `Gaussian blur…`, `Motion blur…`, `Lens blur…`, `Unsharp mask…` (sharpens), `Add noise…`, `Reduce noise…`, `Pixelate…`, `Emboss…`, `Find edges`, `Vignette…`. Blurs may spread past the layer's pixels; with a selection they stay inside it.
- `File`: `Export…` saves a PNG, JPG, WebP, PSD, OpenRaster or orOS (`.orosphoto`) file to your device, `Save to Pictures…` saves it in the Pictures folder of the orOS disk, `Batch process…` works on many photos at once (see below), `Use as wallpaper` makes it the wallpaper of this device, and `Send to someone…` sends the picture as a link (see [Send to someone](help:t/share)): it goes as a JPEG of at most 2048 px, without hidden details such as place or camera.
- `File` › `Send to…` saves the picture as a PNG in Pictures and sends it on: to Mail as an attachment, to Layout onto a page, or to someone.
On a phone all menus are under the menu button.

## Select subject and remove background
- `Select` › `Subject…` finds the main subject of the picture (a person, an animal, an object) and selects it, with a soft edge. Then adjust, copy or mask just that part.
- `Layer` › `Remove background…` does the same and hides everything else with a layer mask on the current layer. Nothing is erased: paint the mask to fix an edge, or delete the mask to get the background back.
- Both look at the whole visible picture. Move the `Edge` slider to keep more or less around the subject, then `Apply`. One `Undo` takes it back.
- They use the same on-device tool as Atelier's background remover. The first time, it needs a download of about 16 MB; after that it works offline, and the picture never leaves your device. If Atelier downloaded it already, there is nothing to download.

## From Google Photos
- On the projects screen, `From Google Photos`: sign in to Google, press `Open Google Photos`, pick one picture and press `Done`. It opens here as a new project.
- orOS reads only that picture, once, and lets go of the sign-in right away. Videos don't open here.

## Camera RAW files
- `Open picture`, drag and drop and `Batch process…` take RAW files from most cameras: DNG, CR2, CR3, CRW, NEF, NRW, ARW, SR2, ORF, RAF, RW2, PEF, SRW, X3F, 3FR, IIQ, ERF and more.
- Photo Lab develops the RAW data itself (with the camera's white balance), then it opens like any photo. This can take several seconds for a big file. On a phone, RAW files are developed at half size to save memory.
- Where the browser can't develop RAW data, Photo Lab opens the preview the camera stored inside the file instead (usually full size or close), and a message says so. That preview has the camera's own look.

## Batch processing
`File` › `Batch process…` (or `Batch process` on the projects screen) does the same work on many photos at once. Your photos are never changed: new files are made.
- `Photos`: add them `From this device…` or `From the orOS disk…` (tick photos in any folder; `Tick all here` ticks a whole folder). RAW files are fine too.
- `Adjustments`: nothing, `The adjustment layers of this picture` (the visible adjustment layers of the open picture, with their opacity), or `A Lightroom preset (.xmp)`.
- `Size`: keep it, or set the `Long side`, `Width`, `Height` or a `Percent`. Smaller photos are not enlarged unless you tick `Also enlarge smaller photos`.
- `Watermark`: a `Text` (its colour and size) or a `Picture` such as a logo, at one of nine places, with its `Opacity`.
- `Saving`: JPG, PNG or WebP with a `Quality`, and the `File names`: {name} is the photo's own name, {n} a number, {nnn} a number with zeros (001), {date} today's date, {w} and {h} the new size. Save into a folder of `Pictures` on the orOS disk, or as `One ZIP file`; the end screen has `Save ZIP…`.
- `Stop` ends the run after the photo it is working on; what is done stays done. Photos that can't be read are listed at the end.

## Automations
Steps you set once and run again and again, on the open picture or on many photos. No code: you pick the steps from a list.
- `File` › `Automations…` (or `Automations` on the projects screen) lists them. `New automation`, give it a name, then `Add a step`: `Tone` (brightness, contrast, saturation, warmth, tint, fade, sepia, vignette), `Look`, `Black and white`, `Blur`, `Sharpen`, `Grain`, `Crop to a ratio` (the largest centred part, such as 1 : 1 or 16 : 9), `Resize`, `Watermark` (a text, its size, colour, place and opacity) and `Export to Pictures` (JPG, PNG or WebP). Move steps with ↑ and ↓.
- `Run` on `The open picture` works like the menus: on the active layer and the selection. The watermark becomes a layer of its own. `Undo` on the message that follows takes every step back.
- `Run` on `Many photos (Batch process)` opens `Batch process…` with the automation picked under `Adjustments`; you can also pick it there yourself. The steps run on every photo after the batch's own resize, before its watermark; an `Export` step only sets the format and quality.
- Automations sync with your other devices, together with those of Atelier and Layout.

## Editing a picture from Atelier
When Atelier sends a picture here to edit, a `Done` button appears at the top. It saves the result as a new picture and sends it back to Atelier; the project also stays in Photo Lab for later changes.

## Photoshop and OpenRaster files
- Photoshop files (`.psd`) and OpenRaster files (`.ora`, from GIMP, Krita and MyPaint) open with their layers: names, positions, opacity, blend modes and visibility.
- Groups are flattened into the layer list (a hidden group hides its layers) and layer masks are applied to the layers' pixels. Text, smart objects and effects come in as the pixels the file saved for them.
- Krita files (`.kra`) open too: just open the file you saved in Krita. Ordinary paint layers in 8-bit RGBA come in with their names, positions, opacity, blend modes and visibility; vector, file, filter and fill layers and masks are left out. When the layers use another colour depth (16-bit, float, CMYK, grey), the file opens as one layer from the picture Krita saves alongside them, and Photo Lab tells you so. For a sure result with any Krita file, use File › Export in Krita and pick OpenRaster (`.ora`).
- `Export…` with `PSD (Photoshop, with layers)` or `OpenRaster (GIMP, Krita, with layers)` keeps your layers for Photoshop, Photopea, GIMP or Krita. Text, shapes, masks, styles and adjustment layers go as pixels.

## orOS file (.orosphoto)
- `File` › `orOS file (.orosphoto)…` (or `Export…` › `orOS file (.orosphoto)`) saves the whole project in one file: every layer with its masks, styles, blend mode and opacity, adjustment layers, text and shapes you can still edit, guides and saved selections. The pixels are kept exactly as they are.
- An .orosphoto is an open ZIP file: unzip it and you find `content.json` (the project), `media` with one PNG per layer, mask or selection, and `preview/image.png`, the finished picture, which any picture viewer opens.
- Open it with `Open picture`, by dropping it on the app, or from your device with “Open with” orOS: it always comes in as a new project. `File` › `Unzipped orOS folder…` opens an .orosphoto that was unzipped, even after you changed its files.

## Lightroom presets
- `Adjust` › `Lightroom preset (.xmp)…` opens a preset saved from Lightroom (Classic or CC) or Camera Raw and adds it as adjustment layers above the current layer, named after the preset: exposure, whites and blacks as `Levels`, contrast, shadows, highlights and the tone curve as `Curves`, white balance, saturation and vibrance, and black and white.
- The layers stay editable: lower a layer's opacity to make the preset weaker, or hide one you don't want. One `Undo` removes them all.
- It is an approximation, because Lightroom works on the raw data. Settings Photo Lab has no layer for (clarity, texture, dehaze, sharpening, noise, grain, vignette, colour mixer, colour grading) are left out, and a message says which.

## Sync and backup
- The list of projects follows you through [orOS sync](help:t/sync). The pictures themselves are kept on the orOS disk, in the `Photo Lab` folder, and Vault Drive syncs them file by file, encrypted.
- If the same picture changed on two devices before they synced, nothing is lost: the other device's version is kept as its own project.
- Undo history, tool settings and colours stay on this device.

## Keyboard shortcuts
- [[Ctrl+Z]]: undo. [[Ctrl+Shift+Z]] or [[Ctrl+Y]]: redo.
- [[Alt+N]]: new picture. [[Ctrl+O]]: open picture. [[Ctrl+Shift+S]]: export.
- [[Ctrl+C]], [[Ctrl+X]], [[Ctrl+V]]: copy, cut, paste. [[Delete]]: clear the selection.
- [[Ctrl+A]]: select all. [[Ctrl+D]]: deselect. [[Ctrl+Shift+I]]: invert selection.
- [[Alt+T]]: free transform. [[Alt+Shift+N]]: new layer. [[Ctrl+J]]: layer via copy. [[Ctrl+E]]: merge down.
- [[Ctrl+U]]: hue / saturation. [[Ctrl+L]]: levels. [[Ctrl+I]]: invert. [[Ctrl+\]]: edit the mask or the pixels.
- [[Ctrl+=]], [[Ctrl+-]], [[Ctrl+0]], [[Ctrl+1]]: zoom in, zoom out, fit, 100%.
- [[Ctrl+Alt+R]]: rulers. [[Ctrl+;]]: guides. [[Ctrl+']]: grid. [[Ctrl+Shift+;]]: snap.
- Tools: [[V]] move, [[M]] marquee, [[L]] lasso, [[W]] magic wand, [[C]] crop, [[B]] brush, [[E]] eraser, [[S]] clone stamp, [[J]] healing brush, [[O]] dodge / burn / sponge, [[R]] smudge, [[G]] paint bucket ([[Shift+G]] gradient), [[I]] eyedropper, [[T]] text, [[U]] shape, [[H]] hand, [[Z]] zoom.
- [[X]] swaps the colours, [[D]] resets them, `[` and `]` change the brush size. Hold [[Space]] to move around.

## Tips
> Want to edit only part of a photo? Select it first: brushes, fills and adjustments stay inside the selection.

## Limits
- Pictures up to 8192 px a side on computers, and 4096 px (about 16 megapixels) on phones. Bigger pictures are made smaller when they open.
- Up to 100 layers a picture. Text and shape layers move and scale but do not rotate; rasterize them first.
- A mask stays in place when you flip, rotate or transform only its layer; whole-picture size, rotation and flips move it with the layer.
- Old undo steps are dropped when they would use too much memory.
- Photoshop files: RGB, Grayscale and CMYK at 8 or 16 bits (CMYK colours are converted simply, without a colour profile). Large documents (PSB) and other colour modes do not open.
- RAW files up to 300 MB. Developing RAW data needs a browser that allows it (Chrome, Edge and other Chromium browsers); elsewhere the preview stored in the file opens. Some rare or very new cameras may not be read; then nothing opens.
- Select subject finds one main subject; with several people or a busy scene the mask may need some painting.
- Batch processing: up to 2000 photos at a time and a 1 GB ZIP. Masks of the adjustment layers are left out. PSD, OpenRaster and SVG files are not taken; the watermark picture and the list of photos are not kept after the dialog closes.
