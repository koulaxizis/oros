# Flashcards
Learn anything with cards: a question on the front, the answer on the back. Flashcards shows each card again just before you would forget it, so a few minutes a day are enough to keep what you learned. The schedule comes from FSRS, a modern open method for spaced repetition.

## Getting started
1. Press `New deck` and give the deck a name, for example "Spanish words".
2. Press `Add card`, write the front and the back, and press `Save`. `Save and add another` keeps the window open for the next card.
3. Back on the list of decks, press `Study` next to the deck.
4. Read the front and try to remember the answer. Press `Show answer`, then say how it went (see below).

## Decks
- Each deck has its own cards. Tap a deck to see its cards, find one with the search box, and open it to change it.
- A deck can live inside another deck: choose it in `Inside deck`. Studying a deck also studies the decks inside it.
- The three numbers next to a deck are the new cards (blue), the cards you are still learning (orange) and the cards to review today (green).
- `Deck settings` changes the name, how many new cards and reviews you get per day, and how much you want to remember.
- `Delete` in `Deck settings` removes the deck, the decks inside it and their cards.

## Card types
- `Basic`: one card, front and back.
- `Basic and reversed`: two cards, one each way. Good for words: "dog → σκύλος" and "σκύλος → dog".
- `Fill in the gap`: write a sentence, select a word and press `Make a gap`. The word is hidden when you study and you fill it in. Every gap number makes its own card; `Gap with the same number` hides several words on the same card. A gap can carry a hint: `{{c1::Athens::city}}` shows "[city]".
Tags (words separated by spaces) help you find cards later.

## Pictures, sounds and formatting
- Under each field of a card, `Picture` and `Sound file` add files from your device. You can also paste a picture into a field or drop files on it. Up to 6 per field.
- `Record` records your voice with the microphone; press it again to stop. Recordings stay on your devices.
- Pictures are made smaller (1600 pixels at most) and saved without their hidden details such as the place they were taken. Sounds can be up to 10 MB.
- The buttons above the fields add simple formatting: `**bold**`, `*italic*`, and lines that start with `- ` or `1. ` become lists.
- When a card shows, its sounds play by themselves. Turn this off in the settings at the top.
- A picture or sound added on another device shows "Not on this device yet" until Vault Drive has copied it.

## Read aloud
- The 🔊 button on a card reads it aloud with a voice installed on your device. Voices that send the text to an online service are never used, so the button only appears when your device has its own voices.
- In `Deck settings`, `Voice for the front` and `Voice for the back` pick the language of each side. `Automatic` reads Greek text in Greek and other text in the app language.

## Studying
After `Show answer`, pick one of four buttons. Each one shows when you will see the card again:
- `Again`: you forgot. The card comes back in a few minutes.
- `Hard`: you remembered with effort.
- `Good`: you remembered.
- `Easy`: you knew it at once.
Be honest: the schedule learns from your answers. `Undo` takes back the last answer. `Edit` fixes a mistake on the card you are looking at.
The `⋯` button has two more actions for the card on screen:
- `Hide until tomorrow` puts the card away for the rest of the study day.
- `Suspend card` keeps the card out of study until you resume it in `Browse`. Its schedule is kept.
`Undo` takes these back too.
New cards come one per day for each note, so the two cards of a reversed note appear on different days.

### How much to remember
`How much to remember` in `Deck settings` is the chance you want to have of remembering a card when it comes back. 90% is a good start. A higher aim shows cards more often; a lower aim needs less time but you forget more.

### The study day
A study day starts at 04:00, so studying after midnight still counts for the day before. You can change the hour with the settings button at the top.

## Import from Anki and other apps
`Import` (on the list of decks) brings in cards from a file:
- **Anki decks** (`.apkg`, and whole collections `.colpkg`), old and new Anki versions. Decks, tags, pictures and sounds come along. Cloze notes stay fill-in-the-gap cards, and "Basic and reversed" stays reversed.
- **Your Anki study history**: tick `Bring my study history`, and each card carries on from where Anki left it. Suspended cards stay suspended.
- **orOS card files** (`.oroscards`), made with `Export` here or by the [Converter](app:converter) from an Anki deck. Only what is not here yet is added: new decks, new cards and, if you tick it, the study history. Opening the same file twice adds nothing, and nothing here is changed or removed. You can also open the file straight from [Files](app:files) or with your device's "Open with".
- **Text files** (`.csv`, `.tsv`, `.txt`), for example Anki's "Notes in plain text" export or a Quizlet export: one card per line. Choose which column is the front, the back and the tags, the card type and the deck. A front with `{{c1::…}}` becomes a fill-in-the-gap card.
Importing the same Anki deck again adds only what is new. Nothing in the file is ever run: card styles and scripts are left out, and the text keeps bold, italic and lists.

## Cards from Notes, Wiki, Writer and other apps
In Notes, Wiki, Writer, Reader or Spreadsheet, choose `Send to an app…` and then `Flashcards`. In Notes and Writer, select some text first to send only that part. Flashcards finds the cards and shows them before anything is saved:
- a line `Question :: Answer` becomes a Basic card, and `Question ::: Answer` a Basic and reversed card;
- a line with words marked `==like this==` becomes a fill-in-the-gap card, one gap for each marked part;
- a heading with text under it becomes a card with the heading on the front;
- in a table, the first row holds the column names, then the first column is the front and the second the back.
If none of these is found, you get one card with the title on the front and all the text on the back. Untick the cards you do not want, change any of them, pick the deck, then press `Make cards`. Cards that are already here start unticked.

## Today and the study reminder
When cards are waiting, the Today app shows a line like "12 cards to study". In the settings at the top, `Remind me to study every day at` sends one notification a day from that time on, while cards are waiting. The reminder is off at first, and it is set on each device separately. The count comes from this device: answers you gave on another device are counted once Flashcards opens here.

## Stats
`Stats` (on the list of decks, or inside a deck) shows your progress for all decks or for one deck and the decks inside it:
- **Today**: answers, minutes, how many times you pressed Again, and how many days in a row you have studied.
- **Cards**: new, learning, young (shown again within 21 days) and mature (21 days or more), and suspended.
- **True retention**: of your answers to cards in review, the share that was not Again, for the last 30 days and for all time. When it is close to `How much to remember` in the deck settings, the schedule fits you.
- **Study days**: a square for every day of the last 12 months, darker on days with more answers.
- **Answers per day** for the last 30 days (learning and review), and **reviews due** on each of the next 30 days.
- **Intervals** and **difficulty** of the cards in review.
Everything is worked out from your answers, so it is the same on every device once Vault Drive has copied the files.

## Export and share
`Export` (on the list of decks, or inside a deck) saves all decks or one deck and the decks inside it:
- **orOS file** (`.oroscards`): decks, cards, pictures and sounds, and your study history if you tick it. Open it in Flashcards on any device, or send it to a friend. It is a ZIP file: unzip it and `content.json` holds the cards and `preview/cards.csv` a plain copy.
- **CSV text**: one line per note with the front, the back, the tags, the deck and the card type, for spreadsheets and other card apps. Pictures and sounds are not in it.
Pictures or sounds that have not reached this device yet are left out of the file, and the toast says how many.

## Browse all cards
`Browse` at the top lists every card, one row per card. Narrow the list with the search box, a deck, a tag or a state (new, learning, due today, review later, hidden, suspended). Tick cards (or the box at the top for all of them) to:
- `Move` their notes to another deck,
- `Add tag` or `Remove tag`,
- `Suspend` or `Resume` them,
- `Delete` their notes.
Tap a row to edit the card.

## Sync and backup
- Decks and settings travel with orOS sync, encrypted.
- Cards and answers are files in the `Flashcards` folder of the orOS disk ([Files](app:files)), and Vault Drive copies them to your other devices. Each device writes only its own files, so two devices can study offline at the same time and every answer is kept.
- Pictures and sounds are files in `Flashcards/Media`, named by their content, so the same picture added on two devices is one file.
- The schedule of each card is worked out again from your answers on every device, so it is the same everywhere.

## Keyboard shortcuts
- [[Space]] or [[Enter]]: show the answer.
- [[1]] [[2]] [[3]] [[4]]: Again, Hard, Good, Easy. After the answer is shown, [[Space]] also means Good.
- [[Ctrl+Z]]: undo the last answer.
- [[R]]: read the card aloud.
- [[-]]: hide the card until tomorrow.
- [[Ctrl+Enter]]: save the card in the card window.
- [[Esc]]: close a window, or go back.

## Find it from anywhere
- The menu's search box finds your decks and cards by their words. Choosing one opens it in Flashcards.

## Limits
- Recording needs a microphone and your permission; how voices sound depends on your device.
- Without the orOS disk (the app opened on its own, outside orOS) cards are not saved.
